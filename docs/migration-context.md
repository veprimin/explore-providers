# ExploreProviders migration — diagnosis and decisions

Reference document. Everything here came from Google Search Console (via the
Supermetrics MCP server) and the live WordPress REST API, in August 2026.

---

## 1. What happened to the site

**0 Google clicks since 26 June 2026.** The collapse had two distinct stages.

### Stage 1 — mid-February 2026: position collapse

| Date | Clicks/day | Impressions/day |
|---|---|---|
| 25 Jan 2026 (peak) | 161 | 1,050 |
| 11 Feb | 41 | 1,286 |
| 13 Feb | 35 | 1,235 |
| **15–17 Feb** | **13 → 4 → 7** | 534 → 436 → 203 |
| 18 Mar | 6 | 698 |

Impressions partially recovered through March (up to ~900/day) but clicks never
came back above 7. That is the signature of falling from roughly position 8 to
position 30+: still shown, no longer clicked.

### Stage 2 — 18–26 June 2026: index removal

Impressions went `101 → 67 → 44 → 14 → 0` over nine days and have stayed at
approximately zero since. The post sitemap's `lastmod` is 2026-06-20, inside
that window — worth investigating what changed on the site around 18–20 June.

### Lifetime totals

- **3,364 clicks**, **137,930 impressions** — the entire history of the site
- Data begins November 2025

## 2. Why — the content audit

| Metric | Value |
|---|---|
| Posts | 459 |
| Pages | 4 |
| Programmatic "X vs Y" comparisons | **398 (87%)** |
| Provider reviews | 61 |
| Genuine editorial articles | **0** |
| Distinct brands driving comparisons | ~29 |
| Typical comparison length | 520–660 words |

29 brands × 28 others ÷ 2 ≈ 406 pairs, against 398 published pages. These were
generated by looping over a brand list against one template.

Sampled pages share identical headings and framing sentences with entity names
swapped, and render **empty pricing cells** — e.g. the Hims row on
`/tuyo-health-vs-hims/` shows `(Code: ) —` with no prices at all.

**Nothing technically blocked indexing.** `index, follow`, canonicals correct,
sitemaps live. This is a content-quality problem matching Google's *scaled
content abuse* policy, enforced algorithmically — which is why there was no
Search Console manual action message.

## 3. The MEDVi finding

MEDVi is the best-performing partner. Its pages split into two clusters with
opposite outcomes.

| Query | Clicks | Impressions | Position |
|---|---|---|---|
| medvi quad reviews | 163 | 4,200 | 14.3 |
| medvi quad | 101 | 3,255 | 10.9 |
| quad by medvi | 57 | 1,759 | 9.2 |
| quad medvi | 41 | 2,188 | 10.4 |

### Cluster B — MEDVi commercial and trust queries. Invisible.

| Query | Impressions | Clicks | Position |
|---|---|---|---|
| medvi glp-1 reviews | 227 | **0** | 65.8 |
| medvi | 97 | 0 | 56.5 |
| is medvi legit | 60 | 0 | 52.6 |
| is medvi legitimate | 42 | 0 | 51.4 |
| medvi promo code | 41 | 0 | 35.7 |
| medvi pricing | 39 | 0 | 58.4 |
| medvi cost | 37 | 0 | 64.7 |
| how much is medvi | 27 | 0 | 67.4 |

Plus a long tail of `is medvi safe`, `is medvi a scam`, `medvi coupon`,
`medvi cost for tirzepatide` — all at position 50–90.

**Cause:** five near-duplicate pages about MEDVi QUAD (the ED product) and
nothing authoritative about MEDVi as a GLP-1 provider. `/medvi-review/` — the
page that should own cluster B — has **2 clicks from 1,455 impressions at
position 52.6**.

### The MEDVi QUAD cannibalisation cluster

| URL | Clicks | Impressions | Position |
|---|---|---|---|
| `/medvi-quad-ed-review-pricing-how-it-works-pros-cons/` | 553 | 7,959 | **8.1** |
| `/medvi-quad-ed-review-...-quad-reviews/` | 517 | 7,222 | 8.5 |
| `/medvi-quad-ed-reviews-quad-by-medvi-ed-review/` | 95 | 9,251 | 13.4 |
| `/medvi-quad-review-2026-pricing-.../` | 27 | 1,883 | 18.0 |
| `/medvi-quad-reviews-cost-benefits-.../` | 9 | 1,997 | 23.4 |
| `medvi-quad.exploreproviders.com` (subdomain) | 10 | 2,252 | 11.2 |

**1,211 clicks — 36% of the site's entire lifetime traffic — split six ways.**

The subdomain is the worst offender: it holds the *commercial* queries
(`medvi quad price` at position 9.4, `quad by medvi cost` at 10.4) and converts
**zero** of them.

Consolidating to `/medvi-quad-ed-review-pricing-how-it-works-pros-cons/` (the
position-8.1 URL) is the highest-value single action available.

**Do not merge QUAD into `/medvi-review/`.** They are separate intents — ED
product versus GLP-1 provider. Keeping them apart is a deliberate decision.

## 4. ED versus GLP-1

| Vertical | Pages | Lifetime clicks | Clicks per page |
|---|---|---|---|
| **ED** | ~22 | **~1,300** | **~59** |
| GLP-1 | ~441 | ~2,070 | ~4.7 |

ED is roughly **12x more efficient per page**. The top four pages sitewide are
all ED. The site under-built the vertical that worked and mass-produced the one
that did not. Hence: **ED first**.

Other ED findings:

- **DirectMax is a CTR problem, not a ranking problem** — `directmax reviews`
  1,481 impressions at position 9.5 for 15 clicks; `is directmax legit` and
  `does directmax work` both at position ~11 with **0** clicks. Fix titles and
  meta descriptions, not the content.
- **The Hims ED page is unfocused** — pulls 100+ queries at position 40–100
  (`hims mental health`, `hims adhd meds`, `hims prep`), so it ranks for all of
  Hims' business instead of ED. 551 impressions, 0 clicks.
- **A partner-vs-partner page with real demand does not exist**:
  `medvi quad vs rugiet`, `quad vs rugiet`, `medvi quad vs viagra`. MEDVi QUAD
  and Rugiet are both ED partners.

### A correction worth remembering

An early hypothesis was that *partner-vs-rival* pages are the valuable ones and
*both-partner* pages are low priority. **The data says the opposite:**

| | Pages | Clicks |
|---|---|---|
| Partner vs rival | 193 | 123 |
| Both partners | 90 | **1,210** |

Traffic followed **brand recognition**, not partner status. `ro-co-vs-willow`
(158 clicks) works because both brands get searched; `medvi-vs-bmi-md` fails
because nobody searches BMI MD. Select comparison pages by **measured demand
first**, then check monetisation.

## 7. Decisions made

| Decision | Rationale |
|---|---|
| Flat root-level URLs, no `/compare/` or `/providers/` | Owner preference; also means the 44 valuable URLs stay byte-identical with no redirect |
| Basic blog, not a web app | Owner preference; also fastest to serve and crawl |
| Purged pages → 410, not 404 or redirect | Signals deliberate removal; bulk redirects read as soft-404s |
| Editorial score, no `AggregateRating` | Supplied ratings had `votes: 0` alongside "4.97 stars" — publishing that as review schema is fabricated data |
| Logos on one host (R2) | Old data spread across 18 hosts, 12 hotlinked from provider domains, 7 missing entirely |
| ED before GLP-1 | 12x better clicks per page, and the only top-10 rankings |
| Do not re-slug the QUAD page | It holds 553 clicks at position 8.1; a prettier slug is not worth a redirect on the best asset |

### Publishing cadence

The original request was one post every two hours (~12/day, ~360/month). **That
is the exact pattern that caused the penalty.** The addressable topic space is
roughly 100 pages:

- 44 GLP-1 provider reviews (35 new)
- ~40 demand-selected comparisons
- ~10 category hubs
- 12 ED pages

The work is **~100 pages once**, then keeping pricing accurate — not 12/day
indefinitely. At 3–4 properly-researched pages a day that is about four weeks.

## 8. Google Search Console — what not to do

The owner asked about removing URLs via the GSC Removals tool and resubmitting.
**Do not.**

- The Removals tool is a ~6 month **SERP suppression**, not a deindex. The index
  entry survives and the page returns when the window expires.
- It sends **no quality signal**. Google still crawls and still counts the pages
  in the sitewide quality assessment.
- Removals are **per-URL**. Since surviving pages keep their existing slugs, a
  removal request would hide the rebuilt page too.
- "Remove all URLs with this prefix" on the root would suppress the entire site.
- URL Inspection "Request Indexing" is capped at ~10–12 URLs/day and **cannot
  override a quality assessment**. Use the sitemap, which has no cap.

The correct mechanism is the **410** status, which requires Google to recrawl
each URL — expect weeks to months.

Also: **do not disavow backlinks.** The problem is on-site content quality.

Recovery is realistically 3–6 months and contingent on the purge being genuine.

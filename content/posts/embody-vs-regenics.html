Quick comparison of the standout differences

This direct comparison focuses on the most distinctive supported differences: pricing and promotions, clinician oversight model, shipping speed and fulfillment, availability of additional therapies, and regulatory status of compounded drugs. Each point uses only supplied facts.

1. Pricing and promotional structure

Embody lists a starting price of $69/month for compounded semaglutide, cut from $299, and $119/month for compounded tirzepatide. Regenics lists semaglutide at $139 for the first month with renewal at $199, and tirzepatide at $189 first month with renewal at $279. The key difference is Embody presents a consistently low monthly price as its headline offer, while Regenics uses a lower first month price that renews higher.

2. Clinician oversight and care model

Embody is described as a licensed telehealth platform with prescription and telemed visits included and a licensed provider review handled online. Regenics is described as nurse-practitioner-led care where licensed nurse practitioners review intake and oversee every prescription. Both use clinician review before prescribing, but Regenics specifically highlights nurse practitioner oversight and a clinician determination of medical appropriateness.

3. Shipping, fulfillment, and pharmacy notes

Embody advertises free shipping with delivery in 1 to 2 days after clinician approval. Regenics advertises free shipping with medication compounded in the USA and notes a LegitScript certified pharmacy network in the detailed description. Embody emphasizes speed of delivery while Regenics emphasizes U.S. compounding and pharmacy network certification.

4. Additional therapies and features

Embody focuses on compounded semaglutide and compounded tirzepatide at aggressive entry pricing and notes fewer premium features such as integrated coaching. Regenics offers compounded semaglutide and tirzepatide plus a Sermorelin peptide therapy as an add-on and lists FSA and HSA eligibility. Regenics also notes LegitScript certified pharmacy network. These are the primary product and feature distinctions available in the sources.

5. Regulatory and medication type

Both sources state their GLP-1 offerings are compounded medications and include the regulatory note that compounded medications are not FDA approved finished products and are not reviewed by FDA for safety, effectiveness or quality. Embody explicitly clarifies that its medications are compounded and not FDA approved finished products while using the same active ingredients as branded options. Regenics similarly notes compounded medications are not FDA approved and that treatment is available only when a licensed clinician determines it is clinically appropriate.

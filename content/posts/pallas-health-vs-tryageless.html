Comparing Pallas Health vs Try Ageless? Quick verdict: choose Pallas Health if you want a clinician to select between FDA-approved brand pens and compounded options with provider care included; choose Try Ageless if you prioritize format choice and the lowest prepaid monthly entry price, including needle-free tirzepatide drops. This article compares supported differences in pricing, medication formats and compounding, clinician and plan structure, and shipping and extras.

Key difference 1: Pricing structure and entry cost

Pallas Health advertises compounded semaglutide at $139 for the first month, then $597 every 12 weeks, a $199 per month average. Compounded tirzepatide is $179 first month, then $897 every 12 weeks, a $299 per month average. Pallas also lists cash-pay pricing for brand-name Wegovy, Ozempic, Zepbound and Mounjaro at separate monthly cash rates. Try Ageless lists per-month figures on a prepaid yearly plan: semaglutide from $199 per month, tirzepatide from $249 per month, and tirzepatide oral drops from $159 per month. Try Ageless states the discount is already applied to every plan and that code SLIM50 takes an extra $50 off each order.

Key difference 2: Medication formats and clinical options

Pallas Health presents both routes from one intake: FDA-approved brand-name GLP-1s such as Wegovy, Ozempic, Zepbound and Mounjaro, plus clinician-personalized compounded semaglutide or tirzepatide prepared in the USA when a brand medication is not the right fit. Try Ageless sells three compounded formats: semaglutide injection, tirzepatide injection, and needle-free tirzepatide oral drops. Try Ageless highlights format switching under one plan with no new consult fee.

Key difference 3: Compounding and regulatory notes

Both providers dispense compounded medications in some plans and include the regulatory caution that compounded medications are not FDA-approved and are not reviewed by the FDA for safety, effectiveness or quality. Pallas Health emphasizes that compounded medications are prepared by US-licensed compounding pharmacies and that Pallas does not manufacture or compound medication. Try Ageless notes that the oral tirzepatide drops are an off-label compounded format because tirzepatide is not FDA-approved in oral form.

Key difference 4: Plan features, clinician care, and extras

Pallas Health includes provider care, clinician review, ongoing check-ins and care-team messaging inside a single plan price with no membership fee, and is 100% online and asynchronous in most states with free expedited and discreet shipping. Pallas requires a video visit in seven states plus Washington, D.C. and notes care-team hours are weekdays 9 AM to 5 PM ET. Try Ageless includes B12 in every plan, allows switching molecules or formats under the same plan with no extra consult fee, and advertises unlimited messaging, free discreet shipping, cancel-anytime flexibility and that the discounted price is built into every plan.

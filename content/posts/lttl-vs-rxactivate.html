LTTL vs RxActivate: how they differ on price visibility and intake

The central difference in the LTTL vs RxActivate comparison is how each provider handles pricing and the intake process. LTTL lists a starting price of $140 and advertises “Up to $140 off your first month · Code auto-applied.” By contrast, RxActivate does not display price or billing terms before a medical eligibility questionnaire and contact-information gate.

1. Pricing and promotional details

LTTL provides a starting price reference of $140 and repeats the promotional line “Up to $140 off your first month · Code auto-applied.” The LTTL evidence also lists other numeric price points within captured text but the clearest supplied fact is the starting price and the promotional phrase. RxActivate explicitly withholds any price or billing terms until after a medical eligibility questionnaire and collection of first name, last name, email and phone number.

2. Intake, eligibility and gating

RxActivate uses a two-minute eligibility questionnaire that requests goal, height, weight, target weight, timeline, medication history, sex assigned at birth, date of birth, state and safety screening. The captured evidence states the flow then displayed likely eligibility and required contact information before revealing plan or price. LTTL’s captured evidence does not include a described eligibility flow; it notes treatment is available only when a licensed clinician determines it is medically appropriate.

3. Medication formulation and pharmacy handling

LTTL advertises compounded GLP-1 medications that are “compounded By U.S.A. Licensed Pharmacies.” The LTTL evidence states medications are prepared in a state-licensed pharmacy and “independently verified in FDA registered labs.” RxActivate is described as clinician-matched GLP-1 plans and the evidence includes the regulatory note that medication-specific approval status must be confirmed and that branded and compounded products are not interchangeable.

4. Regulatory and quality notes

Both sources include regulatory caution. LTTL’s evidence explicitly states “Compounded medications are not FDA-approved and are not reviewed by FDA for safety, effectiveness or quality.” RxActivate’s captured text includes similar caution that treatment is available only when a licensed clinician determines it is medically appropriate and that medication-specific approval status must be confirmed.

Summary and final guidance on LTTL vs RxActivate

In the LTTL vs RxActivate comparison, LTTL is the clearer option for users who want up-front price signals and explicit lab-verification claims. RxActivate is the clearer option for users who expect a clinician-matched plan but accept that pricing is gated behind an eligibility quiz and contact capture.

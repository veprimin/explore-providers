Regenics vs HumeCare+ answers the central choice: lower out-of-pocket entry or a bundled protocol with body-composition tracking. Early verdict: Regenics starts lower with compounded semaglutide from $139 for the first month and focuses on nurse-practitioner telehealth and a Sermorelin add-on; HumeCare+ is pricier but includes glycine, B12/B6, monthly body-composition scans, an app, clinician access, and a 10%-or-your-money-back promise over six months.

How the programs compare at a glance: price, protocol, and oversight

Both providers use compounded GLP-1 medications and require a licensed clinician to determine medical appropriateness. Key supported differences are starting price, bundled supportive ingredients and tracking, clinician model and access, promotional terms and regulatory notes about compounded products.

1) Starting price and promotional structure

Regenics advertises compounded semaglutide starting at $139 for the first month and tirzepatide at $189 first month, with renewals noted at higher monthly prices (semaglutide renewing at $199/month and tirzepatide at $279/month). HumeCare+ lists semaglutide from $199/month and a tirzepatide protocol from $299/month, with promotional markdown language on the landing page. Both are cash-pay offers; Regenics explicitly states no insurance is required and plans are FSA/HSA eligible. Promotional pricing applies to the first month at Regenics and HumeCare+ shows promotional pricing that may change.

2) Protocol design: bare medication versus bundled support

Regenics positions itself as a straightforward telehealth access point for compounded semaglutide and tirzepatide plus an optional Sermorelin peptide therapy. HumeCare+ sells a bundled protocol that combines compounded GLP-1 medication with glycine and vitamins B6 and B12, plus monthly body-composition scans, an app, and clinician optimization to address muscle loss and side effects that the company says bare GLP-1 programs may ignore.

3) Clinician model and member access

Regenics care is described as nurse-practitioner-led, with licensed nurse practitioners reviewing intake before prescribing. HumeCare+ describes physician-prescribed medications, continuous physician optimization, unlimited clinician access, and a U.S. care team available 24/7. Both require a licensed clinician to determine appropriateness.

4) Guarantees, shipping, and delivery features

HumeCare+ advertises a 10%-or-money-back promise over six months, subject to full terms, and free next-day delivery with cancel-anytime subscription language. Regenics advertises free shipping with medication compounded in the USA but does not capture specific shipping or cancellation detail on the supplied page. Neither provider’s compounded medications are FDA-approved finished products; both notes that compounded medications are not evaluated by the FDA for safety, effectiveness, or quality.

5) Secondary features and add-ons

Regenics highlights a Sermorelin peptide therapy add-on and a LegitScript certified pharmacy network. HumeCare+ emphasizes app-based tracking, body-composition scans to monitor lean mass, bundled glycine and B vitamins for muscle protection and side-effect mitigation, and a money-back promise based on internal results. HumeCare+ also cites internal member analyses and published trial citations alongside its claims, noting individual results may vary.

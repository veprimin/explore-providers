How SkinnyRx vs YourEra differ at a glance

Both companies offer clinician-reviewed, telehealth pathways to GLP-1 therapy and both supply compounded medications that are not FDA-approved finished products. The most distinctive differences supported by the source are product format and presentation, starting price and plan structure, shipping speed and logistics, included clinical support and bundled services, and FSA/HSA or payment options.

1. Product format and presentation

SkinnyRx emphasizes daily semaglutide tablets described as an oral alternative to injectable GLP-1s and notes these are “daily tablets containing Semaglutide.” YourEra offers compounded GLP-1 options including semaglutide and tirzepatide and clarifies these are compounded medications prepared by state licensed pharmacies and are not the same as brand-name injectables.

2. Price and plan structure

Price is a clear dividing point. SkinnyRx lists “as low as $249/mo” and mentions Affirm buy now pay later as an option. YourEra advertises compounded semaglutide “from $99/month” on a 6 month plan and tirzepatide “from $199/month” on a 6 month plan, with the lowest advertised rates tied to a 6 month commitment and a YourEra15 promo code referenced on the landing page.

3. Shipping speed and delivery

SkinnyRx promotes free overnight shipping on all orders and says you will receive shipping and tracking information within 2 business days after prescription processing. YourEra advertises free 1 to 2 day shipping from licensed US pharmacies with discreet delivery.

4. Clinical oversight and included services

Both require clinician review and prescriptions are issued only if a licensed provider determines treatment is appropriate after evaluation. YourEra specifically lists the provider consult, prescription, cold shipping, dose adjustments and 24/7 support as bundled into its plan price. SkinnyRx highlights licensed providers who tailor treatment, ongoing support, and access to a patient care team.

5. Regulatory and quality notes

Both sources explicitly state that compounded medications are not FDA-approved finished products. SkinnyRx adds that partner pharmacies perform third-party testing every 3 to 6 months to confirm concentration within plus or minus 10 percent. YourEra notes compounding by state licensed pharmacies and reiterates that compounded products are not FDA-approved.

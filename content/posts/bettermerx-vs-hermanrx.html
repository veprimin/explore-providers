Medication formats and options

BetterMe Rx supports both injectable and oral compounded semaglutide and tirzepatide, including an uncommon needle-free daily tirzepatide tablet, and advertises four medication and administration options. HermanRx lists compounded semaglutide and tirzepatide formulations paired with vitamin B12 and notes it may offer both compounded and FDA-approved medications depending on a provider recommendation. Both providers sell compounded GLP-1 options; BetterMe Rx highlights injectable plus oral parity while HermanRx emphasizes the bundled B12 inclusion.

Shipping, provider review and timelines

BetterMe Rx includes a provider review, pharmacy compounding, supplies and free 1 to 2 day shipping in its landing-page evidence. HermanRx states provider review in about 24 hours and delivery in about 72 hours when prescribed, and advertises free discreet shipping in plain unmarked packaging to all 50 states. HermanRx publishes a faster, specific timeline in its marketing, whereas BetterMe Rx advertises free 1 to 2 day delivery as captured on its landing page.

Fees, membership and billing

BetterMe Rx states no membership fee, no insurance requirement and cancel-anytime language appears alongside prepaid longer-term plans. HermanRx states genuinely all-inclusive pricing with no membership fee and no separate consultation charge; the HermanRx monthly prices are described as covering the provider review, prescription, ongoing support and free discreet shipping. The main contrast is BetterMe Rx reduces monthly-equivalent cost via prepaid longer plans, while HermanRx offers single monthly all-in pricing with no separate fees.

Regulatory and clinical notes

Both providers state that compounded medications are not FDA-approved and are not reviewed by FDA for safety, effectiveness or quality. BetterMe Rx specifies treatment is available only when a licensed clinician determines it is medically appropriate. HermanRx likewise notes treatment is available only when a licensed clinician determines it is medically appropriate and that compounded medications are not reviewed by the FDA.

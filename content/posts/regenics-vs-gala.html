Price structure and transparency

Regenics publishes promotional entry pricing: semaglutide starts at $139 for the first month and tirzepatide at $189 for the first month, with renewals listed as $199 and $279 respectively. Those promotional rates apply to the first month only. Gala markets a flat all-inclusive price and a $220 promotional discount, and claims the same price at every dose, but the captured landing text did not expose a clear base dollar price for comparison. Both note that treatment is available only when a licensed clinician determines it is medically appropriate.

Clinical model and included services

Both providers operate as telehealth platforms that require clinician review before prescribing compounded GLP-1 medications. Regenics is described as nurse-practitioner-led with prescriptions overseen by licensed nurse practitioners and a LegitScript certified pharmacy network. Gala includes a provider visit and prescription as part of its service and advertises provider check-ins and dose monitoring. Neither provider claims FDA approval for compounded medications; both explicitly note that compounded medications are not FDA-approved finished products and are not reviewed by FDA for safety, effectiveness or quality.

Shipping, membership and cancellation

Regenics lists free shipping and that medications are compounded in the USA, with pharmacy network LegitScript certified. Gala emphasizes free two-day shipping, no monthly membership and cancel anytime language in captured landing content. Gala also frames its offer as direct-pay with no insurance needed, while Regenics similarly notes no insurance is required and that plans are FSA/HSA eligible. Specifics about fees, membership terms, or cancellation beyond these captured statements were not supplied in detail.

Unique features and extras

Regenics uniquely highlights Sermorelin peptide therapy as an add-on, priced in captured FAQ text at $399 for a 15mg injectable, and positions itself as a mid-pack price option with nurse-practitioner-led care. Gala emphasizes a single flat price from first dose to last and promotional messaging about a $220 discount plus testimonials in the captured content claiming weight changes, which are provider marketing claims captured on the page. Users should treat those testimonials as marketing, since no validated clinical outcomes were supplied in the captured evidence.

Regulatory and safety note

Both providers include the regulatory qualifier that compounded medications are not FDA-approved and are not reviewed by FDA for safety, effectiveness or quality. Each states treatment is available only when a licensed clinician determines it is medically appropriate.

Which to choose: final guidance

Choose Regenics if you want lower advertised promotional first-month pricing for semaglutide or tirzepatide, nurse-practitioner-led telehealth oversight, a LegitScript certified compounding pharmacy network, and the option to add Sermorelin peptide therapy. Choose Gala if you prefer an advertised flat all-inclusive price with a provider visit included, free two-day shipping, and messaging that emphasizes the same monthly price at every dose. Both require clinician approval and both offer compounded medications that are not FDA-approved finished products.

1. Price and entry cost

Embody advertises the lowest entry price: compounded semaglutide at $69/month, reduced from $299, and compounded tirzepatide at $119/month. 2. Medication types: name-brand versus compounded only

Shed offers both FDA-approved name-brand GLP-1 medications and compounded alternatives on one platform, letting patients choose branded options or compounded options based on budget and preference. Embody provides compounded semaglutide and compounded tirzepatide only. Both sources note that compounded medications are not FDA-approved finished products and are prepared by a licensed pharmacy based on a provider prescription.

3. Coaching and support

Shed integrates continuous coaching into treatment so coaching is bundled with medication and support continues through titration and lifestyle guidance. The source positions coaching as a differentiator versus competitors that provide prescription-only services. Embody follows a prescription-forward, no-frills model; its materials emphasize telemed clinician review, flat pricing, and clinician support for dosing and side-effect questions but do not include integrated coaching or advanced monitoring features.

4. Shipping, delivery, and commitments

Embody advertises free shipping with delivery in 1 to 2 days after clinician approval and no long-term commitment, cancel anytime. Shed states medication is shipped to your home after clinician review; Shed also promotes membership options and a weight-loss guarantee with terms and conditions. Both providers note that treatment is available only when a licensed clinician determines it is medically appropriate.

5. Speed of intake and approval

Shed highlights a fast 2-minute approval assessment to check eligibility before clinician review and issuance of prescriptions. Embody describes clinician review handled entirely online with shipping within 1 to 2 days after approval. Both require a licensed provider review before prescriptions are issued.

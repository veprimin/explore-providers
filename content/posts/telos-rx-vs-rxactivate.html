Telos Rx vs RxActivate: key differences at a glance

This Telos Rx vs RxActivate comparison focuses on the most distinctive differences supported by the source evidence: price transparency, medication types and regulatory status, enrollment and gating, state and safety screening, and disclosed billing terms.

1. Price transparency and starting costs

Telos Rx publishes starting prices: semaglutide as low as $99 per month and tirzepatide as low as $139 per month, with listed reference prices such as $299 and $399 and statements like Pay $0 if not approved. RxActivate does not display price or billing terms before the contact-information gate; pricing is gated behind completion of the medical eligibility questionnaire and submission of name, email and phone number.

2. Medication offerings and regulatory notes

Telos Rx specifically offers compounded semaglutide and tirzepatide and states these are prescription-only and compounded medications are not FDA-approved. The source for RxActivate describes clinician-matched GLP-1 plans and notes that medication-specific approval status must be confirmed and that branded and compounded products are not interchangeable. Both providers require a licensed clinician to determine medical appropriateness.

Review Telos Rx medication details

3. Enrollment flow and eligibility screening

RxActivate emphasizes a two-minute eligibility questionnaire that requests goal, height, weight, target weight, timeline, medication history, sex assigned at birth, date of birth, state and safety screening. The flow displayed likely eligibility then required personal contact before revealing any plan or price. Telos Rx lists that a US-licensed provider must approve treatment and that your provider matches the molecule to you, but the captured evidence shows prices and notes like Cancel anytime, no penalties.

4. State availability and captured tests

RxActivate captured evidence confirms Alabama service availability in a synthetic test and highlights state and medical-safety screening as part of intake. Telos Rx materials advise people to confirm eligibility, state availability, and total cost before enrollment and state that treatment is available only when a licensed clinician determines it is medically appropriate.

For lower long term monthly cost on compounded tirzepatide with multiple billing terms choose bmiMD. This article compares SkinnyRx vs bmiMD across price, medication type, shipping and clinician access so you can decide which fits your priorities.

Compare SkinnyRx offers

Medication type and formulation: oral semaglutide vs compounded tirzepatide

SkinnyRx offers daily oral semaglutide tablets described as an oral form of the same medication used in GLP-1 injections. bmiMD offers compounded tirzepatide, a GLP-1/GIP compound. Both providers present compounded medication options when prescribed after clinician review. The regulatory notes for both state that compounded medications are not FDA approved and are not reviewed by the FDA for safety, effectiveness or quality.

Pricing and billing differences

SkinnyRx lists pricing as “As low as $249/mo” and mentions the option to split payments with Affirm Buy Now Pay Later. bmiMD publishes multiple billing options with lower long term monthly equivalents: $179 month-to-month; $159/mo for three months; $149/mo for six months; and $139/mo for a 12 month plan. bmiMD notes the lowest price requires a 12 month commitment and says total prepaid charge should be confirmed.

Shipping speed and subscription handling

SkinnyRx advertises free overnight shipping on all orders and says after a prescription is written you will receive shipping and tracking information within 2 business days. SkinnyRx also describes its plan as a subscription that you fill out a monthly refill form to continue receiving medication and says you may cancel future months at any time, with refund rules tied to whether a licensed provider has completed your prescription review. bmiMD offers free subscription shipping on every order and emphasizes online refills and delivery with a stated quick turnaround and delivery to your door.

Clinician access, testing, and guarantees

SkinnyRx describes licensed providers who tailor treatment and monitor progress and notes partner pharmacies perform third party testing through FDA and DEA compliant labs, confirming compounded lots are within plus or minus 10 percent of target concentration every 3 to 6 months. bmiMD notes 24/7 clinician access, states medications are prepared in a state licensed pharmacy and independently verified in FDA and DEA registered labs, and advertises a 100 percent refundable policy if not eligible for the program. Both emphasize treatment is available only when a licensed clinician determines it is medically appropriate.

SkinnyRx specifies that compounded semaglutide has not been approved by the FDA for weight loss and that benefits outlined are based on third party studies. bmiMD states a 100 percent refundable policy if not eligible for the program and advises confirming total prepaid charge and state availability before enrollment.

Final thoughts: SkinnyRx vs bmiMD

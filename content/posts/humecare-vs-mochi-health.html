How they differ at a glance: HumeCare+ vs Mochi Health

HumeCare+ sells a bundled GLP-1 protocol: physician-prescribed compounded semaglutide starting at $199/month or tirzepatide protocol at $299/month, combined with glycine, vitamins B6 and B12, monthly body-composition scans, an app, and a 10%-or-your-money-back promise over six months. Mochi Health positions live video visits with a doctor bookable in about 5 minutes, membership-plus-medication pricing starting from $99/month, and a 50% off first month promotion via code BESTWLHALFOFF.

Three most distinctive differences

1) Clinical model and visit style

HumeCare+ provides physician-prescribed treatment and continuous clinician optimization with unlimited clinician access and a U.S. care team available 24/7, centered around an app and ongoing monitoring. Mochi Health’s defining feature is synchronous live video visits with a doctor in about 5 minutes, a different clinical experience from the asynchronous text-review model used by many competitors.

2) Protocol contents and tracking

HumeCare+ bundles compounded GLP-1 medication with glycine, vitamin B12 and B6 built into the formulation and includes monthly body-composition scans that track lean mass versus fat. Mochi Health emphasizes access to GLP-1 prescriptions and a membership model; Mochi’s materials list compounded semaglutide, tirzepatide, oral options, and other treatments but do not present bundled glycine or monthly body-composition scans as part of a standard protocol.

3) Pricing structure and promotions

HumeCare+ lists semaglutide from $199/month and tirzepatide + B12 + glycine from $299/month; pricing is cash-pay and noted as a promotional discount that may change. Mochi Health combines membership plus medication with standard pricing starting from $99/month, offers 50% off the first month with code BESTWLHALFOFF, and lists promotional first-month rates such as $39 for some new-customer offers with ongoing terms that vary by state.

What the sources say about results, safety, and guarantees

HumeCare+ advertises a 10%-or-your-money-back promise over six months and reports internal, company-reported figures; it also states that compounded medications are not FDA-evaluated for safety, quality, or efficacy. Mochi Health provides promotional first-month pricing and notes that individual results are not guaranteed and will vary; it also uses compounded medications and repeats the same regulatory note about FDA evaluation. Both platforms state treatment is available when a clinician determines it is medically appropriate and that individual results may vary.

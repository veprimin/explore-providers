How these providers differ at a glance

This Shed vs CoreAgeRx comparison highlights the most distinct supported differences: medication options and regulatory notes, pricing and billing approach, coaching and support, assessment speed and eligibility, and what each is best for. All statements below come only from the supplied source material.

1. Medication options and regulatory status

Shed offers both FDA approved name-brand GLP-1 medications and compounded alternatives. The source explicitly notes that compounded medications are not FDA approved and are prepared by a licensed pharmacy based on a provider’s prescription. CoreAgeRx lists tirzepatide and semaglutide options with specific dose ranges but the captured content does not specify whether those are name-brand, compounded, or mixed; medication-specific approval status must be confirmed, and branded and compounded products are not interchangeable according to the source.

2. Pricing and billing model

Shed lists starting prices for compounded injections as starting at $199/month for compounded semaglutide and $299/month for compounded tirzepatide, plus a limited time 10% off your first month. The source also states Shed does not publish standard pricing and provides a personalized quote after intake. CoreAgeRx advertises flat monthly pricing with no membership fee and stable billing: tirzepatide at $129/month and semaglutide at $79/month with the claim of same price every month.

3. Coaching, support and service model

Shed emphasizes integrated, continuous coaching as part of treatment, positioning coaching as bundled rather than an optional add on. The source frames coaching as support through titration, lifestyle changes and plateaus. CoreAgeRx materials captured do not describe ongoing coaching or bundled behavioral support in the captured content; the focus in the source is price stability and dose availability.

4. Assessment time and enrollment steps

Shed offers a 2-minute approval assessment to check eligibility, after which a licensed clinician reviews the case and issues prescriptions if appropriate. CoreAgeRx advertises a 3-minute online visit and also notes treatment is available only when a licensed clinician determines it is medically appropriate. Both require clinician review before prescriptions are issued.

5. Best for and notable cautions

The source describes Shed as best for patients who want more than a prescription and prefer integrated coaching alongside medication. CoreAgeRx is described as best for people who confirm eligibility, medication, total cost, renewal terms and state availability before enrollment. The CoreAgeRx source also cautions that pricing, eligibility, pharmacy, state coverage and renewal terms may change.

HermanRx vs FeelGood Meds: what differs most

This comparison focuses on five supported differences: monthly price points and inclusions, molecule availability, shipping and timeline, membership or consultation fees, and the regulatory note about compounded medications. The primary keyword HermanRx vs FeelGood Meds appears early to match search intent.

1. Monthly pricing and what is included

HermanRx lists compounded semaglutide plus vitamin B12 at $249 per month and compounded tirzepatide plus B12 at $349 per month, described as all-inclusive covering the provider review, prescription, medication, ongoing support and free discreet shipping, with no membership fee and no separate consultation charge. FeelGood Meds lists compounded injections starting at $149 per month, compounded tablets starting at $249 per month, and a branded option shown from $1,999 per month. FeelGood Meds asserts no membership fee and free shipping.

2. Molecule and product options

HermanRx specifically lists both compounded semaglutide and compounded tirzepatide with B12 available. FeelGood Meds lists compounded injections and compounded tablets and also shows a branded medication option starting at $1,999 per month. Both providers note that treatment is available only when a licensed clinician determines it is medically appropriate.

3. Timeline and shipping

HermanRx publishes a rapid timeline: roughly a 5-minute assessment, ~24 hour provider review and ~72 hour delivery when prescribed, and a general FAQ range of 3 to 7 business days depending on treatment type and location. FeelGood Meds captured evidence lists free shipping but does not provide the same specific published timeline in the captured content.

4. Fees, membership and platform scope

HermanRx emphasizes no membership fee and no separate consultation charge and says it operates across five treatment categories where patients can consolidate care. FeelGood Meds also advertises no membership fee and no hidden fees in the captured content and highlights a weight loss money back guarantee in its messaging, though full details and eligibility are not supplied in the captured evidence.

5. Regulatory note about compounded medications

Both providers state that compounded medications, when offered, are not FDA-approved and are not reviewed by FDA for safety, effectiveness or quality. HermanRx explicitly notes that compounded semaglutide and tirzepatide are not FDA-approved as finished products and that availability depends on clinician determination. FeelGood Meds includes the same regulatory note in its captured content.

1. Pricing and plan structure

MedVi advertises a winter promotional first-month price of $179 including fast, free shipping, then a higher standalone month at $299 and a three-month option at $649. BetterMe Rx advertises lower monthly-equivalent pricing on longer prepaid plans, with semaglutide injection shown as $205 monthly or as low as $145/month on a 12-month plan. The key difference is MedVi emphasizes a low promotional entry price while BetterMe Rx emphasizes lower long-term per-month rates for longer prepaid terms.

2. Medication choices and formats

MedVi offers clinically dosed compounded semaglutide only. BetterMe Rx offers both semaglutide and tirzepatide, each available as injectable and oral formats according to their published materials. If having tirzepatide or a needle-free oral tablet is important, BetterMe Rx provides options MedVi does not.

3. Delivery, supplies and cancellation

Both providers include shipping and pharmacy compounding in their descriptions. MedVi states fast, free shipping is included. BetterMe Rx specifies free 1 to 2 day delivery and that supplies are included. BetterMe Rx also calls out cancel-anytime language next to its prepaid plan messaging, while MedVi highlights no clinic visits and an online process.

4. Clinical review and ongoing support

MedVi emphasizes a simple online intake with a licensed clinician review and 24/7 certified medical support. BetterMe Rx provides a free evaluation and a U.S. licensed provider review before dispensing, with treatment available only when a clinician determines it medically appropriate. Both use telehealth clinician judgment; MedVi additionally highlights round the clock support.

5. Regulatory and safety notes

Both sites explicitly note compounded medications are not FDA-approved and are not reviewed by FDA for safety, effectiveness, or quality. MedVi adds detailed language about compounded drugs being produced in FDA-regulated facilities but not FDA-approved or evaluated, and that prescriptions are at the discretion of providers. BetterMe Rx repeats that compounded medications are not FDA-approved and treatment is subject to clinician appropriateness.

What this means for you

MedVi vs BetterMe Rx boils down to whether you prioritize an accessible promotional entry and 24/7 support or whether you need medication choice and lower long-term monthly-equivalent pricing on longer prepaid plans. Neither provider bills insurance for compounded medications as described; eligibility and prescribing are subject to clinician review for both.

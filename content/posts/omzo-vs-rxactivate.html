Program type and what each provider offers

Omzo: Clinician-guided GLP-1 care that connects users with licensed U.S. providers and a primary compounded medication program. Omzo states its compounded medications are prepared and shipped by a state licensed sterile compounding pharmacy and that those compounded drugs are not FDA approved. Providers can alternatively write prescriptions for branded medications, but Omzo does not sell or ship branded products.

RxActivate: Describes clinician-matched GLP-1 plans after a two minute eligibility questionnaire. The site notes treatment is available only when a licensed clinician determines it is medically appropriate. It also records that compounded medications, when offered, are not FDA approved and not reviewed by FDA for safety, effectiveness or quality.

Pricing transparency and enrollment flow

Direct comparison: Omzo publishes starting prices for its programs while RxActivate hides pricing behind an eligibility and contact gate. Omzo lists semaglutide starting at $179 and tirzepatide starting at $279. RxActivate requires completion of the intake questionnaire and submission of name, email, and phone before revealing any plan or price; no price or billing terms were displayed prior to that step.

Clinician involvement and treatment decisions

Both Omzo and RxActivate rely on licensed clinicians to determine medical appropriateness. Omzo emphasizes clinician guided plans reviewed by licensed U.S. providers and notes that providers make the final determination as to whether to prescribe any medication. RxActivate also states treatment is available only when a licensed clinician determines it is medically appropriate and uses its eligibility flow to match users with clinicians.

Oak Longevity vs HermanRx is a straightforward comparison of two telehealth options for compounded GLP-1 weight-loss medications. Early verdict: Oak Longevity is clearly the lower-cost option, while HermanRx bundles B12, a specific timeline, and nationwide shipping into an all-inclusive package.

Key differences at a glance: Oak Longevity vs HermanRx

This comparison focuses on the most distinctive supported differences: price, what is included with the monthly fee, shipping and timing, subscription and billing model, and best-for guidance. Every claim below reflects the supplied source details and preserves qualifiers.

1. Price: clear gap in starting monthly cost

Oak Longevity lists semaglutide starting at $133/month and tirzepatide from $199/month. HermanRx lists compounded semaglutide with B12 at $249/month and tirzepatide with B12 at $349/month. The difference is the single-line monthly price, with Oak positioned as an industry-leading low price and HermanRx positioned as higher priced but all-inclusive with B12 added.

2. What the monthly price covers

Oak Longevity emphasizes one flat price for all dosages, free health coaching, and free shipping. It states billing is monthly on a non-recurring basis with no subscription or membership fees and that three-month prescriptions are billed upfront. HermanRx describes genuinely all-inclusive pricing: the provider review, prescription, medication, ongoing support and free discreet shipping are included with no separate membership or consultation fee. HermanRx explicitly bundles vitamin B12 with its compounded GLP-1 offerings.

3. Shipping, timeline, and availability

Oak advertises free shipping with tracking and same-day approval available for qualifying patients, with most patients receiving medication within a few days of approval. HermanRx publishes a roughly 24 hour provider review and about 72 hour delivery timeline for orders when prescribed, and notes a wider 3 to 7 business day window in general FAQs. HermanRx states free discreet shipping to all 50 states. Oak’s page captured asks what states it is available in but does not supply the all-states claim in the captured summary.

4. Clinical review and patient flow

Oak Longevity uses an asynchronous clinical review process where a licensed clinician reviews intake without requiring a video call. Oak states board-certified physicians in your state review every case. HermanRx describes a short assessment, an approximate 24 hour provider review, and a secure patient portal plus 24/7 patient support. Both providers say treatment is available only when a licensed clinician determines it is medically appropriate.

5. Regulatory and product type notes

Both providers offer compounded semaglutide and tirzepatide in part of their offerings. Each source includes the regulatory note that compounded medications are not FDA-approved and are not reviewed by FDA for safety, effectiveness or quality. Oak’s materials explicitly describe its offered medications as compounded versions of the active ingredients in Ozempic/Wegovy and Mounjaro/Zepbound. HermanRx states compounded medications are not FDA-approved and that it may offer FDA-approved or compounded medications depending on the provider recommendation.

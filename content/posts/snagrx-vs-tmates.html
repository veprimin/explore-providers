SnagRx vs TMates: the key differences

Both providers offer compounded semaglutide and state that compounded medications are not FDA-approved and are not reviewed by FDA for safety, effectiveness or quality.

1. Price model and required commitment

SnagRx advertises compounded semaglutide from $69/month and compounded tirzepatide from $119/month on a 12-month plan; the same meds are $99 and $149 month-to-month. SnagRx emphasises a price locked flat at every dose and at renewal. TMates offers a flat $199/month rate that it states applies at every dose with no step-up fees. Neither provider uses branded FDA-approved finished products.

2. Clinical access and oversight

TMates highlights 24/7 access to U.S.-based doctors and virtual check-in appointments for ongoing oversight, and states licensed coverage in all 50 states. SnagRx operates with a clinician network called OpenLoop Health that it states covers 48 states; SnagRx also states an OpenLoop clinician typically reviews intake within five hours and always within 24.

3. Pharmacy sourcing and shipping

SnagRx names its four US 503A partner pharmacies and promises next-day UPS/FedEx shipping in temperature-controlled packaging when orders are approved. TMates states its medications are formulated in U.S.-based facilities and offers free shipping; TMates says it ships to most states with availability based on medical eligibility.

4. Program extras and guarantees

SnagRx advertises a full refund if a prescription is declined and a 6-month weight-loss guarantee with terms. SnagRx also states LegitScript certification that patients can verify. TMates focuses on clinical supervision and does not provide the same public guarantee language in the supplied evidence.

Key differences at a glance for Shed vs SkinnyRx

This comparison highlights the most distinctive supported differences: medication format and options, coaching and support, pricing and discounts, delivery and payment flexibility, and regulatory notes about compounded products.

1. Medication format and options

Shed offers both FDA-approved name-brand GLP-1 options and compounded alternatives on one platform. SkinnyRx offers daily oral semaglutide tablets as a tablet format alternative to injections. That means Shed can give you branded injectables or compounded injections, while SkinnyRx delivers a tablet taken daily under the tongue.

2. Coaching and ongoing support

Shed integrates continuous coaching into treatment so coaching is bundled with medication and supports titration, lifestyle changes, and plateaus. SkinnyRx states it provides personalized care and ongoing support with licensed providers who adjust dosing as needed, and unlimited access to patient care and providers for questions.

3. Pricing, discounts, and payment details

Shed advertises compounded semaglutide injections starting at $199/month and compounded tirzepatide injections starting at $299/month, plus a limited time 10% off your first month. Shed does not publish standard pricing and says you receive a personalized quote after intake. SkinnyRx lists oral semaglutide as low as $249/month and notes options to split payments with Affirm.

4. Delivery, payment flexibility, and testing

SkinnyRx provides free overnight shipping on all orders and allows FSA/HSA payment, which is a specific pre-tax funding advantage. SkinnyRx says medication is processed and you receive shipping and tracking information within 2 business days after prescription. Shed’s captured evidence does not supply a shipping speed claim in the source material; it does offer a membership and coaching options.

5. Regulatory and quality notes

Both sources include regulatory notes that compounded medications are not FDA-approved and are not reviewed by FDA for safety, effectiveness, or quality. SkinnyRx adds that partner pharmacies perform third-party testing every 3 to 6 months to confirm concentration within plus or minus 10% of the active ingredient for compounded lots it prescribes. Shed clearly states compounded medications are not FDA-approved and are prepared by a licensed pharmacy based on a provider’s prescription.

How each provider handles clinician review and access

Both Shed and SkinnyRx require a clinician review before a prescription is issued. Shed uses a 2-minute online assessment to check eligibility quickly and then a licensed provider confirms treatment. SkinnyRx provides an online consultation and notes prescriptions are only available after a healthcare provider review; SkinnyRx also documents its cancellation and refund policy tied to the provider review stage.

Shed vs SkinnyRx: final take and who should choose which

In short, Shed vs SkinnyRx splits on format and support: Shed is strongest for patients who want both name-brand and compounded injectable options combined with continuous coaching and a fast 2-minute assessment. SkinnyRx is strongest for people who want an oral semaglutide tablet, the ability to use FSA/HSA pre-tax funds, free overnight shipping, and documented third-party testing of compounded lots.

Choose Shed if you want integrated coaching, access to both FDA-approved name-brand GLP-1 options and compounded injections, and a 2-minute approval assessment, plus 10% off your first month as stated in Shed materials.

Choose SkinnyRx if you prefer daily oral semaglutide tablets, need FSA/HSA payment flexibility, free overnight shipping, and the specific third-party testing practices described by SkinnyRx.

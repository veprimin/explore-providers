The Directmeds vs Telos Rx choice comes down to pricing structure, formulation options, shipping and state availability. Early verdict: choose Directmeds if you prioritize injectable and sublingual compounded options with free next-day shipping and no separate membership fee; choose Telos Rx if initial as-low-as price points of $99 for semaglutide or $139 for tirzepatide are the key factor and you will confirm eligibility with a US-licensed provider.

Compare Directmeds offers and shipping

How pricing and promotions compare

Directmeds advertises treatment starting at $147 and cites a summer promotion that saves $150, while its FAQ lists standard pricing of $297 per month for semaglutide and $399 per month for tirzepatide. Telos Rx presents semaglutide as low as $99 per month and tirzepatide as low as $139 per month, with the note that pricing applies if a licensed provider approves you. Both providers state that compounded medications are not FDA-approved.

Formulation and compounding differences

Directmeds explicitly offers compounded semaglutide and tirzepatide in injectable and sublingual formats and uses U.S.-based 503A compounding pharmacies. Telos Rx markets compounded semaglutide and tirzepatide but captured evidence does not list formulation formats beyond compounded status. Both sources emphasize that compounded medications are prescription-only and not FDA-approved, and both require clinician or provider review before prescribing.

Shipping, fees, cancellation and refunds

Directmeds includes medication, telehealth clinician review, supplies and free temperature-controlled next-day UPS or FedEx shipping, with no separate monthly membership fee and month-to-month cancellation; subscribe-and-save plans require the stated term and Directmeds offers a full refund if a prescription is not approved. Telos Rx notes cancel anytime with no penalties in captured text and states pay 0 if not approved in some captured lines; captured landing-page evidence also notes that actual pricing, eligibility and renewal terms may change and should be confirmed.

Clinical review and eligibility

Both Directmeds and Telos Rx require review by a licensed clinician or US-licensed provider and both state treatment is available only when a licensed clinician determines it is medically appropriate. Telos Rx emphasizes that the provider decides which molecule is right for you and notes individual results vary and are not guaranteed. Directmeds states treatment is subject to clinician review and that a full refund is available if a prescription is not approved.

State availability and regulatory notes

Directmeds is not available in Mississippi or Louisiana according to captured evidence. Telos Rx captured content warns that pricing, eligibility, pharmacy and state coverage may change and that patients should confirm eligibility and state availability before enrollment. Both providers include the regulatory note that compounded medications are not FDA-approved and are not reviewed by FDA for safety, effectiveness or quality.

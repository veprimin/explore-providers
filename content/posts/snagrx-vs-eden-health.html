SnagRx vs Eden Health: both sell telehealth GLP-1 programs, but they differ on pricing structure, compound vs unspecified sourcing, clinician coverage, shipping and included services. Early verdict: SnagRx is the lower-cost compounded option on a 12-month plan with a price lock; Eden Health lists higher monthly pricing and emphasizes a guided program with a lifetime price guarantee.

How pricing and price guarantees compare

On headline pricing SnagRx advertises compounded semaglutide from $69/month and tirzepatide from $119/month as 12-month plan rates; month-to-month equivalents are $99 and $149. SnagRx states the rate is locked flat at every dose and at renewal. Eden Health lists monthly pricing at every dose: semaglutide $99 and tirzepatide $129, and states a lifetime price guarantee. Both providers say no insurance is required and both include shipping in their monthly price.

Compounded medication status and regulatory notes

SnagRx explicitly sells compounded semaglutide and tirzepatide produced via named US 503A partner pharmacies and states its compounded medications are not FDA-approved finished products. The SnagRx page encourages prospective users to speak with providers about risks and notes that compounded medications are not reviewed by the FDA for safety, effectiveness or quality. Eden Health’s captured materials note that medication-specific approval status must be confirmed and that compounded medications, when offered, are not FDA-approved; full sourcing and regulatory detail was not published on the captured page.

Clinician review, eligibility and program support

Both platforms use clinician review to determine medical appropriateness. SnagRx describes an intake that takes about five minutes, an OpenLoop clinician review typically within five hours and always within 24 hours, and a clinician meeting after checkout who can decline prescription eligibility; a declined prescription is refunded in full. Eden Health describes a clinician-reviewed guided program but the captured page did not publish expanded details about review times, state coverage or eligibility criteria. SnagRx names its clinician network; Eden’s captured content did not name a clinician partner on the landing page.

Shipping, membership, and cancellation terms

SnagRx advertises next-day shipping via UPS or FedEx in temperature-controlled packaging, no membership fee, and the 12-month plan commitment to access the $69 and $119 rates with a cancel-anytime claim. Eden Health lists free expedited shipping, no membership fees, and monthly pricing at every dose with a lifetime price guarantee. Specific renewal or state-availability limits for Eden were not published on the captured page. SnagRx states clinician coverage across 48 states; Eden’s state coverage was not provided in the captured evidence.

Distinctive differences to weigh

  • Pricing model: SnagRx requires a 12-month plan to access the lowest advertised rates and emphasizes a locked flat rate across titration. Eden presents a single monthly price and a lifetime price guarantee on the captured page.
  • Compounded medications and disclosure: SnagRx names its US 503A partner pharmacies and repeatedly notes compounded status; Eden’s captured page warns confirmation is needed and did not publish pharmacy details on the captured page.
  • Clinician transparency: SnagRx names OpenLoop Health and provides claims about review timing and refund for declined prescriptions. Eden states clinician-reviewed programs but the captured content lacked specifics on clinician network or review timing.
  • Support and programming: Eden frames a guided GLP-1 program that pairs medication, care and support under one price. SnagRx states it provides prescription and telemed visits but does not include coaching or nutrition programming according to its captured evidence.

Conclusion and final guidance

SnagRx vs Eden Health comes down to whether you prioritize lower compounded pricing and named pharmacy and clinician details or a guided program with a single monthly price and a lifetime price guarantee. SnagRx is the lower-cost compounded option on the advertised 12-month plan with price locking, named 503A pharmacies, next-day shipping and an OpenLoop clinician network covering 48 states. Eden Health lists semaglutide at $99/month and tirzepatide at $129/month with free expedited shipping and a lifetime price guarantee, but the captured page did not publish expanded clinician, pharmacy or state-coverage details.

Choose SnagRx if you want compounded semaglutide from $69/month on a 12-month plan, named US 503A pharmacies, a stated price-lock at every dose and next-day shipping.

Choose Eden Health if you prefer the $99/month semaglutide or $129/month tirzepatide pricing presented as a guided program with a lifetime price guarantee and free expedited shipping, and if you will confirm eligibility, medication sourcing and renewal terms before enrolling.

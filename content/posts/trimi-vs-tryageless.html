Trimi vs Try Ageless pits two telehealth programs that sell compounded semaglutide and tirzepatide. Quick verdict: Trimi is centered on low flat per-dose pricing and overnight shipping, while Try Ageless emphasizes multiple formats including needle-free oral drops and a built-in discount structure.

Headline differences: pricing, formats, and guarantees

Both programs prescribe compounded GLP-1 medications via licensed U.S. clinicians and ship to your door, but they differ in three main ways: price structure, medication formats, and consumer protections. Trimi advertises compounded semaglutide from $99/mo on the annual plan and tirzepatide at $125/month with flat pricing that does not rise as dose increases and free overnight, temperature controlled shipping. Try Ageless lists semaglutide from $199/mo, tirzepatide from $249/mo, and needle-free tirzepatide oral drops from $159/mo on yearly plans and states a discount is already applied to every plan plus a stackable SLIM50 code for an extra $50 off.

Medication formats and flexibility

Format is a major decision point in Trimi vs Try Ageless. Trimi offers compounded semaglutide and tirzepatide as injectable compounded medications. Try Ageless sells compounded semaglutide injection, compounded tirzepatide injection, and needle-free tirzepatide oral drops. Try Ageless highlights that the drops are a daily sublingual liquid and that patients can switch molecules or formats under the same plan with no new consult fee.

Pricing structure and entry cost

Compare advertised entry points and pricing mechanics. Trimi promotes compounded semaglutide from $99/mo on annual plans and tirzepatide from $125/mo with flat pricing at every dose and a 6 month money back promise if you follow the plan and do not lose weight, subject to terms. Try Ageless emphasizes that a discount is already applied to every plan, listing the lowest yearly per-month price as $159/mo for drops, $199/mo for semaglutide, and $249/mo for tirzepatide, and that code SLIM50 stacks for an extra $50 off. Try Ageless also notes lowest advertised prices require the prepaid yearly plan and that monthly rates are higher on month-to-month billing.

Clinical oversight, pharmacies, and regulatory notes

Both vendors use licensed U.S. clinicians to prescribe compounded medications. Trimi states its clinical oversight is provided by Arora Health with Medical Director Dr. Sean Arora, MD, and that compounding is performed by state-licensed 503A pharmacies including VialsRx and GreenwichRx, with certificates of analysis available on request. Try Ageless says it is operated by Lovely Meds, Inc and dispenses compounded medications prepared by state-licensed U.S. 503A pharmacies and is LegitScript-certified and HIPAA-compliant. Both sources include the regulatory qualification that compounded medications are not FDA-approved and are not reviewed by the FDA for safety, effectiveness, or quality. Try Ageless specifically notes that oral tirzepatide drops are an off-label compounded format and that tirzepatide is not FDA-approved in oral form.

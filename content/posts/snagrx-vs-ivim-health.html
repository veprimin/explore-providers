This quick SnagRx vs IVIM Health comparison focuses on pricing structure, clinical program and outcomes claims, independent satisfaction signals, and fulfillment details.

Key differences at a glance

Compare the most distinctive differences supported by the sources: plan pricing and commitment, clinician protocol and outcome claims, external ratings and verification, and pharmacy/shipping details. Each item below references supplied facts only.

1. Pricing model and commitment

SnagRx advertises compounded semaglutide from $69/month and tirzepatide from $119/month as 12-month plan rates; month-to-month rates are $99 and $149. SnagRx states the 12-month figures are the plan rates and that the rate is locked flat at every dose for the life of treatment.

IVIM Health lists semaglutide at $75/month and tirzepatide at $133/month on its landing content, and describes an ongoing price that markets show as $174/month after initial pricing. IVIM also advertises a free month membership with code GIFT75 and a no-obligation free consultation valued at $75.

2. Program design and outcome claims

SnagRx is presented as a direct-pay telehealth platform delivering compounded GLP-1 medications with telemed clinician review via the OpenLoop Health network. SnagRx notes clinician review typically within five hours and next-day shipping for approved orders. SnagRx publishes that OpenLoop Health clinicians determine eligibility and that compounded medications are not FDA-approved finished products.

IVIM Health promotes a proprietary GLP-1 ID protocol and states that, based on its published study, IVIM patients following the protocol lost an average of 46% more weight over 68 weeks compared to another published clinical GLP-1 trial. The source qualifies that this is not a head-to-head study and that the 40% or 46% figures are protocol or internal claims, not independent randomized trials.

3. Third-party validation and trust signals

SnagRx states it holds LegitScript certification and names its four US 503A partner pharmacies and the OpenLoop Health clinician network. IVIM Health highlights unusually strong third-party satisfaction signals, claiming 4.9-star ratings on both Google and Trustpilot, which the source presents as a rare dual verification in this market.

4. Fulfillment and pharmacy details

SnagRx names its partner US 503A compounding pharmacies, says orders ship next-day via UPS or FedEx in temperature-controlled packaging, and discloses that compounded medications are produced in FDA-regulated facilities but are not FDA-approved products.

IVIM states its medications are prepared by independent, licensed compounding pharmacies, notes compounded drugs are not FDA-approved, and indicates not all services are available in every state.

Safety and regulatory notes

Both sources clearly state their GLP-1 products are compounded medications and are not FDA-approved finished products. SnagRx and IVIM each note that compounded drugs have not undergone FDA review for safety, efficacy, or quality and that clinician judgment determines prescription eligibility.

Medication type and regulatory notes

CoreAgeRx lists branded medication names and doses: “TIRZEPATIDE Any dose up to 7.5 mg” and “SEMAGLUTIDE Any dose up to 1.0 mg” and includes a regulatory note: “Compounded medications, when offered, are not FDA-approved and are not reviewed by FDA for safety, effectiveness or quality.” It also states treatment is available only when a licensed clinician determines it is medically appropriate.

Telos Rx explicitly markets “COMPOUNDED SEMAGLUTIDE & TIRZEPATIDE” and repeats that these compounded medications are prescription-only and not FDA-approved. Telos Rx describes mechanism differences: semaglutide acts on GLP-1; tirzepatide acts on GLP-1 and GIP, with a US-licensed provider deciding which is right for you.

Prescribing process, approvals, and clinician role

Both sources state a licensed clinician or US-licensed provider reviews and approves prescriptions. CoreAgeRx notes a “3-MINUTE ONLINE VISIT · NO INSURANCE REQUIRED” and that treatment is “available only when a licensed clinician determines it is medically appropriate.” Telos Rx similarly states medications are “prescribed only after review by a US-licensed provider” and that a US-licensed provider decides which medication is appropriate.

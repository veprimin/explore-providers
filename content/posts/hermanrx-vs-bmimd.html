Quick verdict: HermanRx vs bmiMD

If you want both semaglutide and tirzepatide available with an all-in monthly price and no membership or consultation fee, HermanRx is the clearer fit. If your primary goal is lower month-to-month pricing for tirzepatide with multi-month savings, bmiMD is the more budget-focused choice.

How these providers differ at a glance

Comparing HermanRx vs bmiMD centers on five differences supported in the source: which GLP-1 molecules are offered, published starting prices, subscription or commitment options, timeline and shipping promises, and membership or consultation fees.

1. Molecule availability

HermanRx offers both compounded semaglutide with vitamin B12 and compounded tirzepatide with B12. bmiMD’s evidence focuses on compounded tirzepatide only. If access to semaglutide specifically matters, HermanRx is the only one of the two that lists it.

2. Pricing structure and starting price

HermanRx lists semaglutide + B12 at $249/month and tirzepatide + B12 at $349/month, described as all-inclusive. bmiMD lists tirzepatide starting at $179 month-to-month and advertises lower per-month equivalents for multi-month plans down to $139/month for a 12-month plan. The sources preserve that these are compounded offerings and pricing formats differ by billing commitment.

3. Subscription commitments and multi-month savings

HermanRx emphasizes an all-inclusive single monthly price with no membership or consultation fee. bmiMD highlights four billing options with lower long-term monthly equivalents but notes the lowest price requires a 12-month commitment and that total prepaid charge should be confirmed. These are factual, provider-stated differences.

4. Timeline, shipping and clinician access

HermanRx advertises a roughly 5-minute assessment, about 24-hour provider review, and about 72-hour delivery if prescribed, with free discreet shipping to all 50 states. bmiMD advertises free subscription shipping, online refills and delivery, and 24/7 clinician access. Both source notes state compounded medications are not FDA-approved.

BetterMe Rx vs HealthRx centers on price structure, medication formats, shipping, clinician review and regulatory status. Early verdict: HealthRx advertises the lowest 12-month prepaid semaglutide price at $99/month while BetterMe Rx emphasizes four format options and free 1–2 day shipping, but both offer compounded GLP-1s that are not FDA-approved and require clinician review.

Price and prepaid plan differences

Both vendors disclose lower advertised monthly prices on longer prepaid plans. HealthRx lists a semaglutide plan from $99/month on a 12-month prepaid plan ($1,163 today; $1,188 at renewal) and tirzepatide plans starting at $179/month. BetterMe Rx lists 12-month equivalents starting at $145/month for compounded GLP-1 treatment and provides per-product monthly and 12-month-equivalent pricing: semaglutide injection $205/mo or as low as $145/mo on a 12-month plan; tirzepatide injection $275/mo or as low as $215/mo; oral semaglutide $250/mo or as low as $185/mo; oral tirzepatide $265/mo or as low as $215/mo.

Formats and medication options

BetterMe Rx highlights four medication and administration options: injectable and oral semaglutide and tirzepatide, with a needle-free daily tirzepatide tablet noted as uncommon in the cash-pay market. HealthRx offers compounded semaglutide or tirzepatide prepared per prescription but emphasizes that the clinician reviewing your file decides whether semaglutide or tirzepatide is the right fit.

Shipping, supplies and how care is packaged

BetterMe Rx bundles medication, provider review, compounding and supplies with free 1–2 day shipping. HealthRx emphasizes overnight cold chain shipping that is tracked and free on every plan. If cold chain handling is a priority, HealthRx specifies cold shipping; BetterMe Rx highlights fast free shipping and inclusion of supplies.

Clinician decisions, dosing and plan terms

HealthRx repeatedly notes that you are not picking a medication off a menu and that the clinician decides whether semaglutide or tirzepatide is appropriate or whether neither is. HealthRx also states your plan price does not change when your dose does. BetterMe Rx requires a licensed U.S. provider review and says treatment is available only when a licensed clinician determines it is medically appropriate. Both require clinician review and both tie lower prices to longer prepaid plans.

Regulatory and safety notes

Both sources clearly state that compounded medications are not FDA-approved and are not reviewed by FDA for safety, effectiveness or quality. HealthRx adds that compounded medication is prepared in a licensed 503A pharmacy cleanroom. BetterMe Rx repeats that compounded medications, when offered, are not FDA-approved and that treatment is available only with clinician approval.

Bottom line: BetterMe Rx vs HealthRx

In summary, BetterMe Rx emphasizes four format choices including injectable and oral options and free 1–2 day shipping with 12-month-equivalent pricing as low as $145/month. HealthRx advertises a lower 12-month semaglutide price from $99/month, overnight cold chain shipping, and clinician-directed choice of semaglutide or tirzepatide with plan price stability for dose changes. Both require clinician review and both sell compounded medications that are not FDA-approved.

Choose one if…

Choose BetterMe Rx if you want both injectable and oral compounded GLP-1 options, free 1–2 day shipping, and fixed per-product pricing with lower monthly equivalents on prepaid multi-month plans.

Choose HealthRx if your priority is the lowest advertised 12-month semaglutide price from $99/month, overnight cold chain tracked shipping, and a clinician-directed decision about which GLP-1 is appropriate.

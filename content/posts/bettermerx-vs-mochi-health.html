How they differ: 4 key comparison points

1. Pricing structure and starting costs

BetterMe Rx lists compounded GLP-1 treatment from $145/month on a 12-month plan, with specific monthly prices and lower 12-month equivalents: semaglutide injection $205/mo or as low as $145/mo on a 12-month plan; tirzepatide injection $275/mo or as low as $215/mo; oral semaglutide $250/mo or as low as $185/mo; oral tirzepatide $265/mo or as low as $215/mo. Mochi Health presents a membership-plus-medication model with pricing from $99/month and promotional offers such as 50% off the first month with code BESTWLHALFOFF and a promotional first-month rate as low as $39 for some treatments. Mochi notes that total monthly cost varies by plan and medication.

2. Visit model and clinical experience

BetterMe Rx uses an online intake reviewed by a licensed U.S. provider; a partnered U.S. pharmacy compounds and ships the prescription subject to clinician review. Mochi Health distinguishes itself by offering live video visits with a doctor bookable in about 5 minutes for real-time discussion, with ongoing appointment availability and 24/7 support. The sources state Mochi’s synchronous video model differs from the asynchronous text-based reviews many competitors use.

3. Medication formats offered

Both providers emphasize compounded GLP-1s, but formats differ in presentation. BetterMe Rx offers four formats: injectable and oral semaglutide and tirzepatide, including a needle-free daily tirzepatide tablet option. Mochi Health lists compounded semaglutide and tirzepatide injections and pills, and additional compounded agents such as orforglipron and other prescriptions across categories, with pricing examples for several compounded options.

4. Shipping, membership, and regulatory notes

BetterMe Rx includes medication, provider review, pharmacy compounding, supplies and free 1 to 2 day shipping and advertises no membership fee, no insurance requirement, no hidden fees, and cancel-anytime language on prepaid plans. Mochi Health operates as membership plus medication, with terms that vary by state, promotional first-month pricing, and ongoing membership fees after promotions; members continue care beyond their first month. Both sources explicitly note that compounded medications are not FDA-approved and are not reviewed by FDA for safety, effectiveness or quality.

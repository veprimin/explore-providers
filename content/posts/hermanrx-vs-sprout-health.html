HermanRx vs Sprout Health comes down to four clear differences: pricing structure, onboarding speed, regulatory framing for compounded meds, and scope of services. Early verdict: choose HermanRx if you want transparent all-in monthly pricing and a multi-service telehealth platform; choose Sprout Health if you prioritize a lower first-month price and very fast approval.

Direct comparison overview: what each provider emphasizes

Both HermanRx and Sprout Health are direct-pay telehealth options for GLP-1 treatment, but they emphasize different selling points. HermanRx highlights all-inclusive monthly pricing for compounded semaglutide + B12 at $249/month and compounded tirzepatide + B12 at $349/month with no membership or separate consultation fee. Sprout Health markets a $50 off first month promotion that brings the initial cost to $199, with ongoing pricing stated as $249/month, plus a focus on “lightning-quick approval” and outcome framing of 10–26% body weight loss over 12 months.

Key differences organized by most distinctive factors

1. Pricing model and fees

HermanRx: all-inclusive monthly prices that explicitly cover the provider review, prescription, medication, ongoing support, and free discreet shipping, and the provider states there is no membership fee and no separate consultation charge. Sprout Health: an introductory discount of $50 off the first month (first month $199) with ongoing standard pricing of $249/month according to the supplied evidence. Sprout Health’s marketing notes the price rises after the discount.

2. Onboarding speed and delivery timelines

HermanRx publishes a specific timeline: roughly a 5-minute assessment, about 24-hour provider review, and about 72-hour delivery if prescribed, while also noting a wider 3–7 business day window for many orders. Sprout Health emphasizes “lightning-quick approval” and rapid shipping after approval but does not provide the same specific 24/72-hour timeline in the captured evidence. If precise published delivery timing matters, HermanRx provides more specific published estimates.

3. Regulatory and compounded medication framing

Both providers note that compounded medications are not FDA-approved as finished products. HermanRx explicitly states its compounded semaglutide and tirzepatide are not FDA-approved and are not reviewed by FDA for safety, effectiveness, or quality. Sprout Health’s evidence also notes compounded medication status must be confirmed and that compounded products are not interchangeable with branded Ozempic or Wegovy in the captured materials.

4. Outcome claims and marketing framing

Sprout Health frames outcomes around a 10–26% body weight reduction over 12 months, citing clinical literature as context, and offers a clear outcome range in its marketing. HermanRx includes marketing language that has referred to about 20% semaglutide weight loss, and the source notes that this advertised ~20% claim exceeds the ~15% average reported in STEP 1; the captured evidence calls out that HermanRx states its compounded medications are not FDA-approved.

5. Scope of services and platform breadth

HermanRx operates across five treatment categories: weight loss, hormones, longevity, hair loss, and sexual health, making it a potential option for patients who want to consolidate multiple treatment types on one platform. Sprout Health is presented as a focused GLP-1 platform built for fast, no-friction entry into GLP-1 therapy.

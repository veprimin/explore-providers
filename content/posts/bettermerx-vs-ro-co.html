BetterMe Rx vs Ro.co: 3 top differences that matter

Below are the most distinctive supported differences between BetterMe Rx and Ro.co drawn strictly from the sources: medication regulatory status and formulation, pricing structure, and platform positioning and convenience.

1. Medication type and regulatory status

BetterMe Rx sells compounded semaglutide and tirzepatide in injectable and oral formats through licensed U.S. providers. The source repeatedly notes that compounded medications are not FDA-approved and are not reviewed by FDA for safety, effectiveness or quality. Ro offers access to the Wegovy pill, described as the first and only FDA-approved oral GLP-1 weight loss medication available on the platforms compared. Ro’s offering is an FDA-approved finished product rather than a compounded medication.

2. Formulation variety and needle aversion

BetterMe Rx provides four medication and administration options: injectable semaglutide, injectable tirzepatide, oral semaglutide, and oral tirzepatide, including a needle-free daily tirzepatide tablet option noted as uncommon in the cash-pay market. Ro is singled out as the mainstream platform offering the Wegovy pill, which removes the need for injections for patients who are injection-averse.

3. Pricing model and entry costs

BetterMe Rx advertises compounded GLP-1 treatment from $145/month on a 12-month plan, with monthly prices shown and lower 12-month equivalents: semaglutide injection $205 monthly or as low as $145/month on a 12-month plan; tirzepatide injection $275 monthly or as low as $215/month; oral semaglutide $250 monthly or as low as $185/month; oral tirzepatide $265 monthly or as low as $215/month. BetterMe Rx notes longer 3-, 6-, or 12-month supplies are billed according to the selected plan and that the lowest prices require a 12-month plan.

Ro advertises a low get-started entry: Get Started Online for Just $39. The source states that ongoing branded medication pricing depends on the treatment plan and medication prescribed and that branded FDA-approved medications cost significantly more than compounded alternatives.

Bottom line and how to choose

BetterMe Rx vs Ro.co comes down to compounded options and lower prepaid prices versus an FDA-approved oral pill and brand trust. BetterMe Rx offers multiple administration formats with lower monthly-equivalent pricing on longer prepaid plans but its compounded medications are not FDA-approved. Ro offers the FDA-approved Wegovy pill and a $39 entry, with ongoing branded medication pricing that may be higher than compounded alternatives.

Choose BetterMe Rx if you want multiple injectable and needle-free oral compounded GLP-1 options and are comfortable with prepaid multi-month billing and the fact that compounded medications are not FDA-approved.

Choose Ro.co if you need or prefer an FDA-approved oral GLP-1, want a low $39 get-started entry, and prioritize regulatory clarity over the lower costs of compounded alternatives.

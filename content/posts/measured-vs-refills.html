How Measured and Refills differ at a glance

Both companies offer compounded GLP-1 products with prescriptions required and note that compounded medications are not FDA-approved. The most distinctive differences supported in the source material are starting price and promotional details, pricing model and dose pricing, shipping speed and discretion, clinical and support features, and regulatory and testing claims. The sections below compare those points directly.

1. Price and promotional structure

Measured lists promotional compounded semaglutide from $89/mo and compounded tirzepatide $189/mo. Refills lists GLP-1 from $99/mo (~$5/day) and tirzepatide at $159/mo. Measured repeatedly cites promotional pricing and internal outcome claims; Refills highlights flat pricing at every dosage and a 40% off new patient special in its captured bullets.

2. Pricing model and dosing charges

Measured presents promotional per month prices and emphasizes compounded semaglutide and tirzepatide options. Refills explicitly promotes flat pricing, the same low price at every dosage, and no titration upcharges. That means Measured promotes specific promotional starting prices while Refills promotes a uniform price across dosages, per the captured evidence.

3. Shipping speed and discretion

Measured markets free two-day shipping and fast free shipping on every order. Refills offers free discreet shipping and express shipping, with tracking and 2 business day shipment processing described after prescription issuance. Measured frames speed as two-day delivery; Refills emphasizes discretion and express handling.

4. Clinical support and access

Measured highlights same-day prescription decisions, unlimited provider messaging, 24/7 Care Team, and dietitian support. Refills describes a licensed clinician review of intake, possible chat with a doctor or direct prescription within 24 hours, unlimited access to patient care team and clinicians at no extra cost, and a 24/7 health concierge. Both require an Rx and indicate prescriptions are issued at provider discretion.

5. Regulatory notes and pharmacy sourcing

Both sources state compounded medications are not FDA-approved and are not reviewed by FDA for safety, effectiveness, or quality. Refills adds that its compounding pharmacies are Legitscript-certified U.S. compounding pharmacies with third-party USP testing and that pharmacies are licensed and inspected. Measured notes compounded medications are not FDA-approved and that treatment is available only when a licensed clinician determines it is medically appropriate.

How the programs differ: formats, pricing and verification

Both providers offer compounded GLP 1s and state that treatment is available only when a licensed clinician determines it is medically appropriate. The comparison centers on three distinctive differences: available formats and medications, pricing structure and discounts, and compounding verification and shipping.

Formats and medications

BetterMe Rx documents four specific options: injectable semaglutide and tirzepatide plus oral semaglutide and oral tirzepatide, noting a needle free daily tirzepatide tablet as an uncommon option in the cash pay market. LTTL describes compounded GLP 1 medications formulated to help regulate appetite and offers “Full dose GLP 1” but captured details do not list the same explicit four format breakdown. Both note clinician review and that compounded medications are not FDA approved.

Pricing structure and discounts

BetterMe Rx lists multi month equivalents and specific monthly prices with qualifiers: compounded GLP 1 treatment from $145/month on a 12 month plan; semaglutide injection $205 monthly or as low as $145/month on a 12 month plan; tirzepatide injection $275 monthly or as low as $215/month; oral semaglutide $250 monthly or as low as $185/month; oral tirzepatide $265 monthly or as low as $215/month. Prices shown monthly include $205, $275, $250 and $265 respectively. BetterMe Rx also says longer 3, 6, or 12 month supplies are billed according to the selected plan and that lowest prices require a 12 month plan.

LTTL materials captured indicate a starting price reference of $140 and promotional language “up to $140 off your first month code auto applied” and price points such as $249 and $129 in the captured evidence. LTTL emphasizes “same price, every dose.” The LTTL capture cautions that current details were not published or could not be verified on the captured affiliate landing page and recommends confirming eligibility and total cost before enrollment.

Compounding verification, shipping and quality notes

BetterMe Rx states medication, provider review, pharmacy compounding, supplies and free 1 2 day shipping are included and that a partnered U S pharmacy compounds and ships the prescription. It also repeats that compounded medications are not FDA approved and are not reviewed by FDA for safety effectiveness or quality.

LTTL states medications are compounded by U S licensed pharmacies and “independently verified in FDA registered labs” with confirmation every 3 6 months to stay within plus or minus 10 percent of target strength in the captured evidence. LTTL also repeats that compounded medications are not FDA approved and are not reviewed by FDA for safety effectiveness or quality. The LTTL capture includes language about delivery timing and formulations being prepared and delivered to your door.

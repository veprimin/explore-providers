Medication options and how they are delivered

Both platforms offer clinician-guided GLP-1 care and access to compounded semaglutide and tirzepatide, but they present the options differently. Omzo connects patients with licensed U.S. providers who may prescribe either compounded medications or write prescriptions for branded medications that patients must fill themselves. JoinFridays offers compounded tirzepatide and semaglutide on its platform and also lists name-brand options with insurance support for separate pricing tiers.

Difference 1: Upfront cost model

Difference 2: Flexibility and commitments

JoinFridays explicitly states no long-term contracts, no membership fees, and the ability to cancel anytime. Omzo’s materials advise confirming eligibility, total cost, renewal terms, and state availability before enrollment, indicating terms and availability may change and that patients should verify specifics before starting.

Difference 3: How compounded and branded options are handled

Omzo describes a model where clinical providers may use a compounded medication program or alternatively write a prescription for branded medications; Omzo does not sell or ship branded drugs and patients fill prescriptions themselves. JoinFridays lists both compounded programs and name-brand options as distinct pricing tiers and notes insurance support for name-brand choices. Both sources include the qualifier that compounded medications are not FDA-approved.

Difference 4: Pricing transparency and promos

JoinFridays advertises active promo codes and specific savings ranges in captured content, and details promo codes such as NEWYOU12 and NYNY12 in its FAQ. Omzo emphasizes clinician-guided care and lists starting prices without captured promo-code details in the supplied evidence.

What the regulatory notes mean for both

Both sources clearly state that compounded medications are not FDA-approved and are not reviewed by the FDA for safety, effectiveness, or quality. Omzo explicitly states its compounded drugs are prepared by a state-licensed sterile compounding pharmacy and are not FDA-approved. JoinFridays includes the same regulatory qualifier for compounded medications.

Final thoughts on Omzo vs JoinFridays

Omzo centers on clinician-reviewed programs with quoted starting prices such as semaglutide starting at $179 and an option for providers to prescribe branded medications that patients then fill. JoinFridays competes on lower entry pricing, a monthly model starting at $117/month, no long-term contracts, and aggressive promo codes on compounded tirzepatide and semaglutide. Both platforms rely on licensed clinicians and both note that compounded medications are not FDA-approved.

How TMates vs Eden Health differ at a glance

This comparison focuses on five key differences supported by the sources: monthly price and pricing guarantees, clinician access and hours, medication options and formulation, state coverage and shipping, and compounded medication regulatory notes.

1. Monthly price and pricing model

TMates: $199/month, every dose, no step-up fees. TMates markets a flat $199/month rate at every dose, which the source repeats as the platform’s primary pricing model.

Eden Health: Semaglutide $99/month and Tirzepatide $129/month, with a lifetime price guarantee and monthly pricing at every dose. Eden emphasizes a guaranteed monthly price for life and no membership fees.

2. Clinician access and program support

TMates highlights 24/7 access to U.S.-based doctors across all 50 states, not just messaging support, and virtual check-in appointments for ongoing medical oversight. Eden’s materials emphasize a guided GLP-1 program with clinician review, but captured landing-page evidence did not verify after-hours access or detailed clinician availability.

3. Medication options and formulation

TMates offers clinically dosed compounded semaglutide formulated in U.S.-based facilities and notes that its medication is compounded. Eden lists both semaglutide and tirzepatide pricing ($99 and $129 respectively) and presents a guided program for each; captured evidence notes that when compounded medications are offered they are not FDA-approved and that medication-specific approval status must be confirmed.

4. State coverage, shipping, and logistics

TMates claims licensed coverage in all 50 states and free shipping, with availability based on medical eligibility. Eden advertises free expedited shipping and no insurance required, while advising customers to confirm pricing, eligibility, pharmacy, state coverage and renewal terms before enrollment.

5. Regulatory and product notes

Both sources include regulatory caution: TMates explicitly states compounded medications are not FDA-approved and are not reviewed by FDA for safety, effectiveness or quality. Eden’s captured evidence similarly notes that medication-specific approval status must be confirmed and that branded and compounded products are not interchangeable.

Conclusion: TMates vs Eden Health

Pricing and introductory offers

Shed lists compounded Semaglutide starting at $199/month and compounded Tirzepatide starting at $299/month and advertises 10% off your first month. Sprout Health advertises $50 off the first month, bringing the initial cost to $199, with ongoing pricing described as $249/month after the discount. Medication types: name-brand versus compounded

Shed offers both FDA-approved name-brand GLP-1 medications and compounded options on the same platform, giving patients a choice based on budget and preference. Sprout Health offers compounded semaglutide and does not provide name-brand Ozempic or Wegovy options. Both providers note that compounded medications are not FDA-approved when offered.

What that means in practice

  • Shed: choice of name-brand and compounded; name-brand carries regulatory assurance and higher cost, compounded options are more affordable.
  • Sprout Health: compounded semaglutide only; positioned as a no-frills, direct-pay model.

Coaching and care model

Shed integrates continuous coaching into treatment rather than only issuing a prescription. The platform markets coaching as support through titration, lifestyle changes, and plateaus. Sprout Health emphasizes a fast, hassle-free entry to GLP-1 treatment and frames outcomes, but does not advertise an integrated ongoing coaching model in the captured evidence.

Approval speed and onboarding

Both platforms use online intake and clinician review, but Sprout Health highlights a lightning-quick approval process and positions itself for fast entry. Shed advertises a 2-minute approval assessment followed by clinician review before a prescription is issued. If minimal onboarding friction is the priority, Sprout Health is presented as the faster option; if rapid eligibility screening plus coaching is preferred, Shed combines a quick assessment with ongoing support.

Outcome framing and guarantees

Sprout Health frames outcomes around a 10 to 26 percent body weight reduction over 12 months and explicitly states it does not offer a formal outcome guarantee in the captured evidence. Shed references clinical trial results for Wegovy in marketing copy and notes a weight-loss guarantee is detailed in its Terms and Conditions; the source instructs readers to refer to those terms for full details. Neither provider promises individual results and both note results may vary.

Regulatory and prescribing notes

Both sources make regulatory distinctions clear. Shed states that compounded medications are not FDA-approved and are prepared by a licensed pharmacy from a provider prescription. Sprout Health also notes compounded medications, when offered, are not FDA-approved and that treatment is available only when a licensed clinician determines it is medically appropriate.

Key price and product differences

Price is the clearest distinction in the altRx vs FeelGood Meds comparison. altRx advertises compounded semaglutide starting at $89/month and compounded tirzepatide starting at $149/month. Medication options and formulation differences

altRx focuses on compounded semaglutide and compounded tirzepatide as GLP-1 and GLP-1+GIP options and promotes a complete treatment kit. FeelGood Meds lists compounded injections and compounded tablets and also displays a branded medication option at a much higher starting price. Care model, clinician review and availability

Both providers require clinician review to determine medical appropriateness. altRx states a licensed provider will review your health evaluation and conduct a telehealth consultation, which may be live video or asynchronous, and that the provider may prescribe compounded GLP-1 or GLP-1/GIP based on the health profile. FeelGood Meds notes treatment is available only when a licensed clinician determines it is medically appropriate and that pricing and eligibility are subject to the live offer. altRx also states the membership is available to residents of all 50 U.S. states with prescribing authority varying by state; FeelGood Meds does not provide a captured statewide availability statement in the supplied evidence.

Fees, shipping, cancellation and guarantees

Both services present no membership or hidden fees and no insurance required. altRx emphasizes no commitment and cancel anytime, same price every month messaging in captured landing page content. altRx includes buy now, pay later options through Affirm, Klarna, and Afterpay. FeelGood Meds highlights free shipping and states a Weight Loss Money Back Guarantee on its page, while also reminding that compounded medications are not FDA-approved.

Regulatory and quality notes both providers share

Both providers include the regulatory reminder that compounded medications are not FDA-approved and are not reviewed by FDA for safety, effectiveness or quality. altRx specifically notes prescriptions are filled by state-licensed 503A compounding pharmacies in its network. FeelGood Meds notes compounded medications when offered are not FDA-approved and are not reviewed by FDA.

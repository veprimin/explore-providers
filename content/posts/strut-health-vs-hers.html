How Strut Health vs Hers differ at a glance

The core difference between Strut Health vs Hers is treatment type and regulatory status. Strut Health specializes in oral-focused programs and offers compounded Semaglutide when medically necessary. Hers centers on FDA-approved brand-name Wegovy and other name-brand GLP-1s, explicitly stating it does not offer compounded medications.

Treatment form and regulatory status

Strut Health promotes oral Semaglutide lozenges as a specialization and notes GLP-1 compounded weight management therapy with Semaglutide. The source states compounded medications are not FDA-approved and are prepared by a U.S.-licensed compounding pharmacy pursuant to an individualized prescription, with medical necessity required by a licensed physician. Hers offers FDA-approved Wegovy, a brand-name semaglutide, and explicitly says it does not provide compounded medications.

Price structure and membership

Starting price differs in how costs are structured. Strut Health lists Oral GLP-1 treatment From $199 per Month and landing-page notes include No Hidden Fees, Free shipping, and Cancel anytime. Hers lists Wegovy from $149/mo and shows a range for other medications. Hers also requires an active Hers Weight Loss Membership for medication access, with a first month fee and auto-renewal terms noted in source text; medication and membership are billed separately and medication is not available without an active membership.

Clinical support and program focus

Both emphasize clinical oversight and ongoing communication but with different models. Strut Health emphasizes guided consultations and ongoing progress communication with a clinical focus on oral medication management. Hers highlights a personalized care team with ongoing dose check-ins and brands Wegovy as doctor-trusted FDA-approved GLP-1 options.

Eligibility, availability, and regulatory notes

Strut Health states compounded Semaglutide is only available for patients that fail or cannot use commercially available GLP-1 medications and that medical necessity is required by a licensed physician. The source repeats that compounded medications are not FDA-approved. Hers notes that Ozempic and Mounjaro are FDA-approved for type 2 diabetes and may be prescribed off-label for weight loss at a clinician’s discretion, and that Wegovy and Zepbound are FDA-approved for weight loss. Hers also warns not all options are available in all 50 states and that prescription and clinical eligibility are required.

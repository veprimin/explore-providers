Gala vs Refills: pricing and billing model

Price is the most tangible difference. Refills advertises GLP-1 from $99/month (~$5/day) and lists tirzepatide at $159/month, with flat pricing the same at every dosage and new patient specials including 40% off. Gala’s landing page advertises a flat all-inclusive price that covers the provider visit, medication, shipping and ongoing care and shows a promotion labeled $220 off; Gala also lists compounded semaglutide from $249/month in its longer headline, but the landing-page evidence notes the base price was not exposed in readable page text. Both providers describe month-to-month plans you can cancel anytime.

Medication options and clinical access

Both Gala and Refills offer compounded GLP-1 therapies. Gala specifically lists compounded semaglutide and tirzepatide and includes the provider visit and prescription in its flat price. Refills lists semaglutide and tirzepatide pricing and notes availability of a GIP+GLP-1 dual agonist. Refills describes a health intake quiz followed by clinician review; depending on needs you may chat with a doctor or receive a prescription within 24 hours. Gala states treatment is available only when a licensed clinician determines it is medically appropriate.

Shipping, memberships and cancellation

Both providers advertise free shipping and no monthly membership or app fees. Gala emphasizes free two-day shipping and a single flat price with no hidden fees, plus a cancel anytime claim. Refills promises free discreet shipping, express shipping, and month-to-month plans with no contracts and no cancellation fees. If shipping speed is critical, Gala highlights two-day delivery while Refills emphasizes discreet and express options.

Support, monitoring and extras

Gala highlights provider check-ins and dose monitoring included as part of the service. Refills highlights free 24/7 health concierge support and unlimited access to its patient care team and clinicians for questions, plus text and email shipment updates. Choose based on whether you prioritize scheduled provider check-ins or around-the-clock concierge access.

Safety and regulatory considerations

Both providers caution that compounded medications are not FDA-approved and are not reviewed by the FDA for safety, effectiveness or quality. Refills notes its compounding pharmacies are licensed and inspected, and that clinicians are part of partner telehealth networks. Gala states that compounded medications, when offered, are not FDA-approved and are not reviewed by the FDA. If you need FDA-approved branded products or specific regulatory assurances, neither landing-page summary supplies that evidence.

Bottom line and how to choose

Gala vs Refills comes down to price transparency, support style and shipping. Refills publishes a clear starting price of $99/month for GLP-1 and highlights 24/7 health concierge support and nationwide availability; Gala markets a flat all-inclusive plan that includes the provider visit, a $220 promotion, and free two-day shipping but does not expose a readable base price on the landing page. Both use compounded medications and include clinician review.

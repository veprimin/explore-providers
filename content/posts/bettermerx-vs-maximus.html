Price and plan structure: BetterMe Rx vs Maximus

Maximus advertises a straightforward flat rate of $132.99/month for compounded semaglutide, described as one of the lower flat rates. BetterMe Rx lists monthly prices and lower 3, 6, or 12-month equivalents: semaglutide injection $205/month or as low as $145/month on a 12-month plan; tirzepatide injection $275/month or as low as $215/month; oral semaglutide $250/month or as low as $185/month; oral tirzepatide $265/month or as low as $215/month. BetterMe Rx notes that the lowest prices require a 12-month plan and that longer plans are billed according to the selected term.

Medication selection and formats

BetterMe Rx explicitly offers both semaglutide and tirzepatide in injectable and oral (tablet) compounded formats. Maximus is presented primarily as a semaglutide program; its captured materials focus on once-weekly semaglutide injections and compounded semaglutide offerings. Maximus has limited published detail on tirzepatide availability in the supplied evidence.

Shipping, billing, and program features

Both providers use a direct-pay telehealth model with no membership fees documented in the supplied evidence. BetterMe Rx includes medication, provider review, pharmacy compounding, supplies and free 1–2 day shipping; it also highlights cancel-anytime language alongside prepaid longer-term plans. Maximus advertises free priority or expedited shipping, prescriptions approved by licensed doctors, and that if a patient is not approved for treatment the medication costs will be fully refunded according to the captured claims. Maximus also notes availability in all 50 states except Mississippi in the supplied details.

Provider review, telemedicine, and sourcing

BetterMe Rx requires a licensed U.S. provider review and a partnered U.S. pharmacy to compound and ship the prescription. Maximus likewise uses licensed doctors to approve prescriptions and fills through trusted U.S. pharmacies via telemedicine; Maximus emphasizes convenient telemedicine access with no clinic visits. Maximus is described as a smaller, newer platform with a thinner public track record in the supplied evidence; BetterMe Rx highlights free provider review and discreet U.S. shipping.

Regulatory note and best-use cases

Both sources include the regulatory qualifier that compounded medications are not FDA-approved and are not reviewed by the FDA for safety, effectiveness or quality. BetterMe Rx specifically repeats that compounded medications, when offered, are not FDA-approved. Maximus materials likewise state the medication is compounded and not an FDA-approved finished product while noting it uses the same active ingredient as branded GLP-1 products.

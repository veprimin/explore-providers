Shed vs HealthRx: quick verdict

Shed pairs name-brand and compounded GLP-1 options with integrated coaching and a 2-minute approval assessment, while HealthRx emphasizes lower prepaid pricing for compounded semaglutide or tirzepatide and overnight cold shipping. Early verdict: choose Shed if you want coaching plus the option of name-brand drugs; choose HealthRx if lowest advertised compound price and overnight cold shipping matter most.

How they differ: pricing, medication choice, coaching, shipping, and approval

1. Pricing model and advertised starting price

Shed lists compounded semaglutide injections starting at $199/month and compounded tirzepatide injections starting at $299/month, plus a limited time 10% off your first month. HealthRx advertises plans from $99/month on a 12-month prepaid semaglutide plan and tirzepatide plans starting at $179/month, with the caveat that the lowest advertised price requires a longer prepaid plan.

See HealthRx prepaid plan pricing

2. Medication options: name-brand availability versus compounded focus

Shed offers both FDA-approved name-brand GLP-1 medications and compounded alternatives on one platform, letting patients choose based on budget and preference. HealthRx emphasizes compounded semaglutide or tirzepatide prepared per prescription; brand-name GLP-1s are referenced as typically costing about $1,000+/mo at retail but HealthRx focuses on compounded plans.

3. Coaching and care model

Shed integrates continuous coaching into treatment rather than simply issuing a prescription, positioning coaching as part of the bundled monthly cost. HealthRx materials captured emphasize the plan, shipping and clinician review but do not document an integrated continuous coaching model in the captured evidence.

4. Shipping, dosing and plan mechanics

HealthRx advertises overnight cold shipping with cold chain, tracked, free on every plan and states your plan price does not change when your dose does. Shed notes medication is shipped to your home after clinician approval, but the landing evidence highlights the 2-minute assessment and bundled coaching rather than specific shipping promises in the captured evidence.

5. Approval process and clinician review

Shed uses a 2-minute online assessment to check eligibility and then a licensed provider reviews the case before issuing a prescription. HealthRx advises that the clinician reviewing your file decides whether semaglutide or tirzepatide is the right fit, or whether neither is; captured copy emphasizes you are not simply picking a medication off a menu.

What to watch for before you enroll

Both providers state that treatment is available only when a licensed clinician determines it is medically appropriate and both note that compounded medications are not FDA-approved. Shed notes name-brand options carry significantly higher costs than compounded alternatives, and HealthRx warns the lowest advertised monthly price requires a longer prepaid plan and that pricing, eligibility, pharmacy, state coverage and renewal terms may change.

Conclusion: Shed vs HealthRx

In short, Shed vs HealthRx hinges on whether you prioritize integrated coaching and access to name-brand GLP-1s or whether your priority is the lowest advertised compounded plan with overnight cold shipping. Both require clinician review and both offer compounded medication options that are not FDA-approved.

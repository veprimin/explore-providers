Price and plan commitment: SnagRx vs Trimi

SnagRx advertises compounded semaglutide from $69/month and tirzepatide from $119/month on the 12 month plan; month to month rates are $99 and $149. Trimi advertises compounded semaglutide from $99/month on the annual plan and tirzepatide for $125/month on the annual plan. Both providers state the annual plan yields the lower rate and both keep the same price as dose increases.

Early verdict on pricing

SnagRx lists the lower annual-plan price. Trimi lists higher annual-plan prices but still uses flat pricing across titration.

Price stability and transparency

Both companies state flat pricing that does not rise as dose escalates. SnagRx repeatedly emphasizes a locked flat rate “for life” and names its four US 503A partner pharmacies and its clinician network OpenLoop Health. Trimi also states same-price-every-dose and lists named 503A pharmacies like VialsRx and GreenwichRx along with Arora Health clinical oversight.

Shipping speed and delivery

SnagRx advertises next-day UPS or FedEx shipping in temperature controlled packaging and states shipments arrive in 1 to 2 days. Trimi advertises free overnight, temperature-controlled shipping and free overnight delivery.

Pharmacies, clinicians and state coverage

SnagRx names its US 503A partner pharmacies and says medical care is delivered by OpenLoop Health covering 48 states; SnagRx also states LegitScript certification. Trimi states prescriptions are written by US licensed clinicians overseen by Arora Health and a medical director licensed in all 50 states, and lists named 503A pharmacies including VialsRx and GreenwichRx.

Guarantees, refunds and program extras

Trimi advertises a 6 month money back promise where you can request a refund if you do not lose weight after following your plan for six months, terms apply. SnagRx offers a full refund if a prescription is declined and advertises a 6 month weight-loss guarantee with terms that apply. Neither provider claims the compounded medications themselves are FDA approved; both include the regulatory note that compounded medications are not FDA approved.

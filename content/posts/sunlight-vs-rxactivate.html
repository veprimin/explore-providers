1. Pricing transparency and billing terms

Sunlight lists personalized GLP-1 plans starting at $88/mo on its landing page and emphasizes “Same price forever” and “no hidden fees” as part of its messaging. The source repeatedly states starting price of $88/mo and free shipping in 1-2 days. By contrast, RxActivate does not display any price, medication fee, membership fee, shipping charge, cancellation term or billing commitment before the contact-information gate. For users focused on immediate price visibility, Sunlight is the clear choice based on the provided evidence.

2. Eligibility flow and clinician involvement

Both providers require clinical review before treatment. Sunlight advertises a 100% online medical visit with prescription and telehealth included and notes users should confirm eligibility, medication, total cost, renewal terms and state availability before enrollment. RxActivate uses a two-minute eligibility questionnaire that requests goal, height, weight, target weight, timeline, medication history, sex assigned at birth, date of birth, and state for medical-safety screening. RxActivate then indicates treatment is available only when a licensed clinician determines it is medically appropriate. The difference is process gating. Sunlight presents pricing and claims telehealth and prescription inclusion up front. RxActivate delays pricing until after the eligibility and contact steps.

3. Medication options and delivery

Sunlight explicitly lists injections and oral options and advertises free and discreet two-day shipping on the landing page. RxActivate describes clinician-matched GLP-1 plans but reveals plan specifics after intake. Neither source supplies a complete list of medications or guarantees interchangeability. The regulatory notes on both sources caution that branded and compounded products are not interchangeable and medication-specific approval status must be confirmed.

4. Lead capture and user information before pricing

Sunlight allows users to see a starting price and shipping claims before submitting contact information. RxActivate displays likely eligibility but requires first name, last name, email and phone number before revealing any plan or price. The RxActivate source also states no payment is requested before the contact-information step. If you prefer to know cost and shipping terms before providing personal contact details, the evidence shows Sunlight provides those signals earlier.

5. Safety, regulatory notes, and state coverage

Both sources include a regulatory note that medication-specific approval status must be confirmed and that branded and compounded products are not interchangeable. RxActivate additionally confirms Alabama as serviceable in a synthetic test and explicitly lists state and medical-safety screening in its flow. Sunlight advises users to confirm eligibility, medication, total cost, renewal terms and state availability before enrollment.

How they compare overall

Sunlight vs RxActivate differs mainly in visible pricing, whether plan details and shipping claims appear before sign up, and the intake gating process. Sunlight presents a starting price of $88/mo, claims same price forever, and advertises free shipping in 1-2 days. RxActivate requires completion of the eligibility questionnaire and contact information to reveal pricing and plan details, while also emphasizing clinician matching and state safety screening.

Conclusion and who should choose which

Sunlight vs RxActivate is a choice between upfront pricing and gated clinician-matched plans. Choose Sunlight if you want landing-page pricing visibility, injections and oral options listed, and shipping claims such as free 1-2 day delivery. Choose RxActivate if you are comfortable completing a two-minute eligibility questionnaire and providing contact information to receive clinician-matched plan details and pricing that are revealed after intake. Both require clinician review and confirmation of medication-specific approval status and state availability.

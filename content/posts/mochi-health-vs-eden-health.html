Mochi Health vs Eden Health comes down to two different models: Mochi emphasizes live video visits and a membership-plus-medication structure starting from $99 with a 50% off first month promotion, while Eden Health offers a single monthly price for medications such as semaglutide at $99 and tirzepatide at $129 with no membership fees. Quick verdict: choose Mochi for real-time video doctor visits and membership-style ongoing support, or choose Eden for transparent single monthly pricing with no membership required.

Core difference 1: Visit model and clinician interaction

Mochi Health offers live video visits with a doctor bookable in about 5 minutes, presenting a synchronous consultation where a licensed physician evaluates your case in real time. Eden Health describes a clinician-guided program but captured evidence emphasizes monthly pricing and notes that treatment is available only when a licensed clinician determines it is medically appropriate. The comparison is direct: Mochi markets immediate live video access as a defining feature, while Eden centers on a guided program with clinician review subject to medical appropriateness.

Core difference 2: Pricing structure and transparency

Mochi uses a membership-plus-medication pricing model. Mochi advertises membership-plus-medication pricing starting from $99 per month, with a 50% off first month offer via code BESTWLHALFOFF and promotional first-month rates as low as $39 in some offers. Mochi’s ongoing cost varies by plan and medication and is described as less transparent than flat-rate competitors.

Eden Health offers single monthly pricing at every dose and advertises semaglutide at $99 and tirzepatide at $129 with no membership fees, a lifetime price guarantee, and free expedited shipping. Eden’s approach is presented as one clear monthly price that includes medication, care, and support. The data captured shows Eden’s prices and benefits as stated on the source evidence.

Core difference 3: Medication sourcing and regulatory notes

Mochi’s offering highlights a focus on compounded medications such as compounded semaglutide and compounded tirzepatide with starting prices provided in source evidence. Mochi explicitly notes that compounded medications are not FDA approved and are not reviewed by the FDA for safety, effectiveness or quality. Mochi also lists FDA-approved branded options and clarifies that prescriptions are provider discretion.

Eden’s captured evidence states monthly pricing for semaglutide and tirzepatide and warns that medication-specific approval status must be confirmed. Eden also notes that branded and compounded products are not interchangeable. Both providers include regulatory qualifiers in their source material, and the evidence shows Mochi emphasizes compounded options while Eden emphasizes a single monthly price with clinician review required for treatment.

How to decide

Use the direct differences above to decide. If a transparent single monthly price with no membership fees and explicit per-medication pricing is your priority, Eden Health matches those attributes according to the captured evidence.

Choose Mochi Health if you want live video doctor visits bookable in about 5 minutes, membership-plus-medication care, and 24/7 support.

Overview: Pallas Health vs FeelGood Meds

The comparison Pallas Health vs FeelGood Meds centers on four distinctive differences supported by the sources: clinician selection of brand versus compounded routes, starting and renewal pricing, how provider care and fees are packaged, and shipping and eligibility notes. Both offer compounded medications and state that compounded products are not FDA approved; beyond that the two services emphasize different benefits.

1. Clinician role and medication routes

Pallas Health emphasizes a US licensed clinician who reviews your health history and personalizes the plan, offering either FDA approved brand-name GLP-1s like Wegovy, Ozempic, Zepbound and Mounjaro, or compounded semaglutide and tirzepatide when a brand medication is not the right fit. FeelGood Meds advertises personalized GLP-1 treatments and offers compounded injections, compounded tablets and a branded option, with eligibility and medication subject to the live offer and a licensed clinician determining medical appropriateness.

2. Starting prices and renewal details

Pallas Health lists compounded semaglutide at $139 for the first month then $597 every 12 weeks, a $199 per month average, and compounded tirzepatide at $179 first month then $897 every 12 weeks, a $299 per month average. Pallas also lists brand-name cash pay prices for Wegovy, Zepbound, Ozempic and Mounjaro on the landing page. FeelGood Meds shows compounded injections starting at $149 per month, compounded tablets starting at $249 per month, and a branded option starting at $1,999 per month. Both sources state that compounded medications are not FDA approved.

3. What is included and membership or fee policy

Pallas Health advertises one all in plan price with provider care, check ins and messaging included and no membership fee. Pallas notes provider care is inside the plan price, with no membership or platform fee, and self serve portal features and a free companion app. FeelGood Meds states no membership fee and that everything needed is included, and highlights a Weight Loss Money Back Guarantee on its page. Neither page supplies full details of what ongoing care entails beyond these statements and both clarify eligibility is subject to clinician review.

4. Shipping, renewal cadence and other logistics

Pallas Health offers free expedited and discreet shipping on every plan and notes compounded plans renew every 12 weeks rather than monthly, so cancellation timing matters. Pallas does not manufacture or compound medication; US licensed compounding pharmacies prepare compounded medications and what you receive may differ in appearance from site images. FeelGood Meds advertises free shipping and no membership or hidden fees. Both providers state compounded medications are not FDA reviewed for safety, effectiveness or quality.

Final recommendation

Choose Pallas Health if you want a US licensed clinician to select between FDA approved brand pens and a per patient compounded preparation, if you value provider care included in a single plan price, or if you need clear 12 week renewal pricing for compounded plans. Choose FeelGood Meds if your priority is a lower advertised starting monthly price for compounded injections or tablets and free shipping, provided you confirm eligibility, renewal terms and state availability during the live offer.

Both pages note that compounded medications are not FDA approved and that eligibility is subject to clinician review. For specific, current pricing and availability confirm figures during intake with each provider.

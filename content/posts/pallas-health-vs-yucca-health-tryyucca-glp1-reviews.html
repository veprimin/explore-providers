Pallas Health vs Yucca Health compares a dual-path provider that offers FDA-approved pens and compounded preparations to a low-cost, heavily accessible compounded specialist. Early verdict: choose Pallas if you want clinician access to brand-name GLP-1s plus compounded options; choose Yucca if your priority is lower compounded monthly pricing and fast online approval.

How the two providers differ at a glance

This comparison focuses on the most distinctive supported differences: medication options, pricing structure, approval and shipping speed, payment and plan flexibility, and compounding and regulatory notes. The primary keyword Pallas Health vs Yucca Health appears here and is addressed throughout.

Medication choice and clinical routing

Pallas Health offers both FDA-approved Wegovy, Ozempic, Zepbound and Mounjaro and clinician-personalized compounded semaglutide or tirzepatide when brand medication is not the right fit. Yucca Health provides personalized compounded semaglutide and tirzepatide only, with no branded FDA-approved pens available.

Pricing and plan structure

Pallas advertises compounded semaglutide at $139 for the first month then $597 every 12 weeks, a $199 per month average, and compounded tirzepatide from $179 first month then $897 every 12 weeks, a $299 per month average. Pallas also lists cash-pay prices for brand-name pens. Yucca advertises compounded semaglutide from $146 per month and compounded tirzepatide from $258 per month, and offers 6-month plans that lower the effective monthly rate.

Approval, shipping, and state coverage

Pallas operates 100 percent online and is asynchronous in most states, with a video visit required in seven states plus Washington D.C. Pallas notes free expedited shipping. Yucca promotes an online intake with approvals typically within 24 hours, UPS 2-Day Air shipping, and licensed providers in all 50 states.

Payments, financing, and plan rules

Pallas is cash-pay only and does not bill insurance. Pallas notes FSA or HSA may require a Letter of Medical Necessity. Pallas prices its compounded plans by medication rather than by milligram, so titrating up does not raise the plan price. Yucca is also direct-pay only, does not accept insurance, but lists Buy Now Pay Later options including Klarna, Afterpay, and Affirm and a Lifetime Price Lock code that can freeze GLP-1 pricing for life when applied.

Compounding and regulatory notes

Both providers offer compounded medications and include the same regulatory caution: compounded medications are not FDA-approved and are not reviewed by the FDA for safety, effectiveness or quality. Pallas clarifies that compounded medications are prepared by US licensed compounding pharmacies and that Pallas does not manufacture or compound medication. Yucca states medications are filled at licensed U.S. compounding pharmacies and that compounded products use the same active ingredients but are not finished FDA-approved products.

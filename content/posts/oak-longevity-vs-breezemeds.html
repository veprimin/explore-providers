Key differences at a glance: Oak Longevity vs BreezeMeds

This comparison focuses on the most distinctive supported differences between Oak Longevity and BreezeMeds: pricing and price structure, product formats offered, subscription and billing terms, shipping and support features, and regulatory notes about compounded medications.

Pricing and price structure

Oak Longevity provides semaglutide starting at $133/month and tirzepatide starting at $199/month with an explicit one flat price for all dosages and no subscription required. BreezeMeds lists semaglutide injection starting at $199/month, tirzepatide injection starting at $299, and oral options displayed from $399, with a separate $169 figure shown without clear product association.

Product formats and availability

Oak Longevity offers compounded semaglutide and compounded tirzepatide as its GLP-1 medications. BreezeMeds displays injectable semaglutide and tirzepatide plus oral semaglutide and oral tirzepatide as options on its intake landing page. The specific pricing tied to each BreezeMeds product requires confirmation, since the landing evidence shows multiple starting prices.

Subscription, billing, and refunds

Oak Longevity states billing is monthly on a non recurring basis with no subscription, and three month prescriptions are billed upfront; it also states no refunds are available after physician approval and once medication has shipped. BreezeMeds did not show clear billing commitments or refund terms in the captured evidence and advises confirming fees and renewal terms before enrollment.

Shipping, support, and clinical review

Oak Longevity includes free shipping with tracking and free health coaching, and notes same day approval is available for qualifying patients. Oak also specifies clinical review is asynchronous and board certified physicians in your state review every case; a video call and insurance are not required. BreezeMeds emphasizes discreet home delivery and ongoing support but the captured evidence advises users to confirm state availability and eligibility.

Regulatory notes and suitability

Both providers note that treatment is available only when a licensed clinician determines it is medically appropriate. Oak Longevity explicitly warns that compounded medications are not FDA approved finished products and are not reviewed by FDA for safety, effectiveness or quality. The BreezeMeds evidence states medication specific approval status must be confirmed and that branded and compounded products are not interchangeable.

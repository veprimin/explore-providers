Distinctive differences to weigh in Trimi vs Strut Health

This comparison centers on five supported differences: medication format and specialization, pricing structure, shipping and fulfillment, clinician and medical oversight, and refund or program guarantees. Each section below compares Trimi and Strut Health directly using only supplied facts.

1) Medication format and clinical focus

Trimi: Compounded semaglutide and compounded tirzepatide are both offered and described as prescribed by US-licensed clinicians and shipped to your door. The content repeatedly references injectable compounded options and tirzepatide as well as semaglutide.

Strut Health: The platform is specialized in oral GLP-1 therapy. Strut focuses on oral semaglutide lozenges and notes clinical processes for sublingual lozenges and injection schedules where relevant, but its stated strength is oral-based program options and oral specialization.

2) Pricing structure and transparency

Trimi: Advertised pricing includes compounded semaglutide from $99/month on the annual plan and compounded tirzepatide at $125/month, with an explicit claim of same price at every dose and flat pricing that never rises as dose increases. Trimi also offers a 6-month money-back promise with terms applied when not approved by medical team and other refund language documented.

Strut Health: The site lists oral GLP-1 from $199/month as a headline and emphasizes no hidden fees and cancel anytime policies. Captured landing details also reference price points and free shipping messaging. Exact plan terms and any dose pricing policies beyond those listed are not supplied.

3) Shipping, pharmacies, and fulfillment

Trimi: Free overnight, temperature-controlled shipping is highlighted repeatedly, and the service notes that medications are compounded by state-licensed 503A pharmacies with named examples and certificates of analysis available on request.

Strut Health: The provider notes free shipping in its captured landing content and highlights straightforward fulfillment for oral semaglutide lozenges. Details about named pharmacies or temperature-controlled overnight delivery are not supplied.

4) Clinician oversight and program support

Trimi: Medical oversight is described as provided through Arora Health, a clinical network led by a board-certified physician licensed in all 50 states, Dr. Sean Arora, MD. Licensed U.S. providers are cited along with clinician review language including refunds if not approved by medical team.

Strut Health: The site emphasizes guided consultations and ongoing progress communication with a clinical team focused on oral medication management. Strut states compounded medication is prescribed based on medical necessity by a licensed physician and that oral specialization concentrates clinical attention on oral protocols.

5) Refunds, eligibility, and regulatory notes

Trimi: Documents a 6-month money-back promise and an automatic refund if not approved by the medical team; terms apply. It also explicitly states that compounded medications are not FDA-approved and are not reviewed by FDA for safety, effectiveness or quality.

Strut Health: States that compounded medications are not FDA-approved and that compounded semaglutide is prepared by U.S.-licensed compounding pharmacies pursuant to an individualized prescription. It also requires medical necessity for some patients, for example when commercially available GLP-1 options fail or cannot be used.

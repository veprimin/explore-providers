Quick verdict: altRx vs BetterMe Rx

The comparison altRx vs BetterMe Rx comes down to price structure, medication formats, shipping speed, and compounding status. Early verdict: altRx emphasizes a lower starting price (compounded semaglutide starting at $89/month and tirzepatide $149/month) and 100% online visits; BetterMe Rx emphasizes multiple formats (injectable and oral) with lower monthly-equivalent pricing only on longer prepaid plans (as low as $145/month on a 12‑month plan) and free 1–2 day shipping.

How their pricing and billing differ

Price is the most visible difference in the altRx vs BetterMe Rx comparison. altRx promotes a limited offer with compounded semaglutide starting at $89/month and compounded tirzepatide at $149/month, including multi‑month discounts in its FAQ. BetterMe Rx shows higher month-to-month figures but lower 12‑month equivalents: semaglutide injection as low as $145/month on a 12‑month plan, tirzepatide injection as low as $215/month on 12‑month, and distinct pricing for oral options.

Medication formats and clinical review

Another key contrast: BetterMe Rx explicitly offers both injectable and oral compounded GLP‑1 options (semaglutide and tirzepatide) and notes the needle-free daily tirzepatide tablet option. altRx focuses on compounded semaglutide and tirzepatide (described as GLP‑1 and GLP‑1/GIP) provided in a treatment kit. Both require a licensed provider review and state that treatment is available only when a clinician determines it is appropriate.

Delivery, supplies, and visit model

altRx highlights 100% online medical visits, free and discreet 2‑day delivery, and a complete treatment kit including vials, syringes, and prep pads. BetterMe Rx includes provider review, pharmacy compounding, supplies, and free 1–2 day shipping; it also notes that longer prepaid plans are billed according to the selected term. Both state no membership or hidden fees and claim cancel-anytime language, though BetterMe Rx’s lowest monthly rates require a 12‑month plan.

Compounding and regulatory note

Both providers sell compounded medications and include the same regulatory qualifier: compounded medications are not FDA‑approved and are not reviewed by FDA for safety, effectiveness, or quality. altRx specifically states prescriptions are filled by state‑licensed 503A compounding pharmacies in its network; BetterMe Rx indicates partnered U.S. pharmacies compound and ship prescriptions.

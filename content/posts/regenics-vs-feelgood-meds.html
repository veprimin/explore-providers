Quick verdict: Regenics vs FeelGood Meds

The primary difference between Regenics and FeelGood Meds is starting pricing and product breadth: Regenics advertises compounded semaglutide at $139 for the first month and compounded tirzepatide at $189 for the first month, while FeelGood Meds lists compounded injections starting at $149 and compounded tablets from $249. Both offer clinician review and both note that compounded medications are not FDA-approved and are not reviewed by FDA for safety, effectiveness or quality. Treatment is available only when a licensed clinician determines it is medically appropriate.

Pricing and renewal differences

FeelGood Meds shows compounded injections starting at $149 and compounded tablets from $249, and also lists a branded option shown from $1,999/mo. FeelGood Meds notes pricing and eligibility are subject to the live offer. Neither provider requires insurance to start.

Conclusion: Regenics vs FeelGood Meds

Regenics tends to lead on promotional entry pricing and offers a nurse-practitioner-led model plus a Sermorelin peptide option, with renewals listed higher in the FAQ. FeelGood Meds lists slightly higher compounded injection entry pricing and more format choices including tablets and a branded option, with pricing and eligibility subject to the live offer. Both require clinician approval and both note that compounded medications are not FDA-approved and are not reviewed by FDA for safety, effectiveness or quality. Regenics vs FeelGood Meds is therefore primarily a choice between lower promotional starting prices plus a peptide add-on at Regenics and broader format and branded options at FeelGood Meds.

Choose Regenics if you want lower first-month compounded GLP-1 promotional pricing and a nurse-practitioner-led telehealth model with a Sermorelin peptide option.

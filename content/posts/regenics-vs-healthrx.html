Regenics vs HealthRx: both offer clinician-reviewed access to compounded semaglutide and tirzepatide, but they differ in starting price, shipping, clinician model, and plan structure. Early verdict: choose Regenics if you want nurse-practitioner-led telehealth with a Sermorelin add-on and a simple first-month promo; choose HealthRx if you prioritize a lower advertised prepaid monthly rate and overnight cold chain shipping.

Explore Regenics pricing and services

How they match up up front

Both providers deliver compounded semaglutide or tirzepatide only when a licensed clinician determines treatment is medically appropriate, and both note that compounded medications are not FDA-approved as finished products. From there, practical differences are clear: Regenics markets promotional first-month pricing and nurse-practitioner-led care with a Sermorelin option, while HealthRx emphasizes lower prepaid monthly pricing, clinician discretion over medication choice, and overnight cold shipping from a licensed 503A pharmacy.

Pricing and plan structure

Regenics: semaglutide starts at $139 first month and renews at $199/month; tirzepatide starts at $189 first month and renews at $279/month. Pricing beyond the first month requires checking the fine print. HealthRx: semaglutide is listed from $99/month on a 12-month prepaid semaglutide plan ($1,163 today; $1,188 at renewal), with tirzepatide plans starting at $179/month. HealthRx notes the lowest advertised monthly price requires a longer prepaid plan and that plan price does not change when your dose does.

Shipping, pharmacy and medication handling

Regenics reports free shipping with medication compounded in the USA and uses a LegitScript certified pharmacy network. HealthRx specifies overnight cold shipping with cold chain, tracked, free on every plan, and that compounded medication is prepared in a licensed 503A pharmacy cleanroom. Both reiterate that compounded medications are not FDA-approved and not reviewed by FDA for safety, effectiveness or quality.

Clinical oversight and prescription decisions

Regenics describes nurse-practitioner-led GLP-1 care where licensed nurse practitioners review intake before anything is prescribed. HealthRx states clinicians review your file and decide whether semaglutide or tirzepatide is the right fit, or whether neither is; it emphasizes you choose the plan, not the specific medication. Both require a licensed clinician to determine medical appropriateness.

Price stability, billing and extras

HealthRx highlights that there is no consultation fee, no shipping charge, and your plan price does not change when your dose does; it also warns that the lowest monthly price requires a longer prepaid commitment. Regenics notes FSA/HSA eligibility, no insurance required to start treatment, and that promotional pricing applies to the first month only and renewals increase to listed renewal rates.

Regenics positions itself as a straightforward cash-pay entry with a secondary peptide line, Sermorelin, offered as an add-on. HealthRx frames its offer around plan pricing mechanics: what you choose is a plan that arrives with either semaglutide or tirzepatide as decided by a clinician, with an emphasis on no extra consultation or shipping fees and stable plan pricing when dose changes occur.

Bottom line and how to choose

Regenics vs HealthRx hinges on priorities: Regenics is best for patients seeking nurse-practitioner-led telehealth, a Sermorelin peptide option, and a simple promotional first month priced at $139 for semaglutide or $189 for tirzepatide. HealthRx is best for people who confirm eligibility and want lower advertised prepaid monthly pricing starting at $99 for semaglutide, overnight cold chain shipping from a 503A pharmacy, and a plan model where dose changes do not change plan price.

Choose Regenics if you want nurse-practitioner-led telehealth, a Sermorelin add-on, and a first-month promotional price subject to renewal increases.

Choose HealthRx if you prefer a lower advertised prepaid monthly rate, overnight cold shipping from a 503A pharmacy, and plan pricing that does not change when your dose does.

Regenics vs HealthRx: both require clinician review, both use compounded medication that is not FDA-approved as a finished product, and both recommend confirming eligibility, pricing and terms before enrolling.

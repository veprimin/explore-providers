FDA-approved brand-name medication versus compounded formulations

One of the clearest differences in this Hers vs Sprout Health pairing is whether medication is a brand-name FDA-approved finished product or a compounded product. Hers explicitly offers FDA-approved Wegovy, described as a brand-name semaglutide product and not compounded. Sprout Health prescribes compounded semaglutide and its captured content notes compounded medications, when offered, are not FDA-approved and are not reviewed by FDA for safety, effectiveness or quality.

Onboarding speed and care model

Hers emphasizes a personalized care team with ongoing dose check-ins, indicating more hands-on follow up. Sprout Health promotes a lightning-quick approval process and a fast, no-friction entry designed for patients who want minimal onboarding time. The comparison shows Hers focuses on ongoing clinician-managed dose checks while Sprout Health focuses on rapid prescription review and delivery.

Outcome framing and claims

Sprout Health frames outcomes around a 10 to 26 percent body weight reduction over 12 months and presents this as expected ranges based on GLP-1 clinical literature, while noting individual results vary and it does not offer a formal outcome guarantee. Hers highlights availability of FDA-approved options and captured landing page examples showing weight changes in sample timelines, but the supplied evidence does not present a formal guaranteed outcome from Hers.

Availability, regulatory notes, and suitability

Hers notes not all treatments are available in all 50 states and that prescriptions are subject to clinician review; it also emphasizes that Ozempic and Mounjaro are FDA-approved for type 2 diabetes and available only to patients who meet clinical eligibility criteria. Sprout Health’s captured content also states treatment is available only when a licensed clinician determines it is medically appropriate and repeats the regulatory caution that compounded medications are not FDA-approved. Both providers operate on a prescription after clinician review and direct-pay models in the captured evidence.

How Sesame Care vs Pallas Health differ at a glance

This comparison focuses on five supported differences: pricing structure, medication options and status, insurance and billing, care model and scheduling, and shipping and plan renewal rules. The primary keyword Sesame Care vs Pallas Health appears here to keep search intent clear.

1. Pricing structure and subscription details

Sesame Care lists ongoing video visits, dedicated provider care, labs and messaging for as low as $59/mo based on a 12-month Success by Sesame commitment. Cash-pay GLP-1 medications are listed as low as $149/mo for semaglutide and $299 for certain tirzepatide doses, with price-with-insurance as low as $25 for some branded options. Medication costs are not included in the subscription price and will vary based on insurance and pharmacy.

Pallas Health presents an all-in plan price that includes provider care and messaging with no membership fee. Compounded semaglutide is $139 for the first month, then $597 every 12 weeks, a $199/mo average. Compounded tirzepatide is $179 first month, then $897 every 12 weeks, a $299/mo average. Brand-name cash-pay prices are listed separately for Wegovy, Zepbound, Ozempic and Mounjaro.

2. Medication options and regulatory notes

Sesame Care offers oral GLP-1s and lists both semaglutide and tirzepatide pricing lines including brand references. It notes Wegovy pill as FDA-approved for adults with obesity or overweight with weight-related problems and includes a regulatory note that medication-specific approval status must be confirmed and that branded and compounded products are not interchangeable.

Pallas Health provides access to FDA-approved brand-name GLP-1s and clinician-personalized compounded semaglutide or tirzepatide prepared in the USA when a brand medication is not the right fit. Pallas explicitly states that compounded medications are not FDA-approved and are not reviewed by FDA for safety, effectiveness or quality, and that Pallas does not manufacture or compound medication.

3. Insurance, billing and refund policies

Sesame Care states that insurance is accepted for weight loss medications and shows price-with-insurance options as low as $25 for some branded medications. Medication costs are otherwise cash pay and will vary based on insurance status.

Pallas Health is cash-pay only and does not bill insurance. Pallas notes FSA/HSA eligibility may require a Letter of Medical Necessity and that pricing changes should be confirmed at checkout. Pallas also notes a full refund is available if a clinician decides treatment is not right for you.

4. Care model, clinician selection and visit format

Sesame Care operates as a telehealth marketplace where patients can browse and select providers based on specialty, availability and pricing, emphasizing large clinician network, flexible scheduling and nationwide access in many states. Experience can vary by clinician.

Pallas Health uses a clinician review to personalize plans and offers both brand and compounded routes from one intake. Pallas is 100% online and asynchronous in most states, with a video visit required in seven states plus Washington, D.C. Provider care is included inside the plan price.

5. Shipping, renewal cadence and other logistics

Pallas highlights free expedited and discreet shipping on every plan, compounded in the USA, and notes compounded plans renew every 12 weeks rather than monthly, so cancellation timing matters. Pallas also lists care-team hours and limits such as no emergency services.

Sesame Care notes medication costs vary with insurance and pharmacy; the source emphasizes flexible appointments and nationwide access in many states but does not supply a universal shipping or renewal cadence in the captured evidence.

Conclusion: Sesame Care vs Pallas Health

Both platforms present online weight-loss care but serve different priorities. Sesame Care is best for people who want broad telehealth choice, flexible scheduling and the ability to pick their clinician, with subscriptions starting at $59/mo and medication costs varying by insurance. Pallas Health is best for people who want a clinician to choose between brand-name pens and a US-compounded preparation, with compounded semaglutide $139 first month then $199/mo avg and free expedited shipping, and with cash-pay only plans that renew every 12 weeks.

Choose Sesame Care if you want marketplace choice, flexible appointments and insurance options. Choose Pallas Health if you want clinician-personalized access to brand or compounded GLP-1s and an all-in plan price that includes provider care.

Pallas Health vs Hers: Quick verdict

Early verdict: Choose Pallas Health if you want clinician access to either brand-name GLP-1s or a lower-cost compounded semaglutide route with no membership fee; choose Hers if you require an FDA-approved brand-name Wegovy® product and accept a membership requirement and branded pricing.

Key differences at a glance

The three most distinctive differences supported by the sources are composition and regulatory status of medication, pricing and billing structure, and enrollment requirements and care model. Below we compare these directly.

1. Medication type and regulatory notes

Pallas Health: Offers FDA-approved Wegovy®, Ozempic®, Zepbound® and Mounjaro® when appropriate, and clinician-personalized compounded semaglutide or tirzepatide prepared in the USA. The source notes that compounded medications are not FDA-approved and are not reviewed by FDA for safety, effectiveness or quality.

Hers: Offers FDA-approved Wegovy® (brand-name semaglutide) and does not offer compounded medications. The source emphasizes name-brand medication and that Wegovy® and Zepbound® are FDA-approved for weight loss. Both providers include the regulatory note that compounded medications are not FDA-approved when mentioned.

2. Price structure and sample pricing

Pallas Health: Lists compounded semaglutide at $139 for the first month, then $597 every 12 weeks, a $199/month average; compounded tirzepatide is $179 first month, then $897 every 12 weeks ($299/mo average). Pallas also lists brand-name cash-pay prices for Wegovy® $1,349/mo and others as examples. Provider care is included in the plan price with no membership fee.

Hers: Lists Wegovy® from $149/mo and injectable from $199/mo in their captured evidence. Hers requires an active membership; pricing notes say medication price includes medication only if prescribed and that an active Hers Weight Loss Membership is required (first month $39, auto-renews at $149/month thereafter). Membership and medication are billed separately and medication is not available without an active membership.

3. Enrollment, care access and logistics

Pallas Health: 100% online, asynchronous in most states (source: 43 of 50 need no video visit) with provider care, check-ins and messaging included in the plan price. Shipping is free and discreet, compounded in the USA. Eligibility and final plan selection are determined by a US-licensed clinician; Pallas does not manufacture or compound medications.

Hers: Provides a personalized care team with ongoing dose check-ins but requires membership for medication access. The source notes treatment is available only when a licensed clinician determines it is medically appropriate and that availability is not in all 50 states. The captured evidence also states medication is not available without an active membership.

4. Billing, cancellation and other practical terms

Pallas Health: Cash-pay only; does not bill insurance. Plans renew every 12 weeks for compounded options, so cancellation timing matters. Pallas says care-team hours are weekdays 9 AM–5 PM ET and no emergency services are offered.

Hers: Also states no insurance required but explicitly requires membership; membership auto-renews at $149/month after an initial month fee. The source cautions that a prescription is not guaranteed and that total cost includes both membership and medication fees.

How each provider frames pros and cons

Pallas emphasizes flexibility between brand and compounded options, one all-in plan price with provider care included, and that compounded plans are priced by medication so titration does not change the plan price. Cons listed include that compounded drugs are not FDA-approved, cash-pay only and some states require a video visit.

Hers emphasizes FDA-approved brand-name Wegovy®, a personalized care team and ongoing dose check-ins, and notes higher starting price than many compounded competitors and that no compounded alternative is offered. The source also highlights membership requirements and potential state availability limits.

Conclusion and final recommendation

In a Pallas Health vs Hers comparison, Pallas Health is better suited to people who want clinician flexibility to choose between FDA brand-name pens and lower-cost compounded options with provider care included and no membership fee. Hers is better suited to people who want an FDA-approved brand-name Wegovy® product and accept that medication access requires an active membership and separate medication billing.

Choose Pallas Health if you want clinician access to both brand and compounded GLP-1 options, free discreet shipping, an all-in plan price that includes provider care and lower-cost compounded semaglutide starting at $139 for the first month.

Choose Hers if you require FDA-approved Wegovy® as a brand-name product and understand that medication access requires an active Hers Weight Loss Membership and that membership and medication are billed separately.

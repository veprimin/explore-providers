Trimi vs Sprout Health answers the core decision: choose lower flat monthly pricing and overnight shipping with Trimi, or choose Sprout Health for faster approval and a $50 first-month discount. Early verdict: if ongoing price and flat dosing matter, Trimi; if rapid start and an initial discount matter more, Sprout Health.

How they differ at a glance

This comparison focuses on the most distinctive differences supported by the sources: ongoing price and pricing model, approval speed and onboarding friction, initial discount and first-month cost, compounded product status and regulatory notes, and shipping or membership features. Both providers prescribe GLP-1 therapies online, but their value propositions and cost structures are different.

Price and pricing model

Trimi: compounded semaglutide from $99/month on the annual plan and compounded tirzepatide at $125/month, with the company emphasizing flat pricing that never rises as dose increases. Trimi also advertises a 6-month money-back promise for weight loss under specified terms. Sprout Health: a $50 off first month promotion bringing the first month to $199, with an ongoing rate of $249/month after the discount. Sprout’s marketing notes the ongoing $249/month is higher than several competitors.

Approval speed and onboarding

Sprout Health emphasizes a lightning-quick approval process and a low-friction, fully online experience. The source frames Sprout as well-suited for patients who want a fast, low-friction entry to GLP-1 therapy. Trimi describes clinician review through a national clinical network led by a medical director and offers automatic refunds if not approved by their medical team. The documentation for Trimi emphasizes clinician review via licensed U.S. providers and named compounding pharmacies.

Initial offers, ongoing cost tradeoffs

Sprout Health’s $50 off the first month reduces the first payment to $199, but the source explicitly states the price rises to $249/month after the discounted month. Trimi’s promotional pricing cites semaglutide from $99/month on annual plans and the claim of same price at every dose. The comparison therefore centers on lower ongoing cost and flat-dose pricing with Trimi versus a meaningful first-month discount and faster approval with Sprout.

Compounded products and regulatory notes

Both providers involve compounded medications in some form. Trimi repeatedly notes that compounded medications are prepared per individual prescription and are not themselves FDA-approved as drugs. Sprout Health’s source also notes that compounded medications, when offered, are not FDA-approved and are not reviewed by FDA for safety, effectiveness or quality. Trimi lists state-licensed 503A pharmacies and says a certificate of analysis is available on request. Sprout frames outcomes using GLP-1 clinical literature and does not offer a formal outcome guarantee.

Shipping, membership, and guarantees

Trimi advertises free overnight, temperature-controlled shipping and states one flat membership covers medication, clinician visits, ongoing check-ins, and free shipping. Trimi also advertises a 6-month money-back promise subject to terms. Sprout’s captured page did not verify shipping or membership details but did emphasize fast prescription review and home delivery following approval.

Which to choose: Trimi vs Sprout Health

Choose Trimi if you prioritize lower ongoing cost, flat pricing across dose changes, free overnight shipping, and a membership that bundles clinician visits and check-ins. Trimi also documents named compounding pharmacies and highlights a 6-month money-back promise subject to terms.

Start Sprout Health fast enrollment

Choose Sprout Health if you prioritize fastest possible approval and a quick start, and if the $50 first-month discount matters more than a lower ongoing monthly rate. Sprout is positioned for patients who want minimal onboarding friction and clear outcome framing, though the source notes Sprout does not offer a formal outcome guarantee. Both providers require clinician review and may offer compounded medication options that are not FDA-approved and are subject to clinician determinations.

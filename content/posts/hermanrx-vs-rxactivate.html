HermanRx vs RxActivate: if you need fast, all-in pricing or a gated eligibility workflow, these two services differ sharply. Early verdict: HermanRx publishes all-inclusive monthly prices and estimated timelines; RxActivate requires completing an eligibility flow before any price or plan is revealed.

How the comparison is organized

This comparison focuses on the most distinctive differences supported by the sources: price transparency and billing model, intake and eligibility gating, timeline and delivery estimates, product and regulatory disclosures, and what each platform explicitly says about availability and scope.

Price transparency and billing model

HermanRx lists clear monthly prices for its compounded GLP-1 offerings: semaglutide + B12 at $249/month and tirzepatide + B12 at $349/month, described as all-inclusive with no membership or consultation fee. In contrast, RxActivate does not display price or billing terms before a user completes the medical eligibility questionnaire and submits contact details. That makes HermanRx the transparent option for upfront cost comparison, while RxActivate requires personal intake to reveal cost.

Intake workflow and eligibility gating

HermanRx documents a short intake and triage timeline: about a five-minute assessment, provider review in ~24 hours, and delivery in ~72 hours for some orders, with a broader FAQ window of 3 to 7 business days depending on treatment. RxActivate emphasizes a two-minute eligibility questionnaire that collects goal, height, weight, target weight, medication history, sex assigned at birth, date of birth, state and safety screening, and then requires first name, last name, email and phone number before showing any plan or price. RxActivate therefore gates pricing and plan details behind the eligibility flow; HermanRx publishes prices up front.

Timeline, shipping and support

HermanRx states free discreet shipping to all 50 states, with a published fast timeline for some orders: ~24 hour provider review and ~72 hour delivery when prescribed, while noting a general 3 to 7 business day window depending on treatment and pharmacy processing. RxActivate confirms state availability in testing for Alabama but does not publish shipping, billing, cancellation or renewal terms before lead capture.

Product offerings and regulatory notes

Both sources note that treatment is available only when a licensed clinician determines it is medically appropriate. HermanRx explicitly offers compounded semaglutide + B12 and compounded tirzepatide + B12 at the stated monthly prices and states that compounded medications are not FDA approved and are not reviewed by the FDA for safety, effectiveness or quality. RxActivate flags that medication-specific approval status must be confirmed and that branded and compounded products are not interchangeable, and it does not display medication details before the contact gate.

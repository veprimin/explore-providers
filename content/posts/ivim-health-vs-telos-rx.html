Key differences at a glance for IVIM Health vs Telos Rx

This comparison uses only captured provider facts. The most distinctive differences are: third-party ratings and program claims, published pricing and membership details, compounded medications and regulatory notes, and how each frames clinician review and availability.

1) Third-party ratings and program claims

IVIM Health: The source documents IVIM’s dual 4.9-star rating on Google and Trustpilot, described as an unusually strong satisfaction signal. IVIM also promotes a proprietary GLP-1 ID protocol that the company says can accelerate weight loss outcomes by up to 40% compared to standard GLP-1 protocols; the source notes this is IVIM’s protocol claim and not an independent clinical trial.

Telos Rx: The captured Telos information does not include third-party rating data. Telos frames its offer around clinician-reviewed prescriptions and molecule selection between semaglutide and tirzepatide but does not present a proprietary outcome claim in the captured evidence.

2) Pricing and membership structure

IVIM Health: Documented pricing includes semaglutide (compounded) at $75/mo and tirzepatide (compounded) at $133/mo on the captured landing-page evidence, plus a quoted $74.99/mo program fee in some materials and FAQ language stating IVIM starts at $99 for the first month with $174/month ongoing; new patients can use code GIFT75 for a free month membership and a no-obligation consultation (a $75 value).

Telos Rx: Captured Telos language emphasizes semaglutide “as low as $99 per month” and tirzepatide “as low as $139 per month” if a licensed provider approves you. Telos states “Pay $0 if not approved” and repeatedly notes prices are “as low as” figures and that your price depends on your plan. Both providers warn that prescriptions require clinician approval.

3) Compounded medications and regulatory notes

Both IVIM Health and Telos Rx offer compounded semaglutide and tirzepatide. The captured evidence for both providers explicitly states these are compounded medications and are not FDA-approved and have not been reviewed by the FDA for safety, effectiveness or quality. Both say prescriptions are subject to clinician review.

4) Clinician review, availability, and cancellation

IVIM: The source notes RX required after clinical evaluation and that compounded medications are prescribed only after consultation with an IVIM provider. IVIM materials also indicate not available in all states and that medications are prepared by independent, licensed compounding pharmacies.

Telos: The captured Telos content states treatment is available only when a licensed clinician determines it is medically appropriate, that compounded medications are prescription-only, and that you can cancel anytime with no penalties according to the landing-page copy.

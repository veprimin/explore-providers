How the two platforms differ at a glance

This comparison centers on 1) pricing and promotional offers, 2) shipping and fulfillment timing, 3) care model and support, and 4) regulatory and product type. Each point highlights direct contrasts between Start Willow and Measured using only the supplied facts.

1. Pricing and promotional structure

Start Willow lists pricing “From $249 per Month” with a note that the first month is $249 and ongoing is $299/month. Measured advertises promotional compounded semaglutide from $89/mo and compounded tirzepatide from $189/mo, while noting promotional pricing may change. These are advertised starting figures and Measured explicitly flags promotional pricing as subject to change.

2. Shipping speed and delivery commitments

Start Willow claims delivery in 3 days or less and mentions same day prescriptions with free 2-Day shipping in landing content. Measured promotes free 2-day shipping on every order and same-day prescription decisions. Both platforms advertise fast shipping, but Start Willow emphasizes a “3 days or less” guarantee while Measured consistently advertises free 2-day shipping.

3. Care model, messaging, and ancillary support

Measured pairs compounded semaglutide and tirzepatide with unlimited messaging, 24/7 Care Team access, and dietitian support, and promotes personalized diet and exercise support. Start Willow describes a personalized treatment plan with a Willow doctor, clinician review, and support from a Willow Care Team, emphasizing clinician-prescribed compounded semaglutide created in collaboration with your Willow doctor. Measured highlights unlimited messaging and dietitian support as distinguishing features.

4. Product type and regulatory notes

Both providers offer compounded GLP-1 medications rather than FDA-approved branded finished products. The supplied evidence states that compounded medications are not FDA-approved and are not reviewed by FDA for safety, effectiveness or quality. Start Willow explicitly notes its semaglutide is compounded and not FDA-approved and that compounded meds are prepared by state-licensed compounding pharmacies meeting USP compounding standards. Measured similarly states compounded medications are not FDA-approved and that prescriptions are issued at provider discretion.

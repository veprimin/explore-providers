Bodybuilding Health+ vs CoreAgeRx: headline differences

This comparison focuses on the most distinctive supported differences: pricing structure, product scope, compounded status and regulatory notes, clinician review and state availability, and membership or billing terms. Both platforms require a licensed clinician to determine medical appropriateness for treatment.

1) Pricing structure and advertised starting prices

Bodybuilding Health+ lists compounded semaglutide starting at $179 per month and tirzepatide starting at $209 per month, with multi-month bundles that reduce effective monthly cost. CoreAgeRx advertises a flat monthly pricing approach with semaglutide at $79 per month and tirzepatide at $129 per month and emphasizes “Same Price. Every Month.” CoreAgeRx warns other programs may increase monthly bills over time.

2) Program scope and services offered

Bodybuilding Health+ is presented as a broad telehealth platform covering weight management, longevity and sexual performance. Its features include GLP-1s, NAD+, sermorelin and ED options, with bundles from one to 12 months. CoreAgeRx messaging centers on clinician-reviewed online weight management with GLP-1s, emphasizing pricing consistency and short online visits.

3) Compounded medications and regulatory notes

Bodybuilding Health+ explicitly lists compounded semaglutide and compounded tirzepatide and states compounded medications, when offered, are not FDA approved and are not reviewed by FDA for safety, effectiveness or quality. CoreAgeRx materials note that medication-specific approval status must be confirmed and that branded and compounded products are not interchangeable.

4) Billing, membership and cancellation

Bodybuilding Health+ repeatedly states there is no monthly membership, no hidden fees, and you can cancel anytime. CoreAgeRx promotes no membership fee and no price hikes, stressing “no hidden fees” and a stable monthly charge regardless of treatment month. Both emphasize clinician review and that treatment is available only when a licensed clinician determines it is medically appropriate.

Directmeds vs Sprout Health: 4 key differences to weigh

This comparison focuses on the most distinctive supported differences: pricing and promotions, approval speed and onboarding, medication format and shipping, and membership/cancellation terms. The primary keyword Directmeds vs Sprout Health appears here and again near the conclusion.

1. Pricing and promotional structure

Directmeds advertises a promotional starting price of $147 and lists standard FAQ pricing of $297/month for semaglutide and $399/month for tirzepatide. The provider notes a Summer promotion that saves $150 and also references a 50% first-month discount in some descriptions. Sprout Health offers $50 off the first month, bringing the initial cost to $199 with ongoing pricing stated at $249/month after the discounted first month. The source notes Sprout Health’s ongoing $249 rate is higher than several competitors.

2. Approval speed and onboarding experience

Sprout Health positions itself on speed: its marketing describes “lightning-quick approval” and a fast, low-friction online intake and prescription review. This makes Sprout Health a fit for patients seeking rapid entry. Directmeds requires a licensed clinician to determine medical appropriateness and conducts clinician review and telehealth visits, which the source describes as clinician-reviewed but does not characterize as lightning-quick.

3. Medication format and shipping

Directmeds supplies compounded semaglutide and tirzepatide in injectable and sublingual formats, with medication, clinician review, telehealth visits, supplies and free next-day UPS/FedEx shipping included and temperature-controlled delivery advertised. Sprout Health prescribes compounded semaglutide and ships medication to patients after approval; the captured Sprout evidence does not include verified shipping timeframes on the landing page.

Directmeds explicitly states no separate monthly membership fee, no hidden fees, and month-to-month cancellation for standard customers; some subscribe-and-save plans require a stated term.

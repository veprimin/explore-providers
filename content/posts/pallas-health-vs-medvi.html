2. Pricing structure and renewal cadence

Pallas advertises compounded semaglutide at $139 for the first month, then $597 every 12 weeks, which equates to a $199 per month average. Compounded tirzepatide starts at $179 first month, then $897 every 12 weeks ($299/mo avg). Pallas also lists cash-pay pricing for branded options. MedVi promotes a winter price of $179 for the first month including fast, free shipping, but notes the ongoing standalone month is $299 unless a multimonth plan is chosen. Preserve the promotional qualifier: MedVi’s $179 is a first-month promo and refills are locked in at the full price specified at checkout.

3. Support model and access to clinicians

MedVi highlights 24/7 support from certified medical professionals and a process that requires no clinic visits. Pallas includes provider care, ongoing check-ins and care-team messaging in its all-in plan price, but care-team hours are weekdays 9 AM to 5 PM ET and Pallas notes no emergency services. Both state that a US-licensed clinician determines eligibility and that clinicians may decline treatment when it is not right for a patient.

4. Shipping, membership, and practical details

Both providers advertise free or fast free shipping. Pallas says free expedited and discreet shipping on every plan and emphasizes no membership fee; MedVi says no membership or hidden fees for the promoted program but references membership options on some pages. Both platforms operate as cash-pay and do not bill insurance, and both recommend discussing FSA/HSA or Letter of Medical Necessity where applicable. Pallas notes compounded plans renew every 12 weeks which affects cancellation timing; MedVi documents multimonth plan options to lower per-month costs.

5. Safety and regulatory notes

Both sources explicitly state that compounded medications are not FDA-approved and are not reviewed by FDA for safety, effectiveness or quality. Pallas clarifies it does not manufacture or compound medications; compounding is done by US-licensed compounding pharmacies and the medication received may differ in appearance from site images. MedVi likewise states it does not produce compounded medications and that they may look different than portrayed. Both urge clinical discussion with providers about risks and benefits.

Conclusion: Pallas Health vs MedVi

In short, Pallas Health is designed for patients who want a clinician to choose between brand-name pens and compounded preparations and who prefer an all-in plan price with clinician care included. MedVi is positioned for people seeking a simple online intake, a promotional first-month price on compounded semaglutide, and round-the-clock certified support.

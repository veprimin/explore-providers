Quick verdict: Pallas Health and HermanRx both sell clinician-reviewed GLP-1 options, but they differ most on pricing structure, brand versus bundled compounded offerings, and service timelines. Pallas emphasizes clinician choice between FDA-approved pens and compounded preparations with a low first-month compounded price; HermanRx offers single all-inclusive monthly pricing with faster published review and delivery timelines.

Explore Pallas Health plans and pricing

How these services overlap and differ

Pallas Health and HermanRx both operate as cash-pay, telehealth options offering compounded semaglutide and tirzepatide when a clinician deems them appropriate. Both providers state that compounded medications are not FDA-approved. From that common ground, three to five clear differences emerge and determine which may suit you better.

Top differences: price structure, medication options, and timelines

1) Pricing model and starting cost

Pallas Health lists compounded semaglutide at $139 for the first month, then $597 every 12 weeks (a $199/month average). Compounded tirzepatide starts at $179 first month, then $897 every 12 weeks ($299/month average). Pallas also publishes cash-pay brand-name monthly prices for Wegovy, Ozempic, Zepbound and Mounjaro by pen.

See Pallas Health compounded and brand pricing

HermanRx presents genuinely all-inclusive monthly pricing: compounded semaglutide + B12 at $249/month and compounded tirzepatide + B12 at $349/month. HermanRx emphasizes no membership or separate consultation fees; its listed monthly price covers provider review, medication, support and shipping.

2) Brand-name options versus bundled compounded products

Pallas Health explicitly offers both FDA-approved brand-name GLP-1s (Wegovy, Ozempic, Zepbound, Mounjaro) and clinician-personalized compounded semaglutide or tirzepatide when a brand medication is not the right fit. HermanRx focuses on compounded semaglutide and tirzepatide combined with B12 as its GLP-1 offering, while noting availability of both FDA-approved and compounded medications depending on the clinician recommendation.

3) Service timelines and geographic notes

HermanRx publishes a fast timeline: roughly a 5-minute assessment, about 24-hour provider review and roughly 72-hour delivery when prescribed, and states availability in all 50 states. Pallas Health is 100% online and asynchronous in most states, with video visits required in seven states plus Washington, D.C.; it notes 43 of 50 states need no video visit. Pallas highlights free expedited shipping but does not publish the same 24/72 timeline.

4) Care model details and support hours

Pallas includes clinician review, ongoing check-ins and care-team messaging in its plan price and notes care-team hours are weekdays 9 AM–5 PM ET with no emergency services. HermanRx similarly includes provider review and ongoing support in its all-inclusive price and advertises 24/7 patient support and a secure portal.

Other practical differences to weigh

Pallas prices compounded plans by medication rather than by milligram, so titration does not raise the plan price; plans renew every 12 weeks, which affects cancellation timing. Pallas is cash-pay only and does not bill insurance. HermanRx emphasizes a single monthly bill that includes B12 and no extra shipping or consultation fees, and it notes plain unmarked packaging and a published general FAQ window of 3–7 business days for some orders.

Verdict and who should pick which

If your priority is a low introductory price for a compounded semaglutide plan or access to both brand-name pens and compounded preparations chosen by a clinician, Pallas Health fits that description with a $139 first-month compounded semaglutide option and published brand prices. If you prefer a single all-inclusive monthly bill that bundles B12, emphasizes rapid provider review and delivery timelines, and 24/7 support across all 50 states, HermanRx provides that model with $249/month for semaglutide + B12.

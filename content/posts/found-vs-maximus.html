How these programs differ at a glance

The comparison below focuses on the most distinctive differences between Found and Maximus: clinical approach and personalization, pricing and insurance handling, medication options and formulation, access model and telemedicine workflow, and geographic or availability notes. Both offer compounded medications, which are not FDA-approved and are not reviewed by FDA for safety, effectiveness or quality.

Clinical approach and personalization

Found positions itself as a biology-first program that conducts a medical evaluation and builds a personalized program around metabolic biology, hormones, blood sugar regulation, sleep, and behavior. Maximus presents a straightforward GLP-1 telemedicine model focused on delivering compounded semaglutide prescribed by licensed doctors with no in-person visits. These are contrasting approaches: Found emphasizes holistic personalization; Maximus emphasizes simplicity and direct access to semaglutide.

Pricing and insurance handling

Found advertises getting started for as low as $39, notes most insurance is now accepted, and says Found handles insurance paperwork including a Medicare GLP-1 Bridge Program offering for eligible Part D members. Maximus lists a low flat rate at $132.99/month and operates on a direct-pay model that does not bill insurance. If insurance compatibility matters, Found explicitly accepts most insurance; if you want a predictable direct-pay price, Maximus lists $132.99/month.

Medication options and formulation

Found offers branded GLP-1s and compounded options including tirzepatide and semaglutide and states compounded GLP-1s are not FDA-approved and may not be available in all states. Found also notes certain medications are FDA-approved for conditions other than weight and disclaims affiliation with brand owners. Maximus focuses on compounded semaglutide through U.S.-based pharmacies and notes it fills prescriptions with the same active ingredient as branded GLP-1 products; Maximus also emphasizes that prescriptions are approved by licensed doctors and medication is compounded domestically. Both providers include compounded medication offerings and the same regulatory note about compounded drugs not being FDA-approved.

Access model and telemedicine workflow

Found combines medical evaluation, ongoing program features, and insurance handling as part of a medically supervised program. Maximus offers convenient telemedicine access with no clinic visits, prescriptions approved by licensed doctors, and shipments from trusted U.S. pharmacies. Maximus highlights no membership fees and transparent pricing; Found highlights a membership model with variable starting rates and insurance billing support. If you prefer no in-person visits and a single flat fee, Maximus is explicit about that model. If you prefer an insurance-handled, medically supervised membership with program elements beyond medication, Found is explicit about that approach.

Availability and program scale notes

Found claims insurance is accepted for many patients and references specific programs like Medicare GLP-1 Bridge Program for eligible Part D members. Maximus states its Weight Loss Protocol is available in all 50 states except Mississippi and notes a smaller public track record than larger competitors. Maximus also states that if a patient is not approved for treatment, medication costs will be fully refunded. Found emphasizes program claims about beginning to see results as soon as month 1 but does not supply guaranteed outcomes; both providers include the regulatory qualifier about compounded medications.

Quick verdict: LTTL and bmiMD both offer clinician-reviewed, online weight-management options with compounded GLP-1 therapies, but they differ most in starting price, medication type, billing options, shipping, and lab or regulatory notes. LTTL lists a starting price of $140 and advertises up to $140 off your first month with code auto-applied. bmiMD lists multiple billing options with a 12-month plan as low as $139/month and free shipping on every subscription order.

Direct comparison: pricing, medication type, and billing

The clearest differences in this LTTL vs bmiMD comparison are the type of compounded medication emphasized, how monthly cost varies by commitment, and whether shipping and lab registrations are specified. LTTL highlights compounded GLP-1 medications and mentions “Full-dose GLP-1” and formulations “compounded By U.S.A. Licensed Pharmacies.” bmiMD highlights compounded GLP-1/GIP (Tirzepatide) with four billing options and explicit per-month prices for 1, 3, 6, and 12 month plans.

Starting price and multi-month pricing

LTTL shows a starting price of $140 and promotional language noting up to $140 off your first month with a code auto-applied. bmiMD publishes explicit tiered pricing: $179 month-to-month; $159/mo for three-month plan; $149/mo for six-month plan; and $139/mo for a 12-month plan. bmiMD also highlights that the lowest price requires the longer commitment, while LTTL emphasizes a single starting price and a first-month promotional reduction.

Medication focus: GLP-1 versus GLP-1/GIP (tirzepatide)

LTTL promotes compounded GLP-1 medications and specifically mentions “Full-dose GLP-1” weight loss medication. bmiMD lists compounded GLP-1/GIP (Tirzepatide) and markets tirzepatide as its primary compounded product. Both describe compounded formulations rather than FDA-approved brand products.

Shipping, lab verification, and clinician access

bmiMD explicitly lists free subscription shipping on every order and 24/7 clinician access with online refills and delivery. LTTL states medications are prepared in state-licensed pharmacies and “independently verified in FDA registered labs,” with delivery to your door, and confirms strength every 3 to 6 months to stay within +- 10% of target strength. bmiMD also states medications are prepared in state-licensed pharmacies and independently verified in FDA- and DEA-registered labs.

Regulatory and treatment availability notes

Both sources clearly note: “Treatment is available only when a licensed clinician determines it is medically appropriate.” Both include the regulatory caution that “Compounded medications are not FDA-approved and are not reviewed by FDA for safety, effectiveness or quality.” LTTL additionally repeats that compounded medications, when offered, are not FDA-approved and are not reviewed by FDA for safety, effectiveness or quality.

How to decide between LTTL vs bmiMD

Focus your decision on the documented differences. Choose LTTL if you value a clear starting price and the advertised up to $140 off your first month and if you prefer a provider that highlights “Full-dose GLP-1” compounded in U.S.A. licensed pharmacies with independent lab verification. Choose bmiMD if you prefer tirzepatide specifically, want explicit multi-month pricing that lowers the monthly equivalent to as low as $139/month with a 12-month plan, and want free subscription shipping and 24/7 clinician access.

Sesame Care vs Regenics quickly: Sesame Care is a telehealth marketplace where patients choose clinicians and subscription care starts as low as $59/mo, while Regenics offers nurse-practitioner-led access to compounded semaglutide and tirzepatide with promotional first-month prices starting at $139 and $189 respectively. Early verdict: choose Sesame Care for clinician choice and insurance options; choose Regenics for lower promotional cash-pay entry and compounded medication options.

How they handle clinicians and care

Sesame Care operates as a marketplace that lets patients browse and select providers based on specialty, availability, and pricing. Regenics delivers care overseen by licensed nurse practitioners who review intake before anything is prescribed. This is a direct tradeoff: Sesame Care emphasizes broad clinician choice and a large clinician network, while Regenics emphasizes a nurse-practitioner-led, streamlined telehealth model.

Pricing structure and medication costs

Sesame Care lists subscription care “as low as $59/mo” based on a 12-month Success by Sesame commitment, and cash-pay GLP-1 medications as low as $149/mo, with insurance accepted for weight loss medications and price-with-insurance examples as low as $25 for some brands. Regenics advertises compounded semaglutide starting at $139 for the first month (renews $199/mo) and tirzepatide $189 first month (renews $279), with promotional pricing applying to the first month only. Medication costs on Sesame Care are not included in the subscription price and will vary based on insurance status and pharmacy.

Medications and regulatory notes

Regenics offers compounded semaglutide and compounded tirzepatide prepared by U.S. licensed compounding pharmacies and notes that compounded medications are not FDA-approved finished products and are not reviewed by FDA for safety, effectiveness or quality. Sesame Care promotes branded and oral options such as semaglutide and tirzepatide pricing and notes that medication-specific approval status must be confirmed; branded and compounded products are not interchangeable. Both providers include language that treatment is available when a licensed clinician determines it is medically appropriate.

Features and best-fit users

Sesame Care is described as best for people who want broad telehealth choice, flexible scheduling, and nationwide access in many states. Regenics is described as best for patients who want a straightforward cash-pay entry into GLP-1 therapy overseen by licensed nurse practitioners and who value a secondary peptide line like Sermorelin. Regenics also markets FSA/HSA eligibility and a LegitScript certified pharmacy network for its compounded medications.

Limitations and important qualifiers

Sesame Care notes that results may vary and milestones are based on a study of injectable semaglutide 2.4 mg when paired with healthy lifestyle changes, and that medication costs are not included in subscription pricing. Regenics states that compounded medications are not FDA-approved finished products and that promotional pricing applies to the first month only. Both platforms require a licensed clinician to determine whether treatment is clinically appropriate.

Conclusion: Sesame Care vs Regenics

Sesame Care vs Regenics boils down to clinician choice, insurance flexibility, and medication type. Choose Sesame Care if you want a marketplace model, the ability to pick clinicians, ongoing subscription care starting as low as $59/mo, and the option to use insurance for branded medications. Choose Regenics if you want lower promotional cash-pay entry for compounded semaglutide or tirzepatide, nurse-practitioner-led telehealth, free shipping with U.S. compounding, and access to Sermorelin peptide therapy. The final choice depends on whether you prioritize clinician choice and insurance coverage or promotional cash-pay compounded medications.

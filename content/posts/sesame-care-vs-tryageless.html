Sesame Care vs Try Ageless is a comparison between a clinician-choice telehealth marketplace and a self-pay compounding-focused program. Quick verdict: pick Sesame Care if you want broad clinician choice and insurance-accepted branded options; pick Try Ageless if you want the lowest prepaid monthly entry price and needle-free compounded tirzepatide drops.

Explore Sesame Care clinician options

How they differ: clinician model, medication format, and pricing

Sesame Care is a telehealth marketplace that lets patients browse and select clinicians based on specialty, availability, and pricing, with ongoing video visits, dedicated provider care, labs, and messaging for as low as $59/mo based on a 12-month Success by Sesame commitment. Try Ageless is a self-pay program that sells compounded semaglutide and tirzepatide in three formats: injections or needle-free tirzepatide oral drops, with yearly per-month pricing from $159 for oral drops, $199 for semaglutide injections, and $249 for tirzepatide injections.

Distinctive difference 1: Clinician choice and marketplace vs centralized program

Sesame Care emphasizes a marketplace model where patients choose clinicians from a large network. The source highlights flexible scheduling and nationwide access in many states. Try Ageless operates as a single program offering clinician-prescribed compounded products with clinician continuity under its plans, and allows switching formats under the same plan with no new consult fee.

Distinctive difference 2: Medication formats and FDA status

Try Ageless sells compounded semaglutide and tirzepatide, including needle-free tirzepatide oral drops. The provider states that compounded medications are not FDA-approved and that oral tirzepatide drops are an off-label compounded format because tirzepatide is not FDA-approved in oral form. Sesame Care lists both oral and injectable branded options, noting Wegovy pill is FDA-approved for weight loss and Zepbound KwikPen is an FDA-approved injectable for obesity when applicable. The evidence includes a regulatory note that medication-specific approval status must be confirmed and that branded and compounded products are not interchangeable.

Distinctive difference 3: Pricing structure, insurance, and discounts

Sesame Care publishes subscription and medication pricing examples. Subscription care is described as low as $59/mo with medication cash-pay examples such as semaglutide cash-pay as low as $149/mo and tirzepatide cash-pay from $299/mo with price-with-insurance figures noted as low as $25 for some strengths. Sesame Care also states medication costs are not included in the subscription price and will vary based on insurance and pharmacy.

Try Ageless positions discounting differently. Its landing evidence says a discount is already applied to every plan with savings of $100+/mo versus retail on yearly plans and a stackable code SLIM50 for an extra $50 off each order. Try Ageless emphasizes no insurance required, free discreet shipping, and yearly per-month pricing that gives lower entry points when prepaid.

Safety and regulatory notes to consider

The evidence includes explicit regulatory language. Try Ageless states compounded medications are not FDA-approved and have not been reviewed by the FDA for safety, effectiveness, or quality. Sesame Care notes that medication-specific approval status must be confirmed and that branded and compounded products are not interchangeable. Neither source in the provided evidence guarantees outcomes; Sesame Care includes milestone examples with the qualifier that results may vary and are based on a clinical study when paired with lifestyle changes.

Which should you choose: Sesame Care vs Try Ageless

Choose Sesame Care if you want a marketplace model that lets you pick from a large clinician network, prefer access to branded FDA-approved options when available, and may use insurance for weight loss medications. Choose Try Ageless if you prioritize the lowest prepaid monthly entry price, want a needle-free tirzepatide oral-drops option, or prefer compounded formats with a built-in discount and the ability to switch formats under one plan. Both providers require clinician review and prescribing where applicable, and medication costs and availability vary by plan and insurance status.

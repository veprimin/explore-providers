Quick comparison: what differs most

The most distinctive differences supported by the sources are price and starting cost, pricing model and subscriptions, support and clinician interaction, shipping and extras, and regulatory notes about compounded medications. The sections below compare Oak Longevity and Refills directly on those points.

Price and starting cost

Refills advertises GLP-1 from $99/month (~$5/day) with flat pricing at every dosage. Oak Longevity lists semaglutide from $133/month and tirzepatide from $199/month. If absolute starting price is the priority, Refills is the lower starting cost according to the sources. Oak Longevity presents slightly higher starting figures but highlights one flat price that covers all dosages.

Pricing model, subscriptions, and cancellations

Both providers state no membership or app fees. Oak Longevity emphasizes no subscriptions and non-recurring monthly billing where you choose to renew payment each month; it also notes three-month prescriptions are billed upfront and no refunds are available after physician approval and once medication has shipped. Refills presents month-to-month service that you can cancel anytime with no contracts or cancellation fees and says you fill out a refill form at the end of each month to continue shipments.

Support, clinician interaction, and extras

Oak Longevity includes free health coaching and free shipping with tracking, and offers asynchronous clinical review with no required video call. Refills offers free discreet shipping and free 24/7 health concierge support, and notes after the intake quiz a licensed clinician will review your information and you may either have a chat with a doctor or receive a prescription directly within 24 hours. Oak leans on included coaching and no video visits; Refills emphasizes always-available concierge and the possibility of a clinician chat.

Availability and pharmacy sourcing

Refills states availability in all 50 US states and that it offers GLP-1s from U.S. pharmacies with partner clinicians. Oak Longevity notes treatment is available only when a licensed clinician determines it is medically appropriate and that patients with BMI below 20 do not qualify for GLP-1 treatment. Both sources state compounded medications are offered and include regulatory notes about compounded products.

Regulatory note and product type

Both providers’ evidence includes the regulatory qualification that compounded medications are not FDA-approved finished products and are not reviewed by the FDA for safety, effectiveness, or quality. Oak Longevity specifically states compounded medications, when offered, are not FDA-approved and are not reviewed by FDA for safety, effectiveness or quality. Refills similarly states compounded drug products are not approved or evaluated for safety, effectiveness, or quality by the FDA.

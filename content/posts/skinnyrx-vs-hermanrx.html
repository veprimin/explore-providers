SkinnyRx vs HermanRx: head-to-head summary

This comparison focuses on five distinctive differences supported by the sources: what the monthly price includes, available GLP-1 molecules, shipping and timeline, billing and fees including FSA/HSA, and regulatory or quality notes about compounded medications. The primary keyword SkinnyRx vs HermanRx appears to identify the comparison throughout.

Difference 1: What the monthly price includes

SkinnyRx lists semaglutide tablets as “As low as $249/mo” and highlights FSA/HSA eligibility and free overnight shipping. HermanRx lists “semaglutide + B12 at $249/month” and explicitly describes that price as all-inclusive, covering provider review, prescription, medication, ongoing support, and free discreet shipping with no membership or consultation fee.

Difference 2: Available medication options

Both providers offer compounded semaglutide options starting at $249/mo. HermanRx additionally advertises compounded tirzepatide + B12 at $349/mo as an available GLP-1 molecule option. SkinnyRx focuses on daily oral semaglutide tablets and presents them as an oral alternative to injectable GLP-1s.

Difference 3: Shipping and timeline

SkinnyRx promotes free overnight shipping on all orders and states that after a prescription is written you will receive shipping and tracking information within 2 business days. HermanRx describes a published timeline of about a 24 hour provider review and roughly 72 hour delivery when prescribed, plus free discreet shipping in plain unmarked packaging to all 50 states.

Difference 4: Billing, fees and flexibility

SkinnyRx advertises FSA/HSA eligibility and the ability to split payments with Affirm Buy Now Pay Later; it also frames plans as a subscription service that can be canceled at any time and details a refund policy tied to prescription review completion. HermanRx emphasizes no membership fee, no separate consultation fee, and a single legible monthly bill that includes review, medication, support, and shipping.

Difference 5: Compounding, testing and regulatory notes

Both providers offer compounded medications and include regulatory notes that compounded medications are not FDA-approved and are not reviewed by the FDA for safety, effectiveness or quality. SkinnyRx states partner pharmacies perform third-party testing through FDA and DEA compliant labs every 3 to 6 months to confirm concentration within plus or minus 10 percent of the active ingredient. HermanRx states it offers both FDA-approved and compounded medications depending on the provider recommendation and plainly states that compounded medications are not FDA-approved.

Pricing structure and billing cadence

Pallas Health uses a mixed pricing approach. For compounded semaglutide Pallas lists a first month from $139, then $597 every 12 weeks which the provider presents as a $199/month average. Pallas also lists compounded tirzepatide at $179 first month then $897 every 12 weeks (a $299/mo average). TMates presents a flat $199/month rate at every dose with no step-up fees as dose escalates. If budgeting predictable monthly cost is most important, TMates’ flat $199/month is explicit. If a low introductory month or access to branded pens matters, Pallas Health documents a first-month pricing option starting at $139.

Medication availability and formulation

Pallas Health documents both FDA-approved brand pens including Wegovy, Ozempic, Zepbound and Mounjaro and clinician-personalized compounded semaglutide and tirzepatide prepared in the USA when a brand medication is not the right fit. TMates’ public detail emphasizes clinically dosed compounded semaglutide formulated in U.S. facilities and gives limited public detail on tirzepatide availability. That means Pallas explicitly offers brand-name options in addition to compounded choices while TMates focuses on compounded semaglutide in its marketing material.

Clinician access, state coverage and care model

Pallas Health is 100% online and says care is asynchronous in most states, noting that 43 of 50 states need no video visit while a video visit is required in seven states plus Washington, D.C. TMates states licensed coverage in all 50 states and advertises 24/7 access to U.S.-based doctors rather than messaging-only support. If continuous physician availability across all states is a priority, TMates documents 24/7 U.S. doctor access and national coverage. If you prefer a single intake that can route to either a brand pen or a compounded product and can tolerate asynchronous care in many states, Pallas documents that model.

Compounding, regulatory notes and plan inclusions

Both providers make clear that their compounded medications are not FDA-approved and are not reviewed by FDA for safety, effectiveness or quality. Pallas specifies that compounded medications are prepared by US-licensed compounding pharmacies and that Pallas does not manufacture or compound medication. Pallas also highlights that plan prices include provider care, ongoing check-ins and care-team messaging with no membership fee and that compounded plans are priced by medication rather than by milligram so titration does not raise the plan price. TMates highlights medications formulated in U.S.-based facilities, clinician dosing, and virtual check-ins with 24/7 physician availability.

Shipping, cancellation and logistics

Pallas advertises free expedited discreet shipping on every plan and notes that compounded plans renew every 12 weeks which affects cancellation timing. TMates advertises free shipping and cancel anytime. Availability for both is subject to medical eligibility and a U.S.-licensed clinician’s review.

Final take: Pallas Health vs TMates

Both Pallas Health and TMates offer clinician-supervised, U.S.-formulated compounded GLP-1 care, but they emphasize different priorities. Pallas Health emphasizes choice between FDA-approved brand pens and compounded options, a low first-month price on compounded plans starting at $139, and plan pricing that is by medication with 12-week renewals. TMates emphasizes a simple flat $199/month pricing at every dose, 24/7 U.S. doctor access, and licensed coverage in all 50 states. The right pick depends on whether you value medication choice and an introductory price or prefer flat predictable monthly billing and round-the-clock physician access.

Choose Pallas Health if you want brand-name pens as an option, compounded semaglutide or tirzepatide with a first month from $139, and a plan price that includes clinician care and messaging.

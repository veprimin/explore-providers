How the two programs structure pricing and plans

Oak Longevity advertises one flat monthly price for all dosages: semaglutide from $133/month and tirzepatide from $199/month, billed monthly on a non-recurring basis. Oak states there are no subscriptions or membership fees and three-month prescriptions are billed upfront when selected. BetterMe Rx lists lower monthly-equivalent pricing for longer plans: compounded GLP-1 treatment from $145/month on a 12-month plan, with semaglutide injection shown at $205 monthly or as low as $145 on a 12-month plan and tirzepatide injection $275 monthly or as low as $215 on a 12-month plan.

Direct comparison: five distinguishing differences

1. Month-to-month cost vs prepaid longer-term pricing

Oak focuses on month-to-month, non-recurring billing with semaglutide from $133/mo. BetterMe Rx achieves its lowest advertised price by using longer prepaid plans, with as-low-as monthly equivalents on 12-month plans.

2. Administration formats available

Oak offers compounded injectable semaglutide and tirzepatide. BetterMe Rx offers both injectable and oral compounded options, including oral semaglutide and oral tirzepatide, making needle-free daily tablets an available format in their lineup.

3. Shipping and extras

Oak includes free shipping and free health coaching with plans and highlights same-day approval for qualifying patients. BetterMe Rx includes free 1 to 2 day delivery and supplies as part of its offering.

4. Billing and cancellation commitments

Oak bills monthly on a non-recurring basis and states patients choose to renew payment each month without penalty; three-month prescriptions are billed upfront. BetterMe Rx bills longer 3-, 6-, or 12-month supplies according to the selected plan and includes cancel-anytime language alongside prepaid longer-term plans.

5. Regulatory and clinical qualifiers

Both providers sell compounded medications. Both sources explicitly note compounded medications are not FDA-approved and are not reviewed by FDA for safety, effectiveness or quality. Both require a licensed clinician or provider review to determine medical appropriateness.

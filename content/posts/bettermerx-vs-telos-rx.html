Pricing structure and transparency

BetterMe Rx presents clear monthly prices plus stated monthly-equivalent reductions for longer prepaid plans, with a headline “from $145/mo on a 12-month plan” and explicit monthly prices that may be reduced on 3, 6, or 12-month plans. Telos Rx emphasizes “as low as” prices such as semaglutide from $99/mo and tirzepatide from $139/mo and notes that final price depends on plan and provider approval. The captured Telos Rx page also shows examples of higher monthly amounts and advises that your price depends on your plan.

Formats and medication options

BetterMe Rx explicitly offers four formats: injectable semaglutide and tirzepatide plus oral semaglutide and oral tirzepatide, with the needle-free daily tirzepatide tablet noted as uncommon in the cash-pay market. Telos Rx describes compounded semaglutide and tirzepatide and explains that tirzepatide works on GLP-1 and GIP while semaglutide acts on GLP-1; the provider states the US-licensed clinician matches molecule to patient. If format choice matters, BetterMe Rx documents oral and injectable options in captured evidence while Telos Rx emphasizes molecule selection guided by a clinician.

Billing terms, cancellation and shipping

BetterMe Rx advertises no membership fee, no insurance required, no hidden fees and free 1–2 day delivery, and it says you can cancel anytime though lowest prices require a 12-month plan and longer plans are billed according to the selected term. Telos Rx communicates “cancel anytime, no penalties” and “pay $0 if not approved” language appears in captured content; shipping and supply specifics were not verified on the Telos Rx landing capture.

Clinical review and regulatory notes

Both providers require review by a US-licensed provider and both state that compounded medications are not FDA-approved and are not reviewed by FDA for safety, effectiveness or quality. BetterMe Rx explicitly states treatment is available only when a licensed clinician determines it is medically appropriate. Telos Rx similarly notes prescriptions are subject to medical approval by a US-licensed provider.

Final comparison and recommendation

BetterMe Rx vs Telos Rx comes down to whether you prioritize documented format choice and included expedited shipping with prepaid-plan pricing (BetterMe Rx) or lower advertised “as low as” monthly prices and explicit cancel-anytime language (Telos Rx). Both require clinician review and both sell compounded medications that are not FDA-approved. If the captured evidence is missing other operational details, those details were not supplied here.

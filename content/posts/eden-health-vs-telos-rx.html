1. Pricing structure and guarantees

Eden Health presents fixed monthly pricing for each dose: semaglutide for $99 and tirzepatide for $129, and it explicitly states a lifetime price guarantee and that no insurance or membership is required. Telos Rx lists semaglutide as low as $99 and tirzepatide as low as $139 per month, but also notes that your price depends on your plan and that pricing and approval are subject to a licensed provider. The difference here is Eden’s stated lifetime price guarantee and no-membership claim versus Telos Rx’s variable, plan-dependent pricing.

2. Medication formulation and regulatory notes

One major distinction is medication status. Telos Rx explicitly offers compounded semaglutide and tirzepatide and states that compounded medications are not FDA-approved and are prescribed only after review by a US-licensed provider. Eden Health notes treatment availability only when a licensed clinician determines it is medically appropriate and includes a regulatory note that medication-specific approval status must be confirmed; it also warns that compounded medications, when offered, are not FDA-approved and are not reviewed by FDA for safety, effectiveness or quality. In short, Telos Rx centers its offering on compounded products; Eden emphasizes clinician review and instructs verification of medication approvals and interchangeability.

3. Shipping, membership, and cancellation

Eden Health lists free expedited shipping, no membership fees, and no insurance required. Telos Rx says cancel anytime with no penalties and includes an offer to pay $0 if not approved; it also notes prices can depend on plan details. If guaranteed shipping and no membership are priorities, the supplied evidence favors Eden Health. If you value cancellation flexibility and plan-dependent pricing, Telos Rx documents cancel-anytime terms.

4. Clinical review and eligibility

Both providers require clinician review. Eden Health and Telos Rx state that treatment is available only when a licensed clinician determines it is medically appropriate or when prescribed after review by a US-licensed provider. Telos Rx reiterates that a US-licensed provider decides which molecule is right for you and that individual results vary and are not guaranteed. The comparison shows parity on the need for medical approval, with Telos Rx emphasizing that compounded meds are prescription-only and require approval.

5. What each provider highlights

Eden Health highlights a guided GLP-1 program built around a single monthly price that it says is guaranteed for life, and it repeats free expedited shipping and no membership. Telos Rx highlights compounded semaglutide and tirzepatide, the dual-action nature of tirzepatide (GLP-1 plus GIP) and that semaglutide acts on GLP-1, with pricing described as “as low as” amounts and subject to provider approval.

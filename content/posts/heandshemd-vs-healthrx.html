How these providers differ at a glance: He & She MD vs HealthRx

Both offer compounded semaglutide or tirzepatide treatment subject to clinician review, but the three most distinctive differences are pricing structure and term length, coaching and clinician access, and shipping and handling. The comparison below uses only supplied facts and qualifiers.

1. Pricing structure and term length

He & She MD: headline semaglutide injections start at $139/month and oral semaglutide and tirzepatide at $199/month as six-month introductory rates. Those are promotional prices that apply to the first six months and then renew at higher six-month prices. HealthRx: advertises semaglutide from $99/month on a 12-month prepaid semaglutide plan ($1,163 today; $1,188 at renewal) and tirzepatide plans start at $179/month. The lowest HealthRx price requires a longer prepaid plan and its plan price does not change when dose changes.

2. Coaching, clinician access and treatment decision process

He & She MD bundles a coaching app that includes 1:1 clinician messaging, GLP-1-specific coaching and progress tracking at no extra cost with each plan. HealthRx emphasizes that the clinician reviewing a file decides whether semaglutide, tirzepatide, or neither is appropriate; customers are choosing the plan it arrives on rather than selecting medication from a menu. Both require clinician consultation and prescription where applicable and availability is subject to clinician review.

3. Shipping and handling

He & She MD ships medication with free expedited shipping and notes medication is shipped straight to your door. HealthRx advertises overnight cold shipping with cold chain, tracked, free on every plan. Both sources state compounded medications are not FDA-approved and include regulatory notes about compounded medications.

Other practical differences

He & She MD frames its offering as a bundle of medication plus support aimed at people who stalled on bare-bones subscriptions and want accountability. HealthRx frames savings as coming from compounded medication and a simpler pricing model without added consultation or shipping fees, while cautioning that the lowest advertised monthly price requires a longer prepaid plan and that pricing, eligibility and state coverage may change.

Safety and regulatory notes both providers share

Both sources state compounded medications are not FDA-approved and are not reviewed by FDA for safety, effectiveness or quality. He & She MD notes medications are produced in USP-compliant, state-regulated pharmacies but not reviewed by the FDA. HealthRx notes compounded medication is prepared in a licensed 503A pharmacy cleanroom and is not FDA-approved.

Final take: He & She MD vs HealthRx

In short, He & She MD vs HealthRx comes down to whether you prioritize bundled coaching and a six-month introductory price or the lowest advertised monthly price via a 12-month prepaid plan and overnight cold shipping. He & She MD includes 1:1 clinician messaging and GLP-1 coaching in its app and uses six-month promotional pricing that renews higher. HealthRx advertises a $99/month 12-month prepaid semaglutide plan, states the clinician decides which medication is appropriate, and offers overnight cold-chain shipping free on every plan. Treatment availability and prescriptions are subject to clinician review for both.

Pricing structure and introductory costs

Pallas Health: Compounded semaglutide is $139 for the first month, then $597 every 12 weeks ($199/mo average); compounded tirzepatide is $179 for the first month, then $897 every 12 weeks ($299/mo average). Brand-name Wegovy, Ozempic, Zepbound and Mounjaro are cash-pay and billed monthly at higher listed amounts. Provider care is included in the plan price and there is no membership fee.

Mochi Health: Marketing highlights 50% Off Your 1st Month with code BESTWLHALFOFF and lists “New lower prices! Compounded Semaglutide starting $99 $60 & Compounded Tirzepatide starting at $199 $90” and a membership-plus-medication pricing model that starts from $99/month. The platform notes that ongoing cost varies by plan and medication and that the $39 promotional rate applies to first month only for new customers, then standard rate is $79 per month in some plans. Pricing is therefore membership plus medication rather than a single flat plan price.

Visit format and clinical experience

Pallas Health: A US-licensed clinician reviews your health history and personalizes the plan. Pallas is 100% online and asynchronous in most states; the landing-page evidence notes 43 of 50 states need no video visit, while a video visit is required in seven states plus Washington, D.C. Provider care, check-ins and messaging are included.

Mochi Health: Distinguishes itself with live video visits bookable in about 5 minutes. Mochi emphasizes that its synchronous video model provides a real-time conversation with a physician who can answer questions and evaluate the case directly. Mochi also advertises 24/7 support and ongoing appointment availability.

Brand-name options versus compounded focus

Pallas Health offers both FDA-approved Wegovy, Ozempic, Zepbound and Mounjaro and clinician-personalized compounded semaglutide or tirzepatide prepared in the USA when a brand medication is not the right fit. Pallas notes that compounded medications are prepared by US-licensed compounding pharmacies and that compounded medications are not FDA-approved.

Mochi Health centers on compounded medication access across multiple categories and lists compounded options including semaglutide injections, tirzepatide injections, an oral semaglutide pill, and other compounded agents. The source also includes the regulatory note that compounded medications are not FDA-approved.

Membership, shipping, and state coverage

Pallas Health: One all-in plan price includes provider care, check-ins and messaging with no membership fee. Free expedited discreet shipping is listed on every plan. Eligibility is determined by a US-licensed clinician. Pallas does not bill insurance and is cash-pay only.

Mochi Health: Uses a membership-plus-medication model. It states reliable GLP-1 prescription access in all 50 states and highlights 24/7 support. Shipping and promotional details vary by plan and state; members continue care beyond their first month and terms vary by state.

Regulatory and safety notes

Both providers include the regulatory qualifier that compounded medications are not FDA-approved and are not reviewed by FDA for safety, effectiveness or quality. Pallas explicitly notes that it does not manufacture or compound medication and that compounded medications are prepared by US-licensed compounding pharmacies. Mochi includes the same regulatory note for compounded products.

Comparing Omzo vs Trimi quickly: Omzo focuses on clinician-guided plans with compounded semaglutide starting at $179 and an option for prescriptions for branded medications, while Trimi emphasizes flat monthly pricing from $99/mo, free overnight shipping, and a 6-month money-back promise. Early verdict: choose Trimi for predictable flat pricing and shipping or Omzo if you want clinician-reviewed access and the option to receive a prescription for branded drugs.

How Omzo vs Trimi differ at a glance

This comparison focuses on five distinctive differences supported by the source: pricing model, shipping and delivery, medication options and regulatory status, clinical oversight and medical network, and guarantees or refunds. Each section compares the providers directly using only the supplied evidence.

Pricing model and starting costs

Omzo lists personalized compounded semaglutide starting at $179 and tirzepatide starting at $279. Trimi advertises compounded semaglutide from $99/month on its annual plan and tirzepatide at $125/month, with flat pricing that does not rise as dose increases. In short, Omzo presents per-treatment starting prices while Trimi promotes an all doses same price monthly model.

Shipping and fulfillment

Trimi highlights free overnight, temperature controlled shipping and free overnight shipping for its medications. Omzo states fast, discreet delivery but does not specify overnight shipping in the captured evidence. If shipping speed and temperature control are priorities, Trimi documents free overnight shipping.

Medication options and regulatory notes

Both providers offer compounded semaglutide and tirzepatide prescribed by U.S. clinicians. Omzo emphasizes a Compounded Medication Program that uses state licensed sterile compounding pharmacies and clearly notes that these compounded drugs are not FDA approved. Omzo also states providers may write prescriptions for FDA approved branded medications but Omzo does not sell, dispense, or ship branded meds; patients fill those prescriptions at their chosen pharmacy. Trimi likewise notes its compounded medications are not themselves FDA approved and are prepared per individual prescription. Trimi additionally names compounding pharmacies and makes a certificate of analysis available on request.

Clinical oversight and provider networks

Omzo connects users with licensed U.S. providers and states clinical services are provided by third party networks of U.S. licensed clinicians who determine eligibility. Omzo notes providers make the final determination on prescriptions and that the website assessment does not create a doctor patient relationship. Trimi cites medical oversight from Arora Health led by Dr. Sean Arora and describes a nationwide clinical network; it says a board certified physician founded the clinical network and that providers review and prescribe across all 50 states. Both emphasize licensed U.S. clinicians but Trimi supplies named clinical leadership in the captured evidence.

Guarantees, refunds, and membership details

Trimi advertises a 6-month money-back promise where, if not approved by the medical team, there is a 100 percent automatic refund and a 6-month refund policy for lack of weight loss under terms. Omzo includes disclaimers that individual results vary and no guarantees of weight loss are made. Neither provider guarantees eligibility or outcomes and both note pricing, eligibility, pharmacy, state coverage, and renewal terms may change.

SnagRx vs bmiMD is a direct comparison of two compounding-focused telehealth options for GLP-1 or GLP-1/GIP therapy. Early verdict: SnagRx is the lowest-cost annual plan for compounded semaglutide and emphasizes a price lock at every dose, while bmiMD centers on compounded tirzepatide with multi-month pricing tiers and 24/7 clinician access.

How pricing and plans differ between SnagRx and bmiMD

Pricing is the clearest difference. SnagRx lists compounded semaglutide from $69/month on a 12-month plan and compounded tirzepatide from $119/month on a 12-month plan; month-to-month pricing is higher at $99 and $149 respectively. bmiMD presents tirzepatide with four billing options: $179 month-to-month, $159 for three months, $149 for six months, and $139 for a 12-month plan. Both vendors require a longer commitment to access the lowest monthly equivalent and state that compounded medications are not FDA-approved.

Clinician review, shipping speed, and pharmacy sourcing

Both providers emphasize clinician review and pharmacy fulfillment but with different specifics. SnagRx names OpenLoop Health as the clinician network and lists four named US 503A partner pharmacies, stating an OpenLoop clinician typically reviews intake within five hours and approved orders ship next-day via UPS or FedEx in temperature-controlled packaging. bmiMD notes state-licensed pharmacy preparation, independent lab verification, and online refills with deliveries timed to need, plus free subscription shipping and confirmation processes for strength.

Product type and regulatory notes

Neither provider offers FDA-approved branded finished products. SnagRx and bmiMD both provide compounded medications and include the regulatory note that compounded medications are not FDA-approved and are not reviewed by FDA for safety, effectiveness, or quality. SnagRx explicitly states its compounded medications are produced in FDA-regulated facilities but that compounded products are not FDA-approved. bmiMD similarly states compounded medications are not FDA-approved.

Service differences: price lock, clinician access, and guarantees

SnagRx highlights a price lock at every dose and renewal, positioning that as protection from re-pricing during titration. bmiMD highlights flexible billing with a lowest monthly equivalent on the 12-month plan and 24/7 clinician access. SnagRx advertises a clinician review timeline and next-day shipping while also naming its clinics and pharmacies and citing LegitScript certification. bmiMD emphasizes online refills, verification in labs, and a 100 percent refundable policy if a patient is not eligible.

Conclusion: SnagRx vs bmiMD and how to choose

SnagRx vs bmiMD comes down to whether you prioritize the lowest locked-in annual price for compounded semaglutide and named pharmacy and clinician details, or whether you prefer multi-tier billing, 24/7 clinician access, and a focus on compounded tirzepatide. Both use compounded medications that are not FDA-approved and both require clinician determination of medical appropriateness.

Choose SnagRx if you want bottom-of-market annual pricing for compounded semaglutide, a price lock at every dose, named US 503A partner pharmacies, and next-day shipping. Choose bmiMD if you prefer compounded tirzepatide with multiple billing options, 24/7 clinician access, and free subscription shipping.

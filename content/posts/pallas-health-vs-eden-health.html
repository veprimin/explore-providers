Top distinctions at a glance

This comparison focuses on the most distinctive supported differences between Pallas Health and Eden Health: pricing structure and billing cadence, medication options and regulatory notes, clinician involvement and care model, shipping and renewal terms, and which patients each provider may suit best.

Pricing structure and billing cadence

Pallas Health offers compounded semaglutide starting at $139 for the first month and then $597 every 12 weeks, described as a $199 per month average. Compounded tirzepatide is $179 first month then $897 every 12 weeks, a $299 per month average. Pallas also lists cash-pay brand-name prices for Wegovy, Ozempic, Zepbound and Mounjaro at specific monthly cash rates. Pallas is cash-pay only and does not bill insurance. Eden Health presents monthly pricing at every dose: semaglutide at $99 and tirzepatide at $129, with a lifetime price guarantee and no membership fees. Eden emphasizes one monthly price, guaranteed for life.

Medication options and regulatory notes

Pallas Health offers both FDA-approved brand-name GLP-1s and clinician-personalized compounded semaglutide or tirzepatide prepared in the USA when a brand medication is not the right fit. The source notes that compounded medications are not FDA-approved and are not reviewed by FDA for safety, effectiveness or quality. Pallas states that compounded plans are priced by medication rather than by milligram so titration does not raise the plan price. Eden Health advertises semaglutide and tirzepatide pricing and says medication-specific approval status must be confirmed; it notes branded and compounded products are not interchangeable. Eden also states treatment is available only when a licensed clinician determines it is medically appropriate, and that compounded medications, when offered, are not FDA-approved and are not reviewed by FDA for safety, effectiveness or quality.

Care model, clinician review, and access

Pallas Health is 100 percent online with a US-licensed clinician reviewing health history to personalize the plan. Pallas states provider care, check-ins and messaging are included in the plan price with no membership fee, and that most states are asynchronous (43 of 50 need no video visit) while seven states plus Washington, D.C. require a video visit. Eden Health describes a doctor-guided GLP-1 program with clinician review and a guided program built around the user; the captured evidence emphasizes clinician determination of medical appropriateness. Eden claims it brings medication, care and support together under one monthly price. Specific hours, refund policy, or state-by-state visit requirements for Eden were not supplied in the captured evidence.

Shipping, renewals, and cancellation notes

Both providers advertise free expedited shipping. Pallas notes free and discreet shipping on every plan and that compounded plans renew every 12 weeks, so cancellation timing matters; Pallas also notes plans include provider messaging and cancel-anytime access. Eden emphasizes free expedited shipping and no membership fees, and states a lifetime price guarantee; the captured evidence warns pricing, eligibility, pharmacy, state coverage and renewal terms may change. Specific cancellation windows for Eden were not supplied in the captured evidence.

Quick comparison: what differs most between Shed and LTTL

This comparison highlights the most distinctive differences: availability of name-brand medications, coaching and program model, pricing and first-month offers, pharmacy verification and testing, and assessment speed. All claims below use only the supplied source evidence.

Medication options: name-brand plus compounded vs compounded-focused

Shed explicitly offers both FDA-approved name-brand GLP-1 medications and compounded options on one platform, letting patients choose based on budget and preference. LTTL emphasizes compounded GLP-1 medications prepared by U.S.A. licensed pharmacies and described as full-dose compounded GLP-1. Both providers note that compounded medications are not FDA-approved and are not reviewed by FDA for safety, effectiveness or quality.

Pricing and first-month offers

Shed lists compounded Semaglutide injections starting at $199/month and compounded Tirzepatide injections starting at $299/month, and a limited time 10% off your first month of medication is mentioned. The source also states Shed does not publish standard pricing and that you receive a personalized quote after intake. LTTL shows a starting price of $140 in the source and states up to $140 off your first month with a code auto-applied; the capture also lists example figures and emphasizes same price every dose and 4 doses/month in some captured details. The evidence for exact standard packages, renewal terms, or state availability for LTTL was not verified beyond the captured landing-page elements.

Pharmacy verification and testing

LTTL’s captured evidence states medications are compounded by U.S.A. licensed pharmacies, independently verified in FDA registered labs, and confirmed every 3 to 6 months to stay within plus or minus 10 percent of the target strength. Shed’s capture notes compounded medications are prepared by a licensed pharmacy based on a provider’s prescription and reiterates that compounded medications are not FDA-approved. LTTL’s landing-page capture emphasizes independent lab verification; Shed’s capture focuses on the clinician review and prescription process for compounded products.

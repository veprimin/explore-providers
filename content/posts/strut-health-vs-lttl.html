How the two providers position their programs

Both companies offer clinician-reviewed telehealth weight-management treatment and compounded GLP-1 options, but they position those options differently. Strut Health markets an oral specialization and guided consultations with an emphasis on oral Semaglutide. LTTL markets full-dose compounded GLP-1 delivered from U.S. state-licensed pharmacies with independent verification in FDA registered labs and a promotional credit of up to $140 off the first month.

Key differences to compare

The most distinctive supported differences are program focus, pricing and introductory credits, medication format and pharmacy verification, prescribing and medical necessity notes, and regulatory disclosures about compounded drugs. Below we compare these points directly using only the supplied facts.

1) Program focus and clinical approach

Strut Health: Specialized oral-focused treatment options and guided consultations. The platform emphasizes oral Semaglutide and states the clinical team focuses on oral medication management rather than splitting attention across injection and oral protocols. LTTL: Clinician-reviewed online weight-management treatment offering full-dose compounded GLP-1; messaging centers on delivering powerful full-dose GLP-1 medication to support appetite regulation and fullness.

2) Pricing signals and introductory offers

Strut Health shows a starting price of From $199 per Month and lists features like No Hidden Fees, No Insurance Needed, and Free shipping in captured landing-page evidence. LTTL cites “Up to $140 off your first month Code auto-applied” and lists price points such as $249 and $129 and references “Same price, every dose.” Pricing, eligibility and renewal terms for LTTL may change according to the captured notes.

3) Medication format and pharmacy practices

Strut Health highlights oral Semaglutide lozenges and also mentions Semaglutide injection as an option, clarifying dosing frequencies for lozenges (once daily) and injections (once a week) as part of its detailed descriptions. Strut Health explicitly states its GLP-1 Semaglutide offering is compounded and prescribed based on medical necessity by a licensed physician. LTTL emphasizes compounded GLP-1 prepared by U.S. state-licensed pharmacies, independently verified in FDA registered labs, and confirmed every 3 to 6 months to stay within plus or minus 10 percent of target strength.

4) Prescribing rules and medical necessity

Strut Health notes compounded medication is prescribed when patients fail or cannot use commercially available GLP-1 medications and that medical necessity is required by a licensed physician. LTTL states treatment is available only when a licensed clinician determines it is medically appropriate and advises confirming eligibility, medication, total cost, renewal terms and state availability before enrollment.

5) Regulatory notes and quality disclosures

Both sources include the same regulatory disclosure in the captured evidence: compounded medications are not FDA-approved and are not reviewed by FDA for safety, effectiveness or quality. LTTL adds details about independent verification in FDA registered labs and periodic confirmation of strength. Strut Health includes an explicit definition and explanation that compounded Semaglutide is prepared by a U.S.-licensed compounding pharmacy pursuant to an individualized prescription.

Telos Rx vs bmiMD: overview of the services

This comparison of Telos Rx vs bmiMD focuses on the facts each provider publishes: Telos Rx offers compounded semaglutide and tirzepatide prescribed online starting at $99 and $139 per month respectively when a US-licensed provider approves you, with statements that you pay $0 if not approved; bmiMD offers compounded tirzepatide with four billing options and month-to-month pricing starting at $179 and a 12-month option that brings the monthly equivalent to $139, plus free shipping and 24/7 clinician access. Both state that treatment is available only when a licensed clinician decides it is medically appropriate and that compounded medications are not FDA-approved.

Key differences: price structure, medication selection, clinician access

The most distinctive differences between Telos Rx and bmiMD are pricing and billing options, whether semaglutide is offered, and the advertised clinical access and shipping terms. Below we compare these points directly using only the supplied evidence.

1. Price model and starting price

Telos Rx advertises semaglutide as low as $99 per month and tirzepatide as low as $139 per month, with a note that you pay $0 if not approved. Telos Rx also lists higher reference figures such as $299 and $399 paired with the lower monthly amounts in its materials and says your price depends on your plan. bmiMD lists tirzepatide at $179 month-to-month, with lower per-month equivalents if you select 3, 6, or 12 month plans down to $139 per month for a 12-month commitment.

2. Medication options offered

Telos Rx offers both semaglutide and tirzepatide, explicitly noting semaglutide acts on GLP-1 while tirzepatide acts on GLP-1 and GIP and that a US-licensed provider decides which is right for you. bmiMD evidence covers compounded tirzepatide only in the captured materials. If access to semaglutide specifically matters, that distinction is supported by the source content.

3. Shipping, clinician access, and plan commitments

bmiMD advertises free subscription shipping on every subscription order and 24/7 clinician access, and it highlights four billing options where the lowest monthly price requires a longer commitment. Telos Rx materials emphasize clinician review by a US-licensed provider, say cancel anytime with no penalties in some descriptions, and include the pay $0 if not approved qualifier. Both sources repeat the regulatory note that compounded medications are not FDA-approved and are not reviewed by FDA for safety, effectiveness or quality.

Oak Longevity vs Directmeds is a price and format showdown for compounded GLP-1 care. Quick verdict: Oak Longevity is the lower-cost, no-subscription option, while Direct Meds emphasizes injectable and sublingual options, clinician visits, and temperature-controlled next-day shipping.

How the two providers differ at a glance

Both platforms offer clinician-reviewed, compounded semaglutide and tirzepatide and note that compounded medications are not FDA-approved. Key differences supported by source evidence are pricing structure and starting prices, medication formats and shipping speed, subscription and cancellation terms, and geographic availability and eligibility rules.

Pricing and advertised starting cost

Oak Longevity advertises semaglutide from $133/month and tirzepatide from $199/month with a one-price-for-all-dosages approach and no subscriptions. Direct Meds advertises treatment starting at $147 as a promotional headline, but its FAQ lists standard prices of semaglutide $297/month and tirzepatide $399/month. Direct Meds also notes a seasonal promotion and a 50% first-month discount in the detailed description.

Medication formats, clinical review, and supplies

Direct Meds offers injectable and sublingual compounded semaglutide and tirzepatide and includes medication, clinician review, telehealth visits, and supplies in its service. Oak Longevity sells compounded semaglutide and tirzepatide and highlights an entirely online asynchronous clinical review with no video call required; it also includes free health coaching.

Shipping, timing, and logistics

Direct Meds advertises free temperature-controlled UPS/FedEx delivery generally in 1 to 2 days or next-day shipping. Oak Longevity advertises free shipping with tracking and says most patients receive medication within a few days of approval, with same-day approval available for qualifying patients.

Subscription policies, refunds, and cancellation

Oak Longevity emphasizes no subscriptions, no membership fees, and billing on a non-recurring monthly basis unless choosing a three-month prescription which is billed upfront; it states no refunds are available after physician approval and once medication has shipped. Direct Meds advertises no monthly membership fee, month-to-month cancellation for standard plans, and a full refund if a prescription is not approved; subscribe-and-save plans require the stated term.

Availability and eligibility notes

Oak Longevity states treatment is available only when a licensed clinician determines it is medically appropriate and that patients with BMI below 20 do not qualify for GLP-1 treatment per its captured landing-page evidence. Direct Meds states treatment is available only when a licensed clinician determines it is medically appropriate and notes it is not available in Mississippi or Louisiana.

What matters most when choosing between Oak Longevity vs Directmeds

Cost-conscious shoppers with uncomplicated histories will find Oak Longevity’s semaglutide from $133 and tirzepatide from $199 compelling, plus no subscription and included health coaching. Those who prioritize multiple administration formats, temperature-controlled next-day shipping, included telehealth visits and supplies, or the option of a first-month promotional discount may prefer Direct Meds. Both platforms require a licensed clinician to determine appropriateness and both offer compounded medications that are not FDA-approved.

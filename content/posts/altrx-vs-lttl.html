Key differences at a glance in the altRx vs LTTL comparison

This comparison focuses on the most distinctive differences supported by the sources: pricing model and advertised starting prices; online visit and enrollment process; compounded medication and regulatory notes; shipping, pharmacy verification and quality checks; and promotional terms or discounts.

1. Price and pricing structure

altRx advertises a limited offer with compounded semaglutide starting at $89/month and compounded tirzepatide at $149/month, and provides multi-month pricing tiers (for example semaglutide drops to $75/mo on a 12-month plan). The provider repeatedly frames itself as “the most affordable GLP-1 program” and highlights “same price every month” language in its marketing.

LTTL lists a starting price context of $140 in captured material and promotes “up to $140 off your first month” with a code auto-applied. LTTL also references numbers like $249 and $129 in captured text, but the landing-page capture notes that current details were not published or could not be verified. For pricing clarity, altRx supplies specific starting and multi-month prices while LTTL’s captured landing-page material emphasizes an introductory discount that is “auto-applied.”

2. Enrollment and clinician review process

altRx describes a 100% online medical visit model with a 2-minute quiz to get started. A licensed provider from CareValidate Health reviews your evaluation and conducts telehealth consultations either by live video or asynchronous review as allowed by state law. If the provider determines treatment is appropriate, a prescription is sent to a licensed pharmacy.

LTTL is described as “clinician-reviewed online weight-management treatment” and repeatedly states treatment is available only when a licensed clinician determines it is medically appropriate. The captured material does not provide detailed steps equivalent to altRx’s 2-minute quiz description; it emphasizes clinician review without an explicit intake flow in the captured evidence.

3. Compounded medication status and pharmacy verification

Both sources state their offerings include compounded GLP-1 medications and include the regulatory note that compounded medications are not FDA-approved and are not reviewed by FDA for safety, effectiveness or quality.

altRx specifies prescriptions are filled by independently owned state-licensed compounding pharmacies in its network and notes “State-Licensed 503A Compounding Pharmacies” in captured material. LTTL states medications are “Compounded By U.S.A. Licensed Pharmacies” and says preparations are “independently verified in FDA registered labs” with confirmation every 3 to 6 months to stay within plus or minus 10% of target strength in the captured text. Those verification claims are part of LTTL’s captured landing-page evidence.

4. Shipping, delivery and plan terms

altRx advertises free, discreet 2-day delivery, an all-inclusive monthly fee that covers medical oversight, medication and 24/7 support, and stresses “no membership, insurance or hidden fees” and “cancel anytime.” Typical processing and delivery time is approximately 4 to 5 business days in the captured description.

LTTL’s captured material states medication is “delivered to your door exactly when you need it” and highlights “same price, every dose.” The capture also warns that pricing, eligibility, pharmacy, state coverage and renewal terms may change and recommends confirming eligibility, medication, total cost, renewal terms and state availability before enrollment.

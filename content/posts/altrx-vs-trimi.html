How altRx vs Trimi differ at a glance

This comparison focuses on the most distinctive differences supported by the source: starting price and pricing model, shipping speed and temperature control, membership and refund policies, and medical oversight and pharmacy practices. Both providers offer compounded semaglutide and tirzepatide options and note that compounded medications are not FDA-approved.

Price and pricing model

altRx advertises a limited offer: compounded semaglutide from $89/month and compounded tirzepatide from $149/month, and calls itself the most affordable GLP-1 program on the market. altRx also lists multi-month discounts with semaglutide dropping to $85/mo on a 3-month plan, $80/mo for 6 months, and $75/mo for 12 months. Trimi lists compounded semaglutide from $99/month on the annual plan and tirzepatide at $125/month on annual plans, and emphasizes flat pricing that never rises as dose increases.

Shipping and delivery

altRx promises free and discreet 2-day delivery for prescribed medication shipped from a licensed pharmacy. Trimi offers free overnight, temperature-controlled shipping. If shipping speed or temperature control is a priority, Trimi explicitly highlights overnight temperature-controlled logistics, while altRx highlights a discreet 2-day option.

Membership, fees and refund policies

altRx emphasizes no membership, no insurance required, no hidden fees, and cancel anytime. Trimi presents a membership model on annual plans and includes a 6-month money-back promise with terms that apply; it also notes that if a patient is not approved by Trimi medical staff, a 100 percent automatic refund is available. Trimi also stresses flat pricing and that one flat membership covers medication, clinician visits, ongoing check-ins, and free shipping on annual plans.

Medical oversight and pharmacies

altRx uses state-licensed 503A compounding pharmacies and says a licensed provider will review a health evaluation and conduct telehealth consultation; if appropriate, a prescription is sent to a licensed pharmacy. altRx states that prescriptions are filled by independently owned and operated compounding pharmacies in its network and that provider availability and prescribing authority vary by state. Trimi likewise uses state-licensed 503A compounding pharmacies, named pharmacy partners are listed, and medical oversight is provided by licensed U.S. providers with a clinical network led by a named medical director. Both include the regulatory note that compounded medications are not FDA-approved and are not reviewed by FDA for safety, effectiveness or quality.

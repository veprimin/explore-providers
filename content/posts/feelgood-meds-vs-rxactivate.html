Head-to-head summary: pricing transparency, intake, product types

Comparing FeelGood Meds vs RxActivate directly, three distinctive differences stand out: listed pricing and product breakdown, how eligibility and pricing are revealed, and the presence of compounded and branded options with regulatory notes. These differences affect how quickly you can confirm cost, product type, and availability.

1. Pricing transparency and visible starting prices

FeelGood Meds publishes specific starting prices: compounded injections from $149/month, compounded tablets from $249/month, and a branded option shown from $1,999/month. RxActivate provides no price or billing terms before the contact-information step; pricing is gated behind completing the eligibility questionnaire and submitting name, email and phone number. Treatment availability for both is subject to clinician review.

2. Intake flow and eligibility screening

RxActivate uses a two-minute eligibility questionnaire that requests goal, height, weight, target weight, timeline, medication history, sex assigned at birth, date of birth, state and safety screening. The flow displayed likely eligibility then required contact details before revealing plans or price. FeelGood Meds does not show a gated contact-information requirement in the supplied evidence and presents pricing and options up front, though final eligibility and terms remain subject to clinician review.

3. Product types and regulatory notes

FeelGood Meds lists compounded injections, compounded tablets, and a branded medication option. The provider states a Weight Loss Money Back Guarantee and free shipping in landing-page text, and notes that compounded medications, when offered, are not FDA-approved and are not reviewed by FDA for safety, effectiveness or quality. RxActivate describes clinician-matched GLP-1 plans and includes the regulatory caveat that medication-specific approval status must be confirmed and that branded and compounded products are not interchangeable.

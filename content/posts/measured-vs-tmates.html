How they compare at a glance: pricing, meds, and access

Measured and TMates both deliver compounded GLP-1 treatments, but they emphasize different tradeoffs. Measured advertises promotional compounded semaglutide from $89/mo and compounded tirzepatide from $189/mo with same-day Rx decisions, unlimited messaging, dietitian support, and free two-day shipping. TMates offers a flat $199/month at every dose for compounded semaglutide, free shipping, and 24/7 access to U.S.-based doctors across all 50 states.

Distinctive differences to weigh

1. Price model and starting cost

Measured highlights promotional compounded semaglutide starting at $89/mo and compounded tirzepatide at $189/mo. TMates uses a flat $199/month price at every dose with no step-up fees as dosage escalates. If your priority is the lowest advertised starting price, the source shows Measured has a lower entry point; if you want predictable flat pricing at all doses, TMates emphasizes that model.

2. Prescribing and clinician access

Measured notes same-day prescription decisions, unlimited provider messaging, and dietitian support. TMates emphasizes 24/7 access to U.S.-based doctors and virtual check-in appointments for ongoing medical oversight in all 50 states. The evidence shows TMates markets continuous physician availability rather than messaging-only support, while Measured emphasizes same-day Rx decisions plus dietitian support and unlimited messaging.

3. Medication options and formulation

Measured offers compounded semaglutide and compounded tirzepatide. TMates highlights clinically dosed compounded semaglutide formulated in U.S.-based facilities and provides less public detail on tirzepatide availability. Both providers state their medications are compounded; both include the regulatory note that compounded medications are not FDA-approved and are not reviewed by FDA for safety, effectiveness, or quality.

4. Shipping and fulfillment

Measured advertises free two-day shipping on every order. TMates advertises free shipping as well but does not claim two-day delivery in the captured evidence. Measured also notes cancel anytime and no insurance required in its landing-page details; TMates notes cancel anytime and says medications are shipped to most states with availability based on medical eligibility.

5. Outcome and claims context

Measured cites an internal analysis claiming average member weight loss at six months was 31 lbs, with a qualifier that individual results vary and are not guaranteed and that internal outcome claims are not independent evidence. TMates includes patient testimonials in the captured evidence but no independent outcome verification. Both sources are clear that compounded medications are not FDA-approved.

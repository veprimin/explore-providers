Shed vs Vaylen is a direct comparison between two telehealth weight-loss platforms that both offer GLP-1 care but take different approaches. Early verdict: choose Shed if you want name-brand options plus continuous coaching; choose Vaylen if you prioritize the lowest published compounded monthly rate and prepaid plans.

Explore Shed name-brand and compounded options

How they differ at a glance

Shed combines FDA-approved name-brand GLP-1 and compounded alternatives with integrated coaching and a 2-minute approval assessment. Vaylen focuses on cash-pay compounded GLP-1 in injectable and oral formats, publishing low annualized rates like semaglutide from $83.25/mo on a 12-month plan and offering a full refund if a clinician declines to prescribe.

Key difference 1: Medication types available

Shed: Offers both FDA-approved name-brand GLP-1 and compounded semaglutide and tirzepatide options. The provider explicitly notes that compounded medications are not FDA-approved and are prepared by a licensed pharmacy based on a prescription.

Vaylen: Sells only compounded GLP-1 formulations across three formats: weekly semaglutide injections, daily oral semaglutide tablets, and weekly tirzepatide injections. Vaylen also discloses that compounded medications are not FDA-approved finished products.

Direct comparison

Shed gives you a branded pathway plus compounded alternatives on one platform. Vaylen offers multiple compound formats, including a needle-free oral tablet, but no branded products.

Key difference 2: Coaching and care model

Shed integrates continuous coaching into treatment rather than purely issuing a prescription. Its materials emphasize ongoing behavioral support throughout titration and beyond. Vaylen bundles telehealth consultations and 24/7 care-team messaging into plan prices but does not present continuous coaching as Shed does.

Key difference 3: Pricing and payment structure

Shed lists starting rates for compounded medications, for example compounded semaglutide injections starting at $199/month and compounded tirzepatide starting at $299/month, and advertises 10% off your first month. Shed does not publish a single standard price for every patient and provides personalized quotes after intake.

Vaylen publishes headline annualized rates that require prepaying a 12-month plan: semaglutide injections from $83.25/mo (billed as $999), oral semaglutide $99/mo on the 12-month plan, and tirzepatide $149/mo on the 12-month plan. Vaylen also lists higher month-to-month prices if not prepaid and notes you pay before a clinician review but receive a full refund if declined.

Key difference 4: Risk management and refunds

Vaylen explicitly offers a full refund if the clinician declines to prescribe after you prepay the annual plan. Shed uses a 2-minute assessment and clinician review before issuing a prescription; Shed advertises 10% off your first month but does not publish the same prepay/refund structure as Vaylen.

Service speed, shipping, and logistics

Vaylen advertises clinician review within 24 hours and free discreet shipping in 1 to 3 business days for fulfilled prescriptions. Shed emphasizes a fast 2-minute approval assessment followed by clinician review and medication shipping, with coaching starting when treatment begins.

How to choose between Shed and Vaylen

Both platforms note that compounded medications are not FDA-approved and that treatment is available only when a licensed clinician determines it is medically appropriate. The choice comes down to whether you value branded options and integrated coaching or the lowest published prepaid compounded rate and an explicit prepaid refund if declined.

Shed vs Vaylen final guidance: Choose Shed if you want access to FDA-approved name-brand GLP-1 alongside compounded options, continuous coaching integrated into treatment, a fast 2-minute assessment, and 10% off your first month. Choose Vaylen if you accept compounded-only medications, prefer the lowest published monthly rate on a 12-month prepaid plan, want an oral tablet option, and value the full-refund-if-declined policy.

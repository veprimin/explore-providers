Shed vs Hers: core difference in medication offerings

Shed lists both FDA-approved name-brand GLP-1s and compounded alternatives on one platform, offering compounded semaglutide and tirzepatide injections and name-brand options. Hers emphasizes FDA-approved Wegovy® (brand-name semaglutide) and states it does not offer compounded medications.

What that means

Shed: the platform explicitly offers compounded medications and notes that compounded medications are not FDA-approved and are prepared by a licensed pharmacy based on a provider’s prescription. Hers: promotes FDA-approved Wegovy® and states name-brand medication with no compounded/generic alternative offered.

Coaching and care model comparison

Shed integrates continuous coaching into treatment, describing coaching as bundled and ongoing rather than a one-time script. Hers provides a personalized care team with ongoing dose check-ins and requires membership for access to medication and care.

Practical effects

Both providers include clinician review before prescriptions are issued, but Shed highlights integrated coaching throughout titration and plateaus. Hers highlights a personalized care team and ongoing dose check-ins tied to its membership model.

Pricing, membership, and starting costs

Both pages supply starting-price cues. Shed lists compounded semaglutide injections starting at $199/month and compounded tirzepatide starting at $299/month and advertises 10% off your first month. Hers lists Wegovy® pill from $149/month and injectable from $199/month but notes an active Hers Weight Loss Membership is required and membership and medication are billed separately.

Key qualifiers

Shed: “Starting at $199/month” for compounded semaglutide and “Starting at $299/month” for compounded tirzepatide; offers 10% off your first month. Hers: “From $149/mo” for some semaglutide options and notes that an active membership is required (initial $39 first month, then auto-renews at $149/month thereafter) and that total cost includes membership plus medication.

Regulatory notes and clinical claims

Both providers include regulatory qualifiers. Shed repeatedly notes that compounded medications are not FDA-approved and are not reviewed by FDA for safety, effectiveness or quality, and that treatment is only available when a licensed clinician determines it is medically appropriate. Hers emphasizes FDA-approved Wegovy® and also notes that Ozempic® and other drugs have specific FDA indications and availability limitations, and that treatment is available only when a licensed clinician determines it is medically appropriate.

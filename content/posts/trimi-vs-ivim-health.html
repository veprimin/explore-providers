Trimi vs IVIM Health: key differences at a glance

This section compares the most distinctive supported differences: price structure and stated starting rates, compounding and regulatory notes, clinician oversight and outcome claims, shipping and membership features, and third-party review signals.

1. Price structure and starting rates

Trimi advertises compounded semaglutide from $99/mo on the annual plan and compounded tirzepatide at $125/month, with a repeated statement that all doses carry the same price so your price never changes as dose is adjusted. IVIM Health lists semaglutide at $75/mo and tirzepatide at $133/mo in captured landing-page text, alongside a program fee of $74.99/mo and published first-month offers such as a free month with code GIFT75. IVIM’s FAQ notes a $99 first month and $174/month ongoing in some captured details; the materials also describe a no-obligation free consultation typically valued at $75.

Claim IVIM’s free month or consult

2. Compounded medications and regulatory notes

Both providers offer compounded semaglutide and tirzepatide. The sources explicitly state that compounded medications are not FDA-approved and have not been reviewed by the FDA for safety, effectiveness, or quality. Trimi emphasizes that medications are compounded by state-licensed 503A pharmacies and that compounded medications are prepared per individual prescription. IVIM’s materials repeat that compounded drugs have not undergone FDA review and note that medications are prepared by independent licensed compounding pharmacies.

3. Clinical oversight and outcome claims

Trimi states prescriptions are provided by US-licensed clinicians through Arora Health, with a named Medical Director and a board-certified physician leading the clinical network. Trimi also mentions a 6-month money-back promise if not approved by medical team? 100% automatic refund appears in marketing lines and a separate 6-month money-back promise is referenced with terms. IVIM markets a proprietary GLP-1 ID protocol and cites internal published studies and retrospective observational studies supporting their protocol and outcomes, including a claim that patients following the Ivim Protocol lost an average of 46% more weight over 68 weeks compared to another published trial; the source material notes this is not a head-to-head study and the 40% or 46% figures are protocol claims, not independent randomized trial results.

Review Trimi clinician oversight

4. Shipping, membership, and cancellation

Trimi highlights free overnight, temperature-controlled shipping and flat pricing with one membership that covers medication, clinician visits, ongoing check-ins, and free shipping. Trimi notes that pricing, eligibility, pharmacy, state coverage and renewal terms may change, and that compounded medications are not FDA-approved. IVIM highlights membership offers such as a free month code and a no-obligation free consult, and also documents program fees and ongoing rates; IVIM materials state the platform does not bill insurance and that not all services are available in all states.

5. Third-party reviews and trust signals

IVIM Health’s captured content emphasizes a dual 4.9-star rating on Google and Trustpilot as an unusually strong third-party satisfaction signal. Trimi’s captured content focuses on flat pricing, named clinical oversight, and pharmacy partners but does not include the dual-third-party rating detail in the provided evidence.

Conclusion and who should choose which

When deciding Trimi vs IVIM Health: choose Trimi if you want flat per-dose pricing that Trimi states never rises as dose increases, free overnight temperature-controlled shipping, and prescriptions managed through Arora Health with named compounding pharmacies and a 6-month money-back promise subject to terms. Choose IVIM Health if you prioritize a provider with captured third-party review validation (dual 4.9-star ratings in the source), access to a no-obligation free consultation and a free month code GIFT75, and a structured GLP-1 ID protocol backed by retrospective studies cited by the company. Remember both offer compounded semaglutide and tirzepatide and both note compounded medications are not FDA-approved and carry typical compounded-product caveats.

Choose Trimi if you need predictable flat pricing and free overnight shipping; choose IVIM Health if independent third-party ratings and a proprietary clinician-guided protocol are your top priorities.

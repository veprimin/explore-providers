Quick verdict: SnagRx is the clearer option on published pricing and named pharmacy partners, while RxActivate requires an eligibility intake and contact information before it reveals any plan or price. This makes SnagRx preferable for people who prioritize transparent compound pricing and named pharmacy disclosure; RxActivate is more appropriate for people who want clinician-matched plans revealed only after intake.

SnagRx vs RxActivate: how they gate price and eligibility

The most obvious contrast between SnagRx and RxActivate is how each platform presents price and eligibility. SnagRx displays starting prices for compounded medications: semaglutide from $69/month and tirzepatide from $119/month on a 12-month plan, and higher month-to-month rates ($99 and $149). SnagRx states those 12-month rates are price-locked flat at every dose and at renewal. By contrast, RxActivate does not disclose any pricing, medication fees, membership fees, shipping charges or cancellation terms before the user completes an eligibility questionnaire and submits name, email and phone number.

Early transparency vs gated pricing

SnagRx: published rates and clear plan terms for the 12-month plan and month-to-month alternative. RxActivate: pricing is gated behind the medical intake and contact-information step; no price or billing terms displayed before lead capture.

Named pharmacies, clinician network, and approval timing

SnagRx names its four US 503A partner pharmacies and identifies OpenLoop Health as its clinician network. SnagRx states an intake takes about five minutes and that an OpenLoop clinician typically reviews it within five hours and always within 24 hours; approved orders ship next day. SnagRx also states a full refund if the prescription is declined.

Eligibility flow and lead capture differences

RxActivate emphasizes clinician-matched GLP-1 plans and a two-minute eligibility questionnaire that asks about goal, height, weight, medication history, sex assigned at birth, date of birth, state and safety screening. In the synthetic test cited, Alabama was confirmed as serviceable. The flow displayed likely eligibility and then required first name, last name, email and phone number before revealing any plan or price. RxActivate therefore prioritizes pre-intake matching and contact capture over upfront pricing visibility.

Who sees cost information and when

SnagRx displays plan pricing and terms up front including the distinction between the 12-month price-lock plan and month-to-month pricing. RxActivate does not show pricing before users supply contact details and complete the eligibility flow.

What the sources say about compounded medications and risks

Both sources include regulatory notes: SnagRx explicitly states that compounded medications are not FDA-approved and are not reviewed by FDA for safety, effectiveness or quality, and it encourages prospective users to speak with their provider about risks. RxActivate notes that medication-specific approval status must be confirmed and that branded and compounded products are not interchangeable. Neither source supplies independent clinical outcome evidence in this comparison.

Final guidance: Choose SnagRx or RxActivate

Choose SnagRx if you want published starting prices and a price lock for the 12-month plan, named US 503A pharmacies, stated next-day shipping and an identified clinician network with stated review timing. Choose RxActivate if you prefer a clinician-matched plan that is revealed only after completing a two-minute eligibility questionnaire and providing contact information; RxActivate confirmed Alabama serviceability in a test and emphasizes state and medical-safety screening.

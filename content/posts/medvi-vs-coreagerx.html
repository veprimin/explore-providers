Pricing structure: promo entry versus flat monthly rates

MedVi leans on a promotional first-month price: the landing-page evidence shows a winter promo at $179 for the first month including fast, free shipping. The source makes clear the ongoing standalone month rises to $299 and offers a three-month plan at $649. CoreAgeRx is presented as a flat pricing model: the captured evidence repeats “Same Price. Every Month.” with semaglutide at $79 per month and tirzepatide at $129 per month and states no membership fee and no price hikes. These are contrasting approaches: MedVi explicitly warns the ongoing price is substantially higher than the first-month promo, while CoreAgeRx emphasizes predictable monthly billing.

Medication options and compounded status

MedVi advertises clinically dosed compounded semaglutide and repeatedly notes that compounded medications are not FDA-approved and are not reviewed by FDA for safety, effectiveness or quality. CoreAgeRx lists both semaglutide and tirzepatide dosing options on the captured materials and highlights that treatment is clinician-determined. The sources support this distinction: MedVi focuses on compounded semaglutide only, while CoreAgeRx explicitly lists tirzepatide availability up to 7.5 mg as well as semaglutide up to 1.0 mg.

If access to tirzepatide is important, the source evidence points to CoreAgeRx; if a compounded semaglutide program and 24/7 support are priorities, the source evidence favors MedVi.

Process, clinician review and support

MedVi’s process is described as a simple online intake with no clinic visits and an OpenLoop Health clinician review after checkout. MedVi emphasizes 24/7 support from certified medical professionals. CoreAgeRx captures a “3-minute online visit” and clinician-reviewed online treatment, with an emphasis on no membership fees and consistent monthly billing. Both require a clinician to determine medical appropriateness, according to the supplied evidence.

Regulatory notes, published results, and limitations

Both sources include regulatory or cautionary language. MedVi explicitly states compounded GLP-1s are produced in FDA-regulated facilities but are not FDA-approved. It also notes results vary and that patient-reported outcomes were used. CoreAgeRx captured content also notes medication-specific approval status must be confirmed and warns branded and compounded products are not interchangeable. Neither source provides an FDA approval claim for compounded products in the captured materials.

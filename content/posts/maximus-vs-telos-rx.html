Pricing and billing: clear flat rate versus advertised starting prices

Maximus advertises a low flat rate of $132.99 per month for compounded semaglutide and describes a straightforward direct-pay model with no insurance billing. Telos Rx presents lower starting figures on its landing page: semaglutide as low as $99 per month and tirzepatide as low as $139 per month, but repeatedly notes prices, eligibility and final terms depend on the live offer and clinician review. Neither provider bills insurance based on the captured evidence; Maximus emphasizes a predictable flat rate while Telos Rx emphasizes conditional “as low as” pricing.

Medication options and clinical matching

Maximus positions itself around compounded semaglutide delivered once weekly. Telos Rx lists both compounded semaglutide and compounded tirzepatide, describing tirzepatide as acting on GLP-1 and GIP and noting a US-licensed provider decides which molecule is right for you. That means Telos Rx documents two medication pathways on its landing page, while Maximus focuses on semaglutide.

Clinician review, telemedicine access, and approval language

Both providers require medical approval by licensed clinicians. Maximus highlights convenient telemedicine access and prescriptions approved by licensed doctors, with prescriptions filled through trusted U.S. pharmacies. Telos Rx states medications are prescribed only after review by a US-licensed provider and that treatment is available only when a licensed clinician determines it is medically appropriate. Telos Rx also explicitly states pay $0 if not approved in its captured content.

Final take: Maximus vs Telos Rx

In short, Maximus is a direct-pay, telemedicine-first option with a clear flat monthly price of $132.99 for compounded semaglutide, shipping and domestic pharmacy sourcing and availability in most states per captured content.

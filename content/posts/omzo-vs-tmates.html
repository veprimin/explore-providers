Medication selection and how they are provided

Both providers offer compounded semaglutide. Clinician access and program oversight

Omzo frames its care as clinician-guided plans reviewed by licensed U.S. providers through third party clinical networks; providers make final prescribing decisions and determine eligibility. TMates highlights 24/7 access to U.S.-based doctors and scheduled virtual check-ins, positioning continuous physician availability as a distinguishing feature.

State coverage, shipping, and logistics

TMates emphasizes licensed coverage in all 50 states and free shipping, with availability based on medical eligibility. Omzo notes fast, discreet delivery but warns that eligibility, state coverage, pharmacy capacity, and renewal terms may change. Both remind users that availability depends on medical eligibility and provider assessment.

Regulatory and safety notes

Both providers explicitly state that compounded medications are not FDA-approved and are not reviewed by FDA for safety, effectiveness or quality. Omzo adds that its compounded medications are produced in FDA-registered facilities but remain unapproved by FDA. Omzo also cautions that individual results are not guaranteed and that the platform does not create a doctor patient relationship by the initial assessment alone. TMates notes compounded semaglutide uses the same active ingredient as branded options while remaining compounded.

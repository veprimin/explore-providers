1. Pricing and contract requirements

Pricing is the clearest distinction in SnagRx vs Refills. SnagRx advertises compounded semaglutide from $69/month and tirzepatide from $119/month as the 12-month plan rates. The SnagRx copy and FAQ state the $69 and $119 figures are the 12-month plan rates; month-to-month equivalents are $99 and $149. Refills lists GLP-1 from $99/month (~$5/day) and states its plan is month-to-month with no contracts, no cancellation fees, and no membership or app fees. If you need the lowest advertised long-term per-month figure and are willing to commit to 12 months, SnagRx presents a lower annual-plan price. If you want cancel-anytime month-to-month flexibility, Refills presents the simpler month-to-month option.

2. How pricing behaves as doses escalate

Both providers advertise flat pricing across dosage escalation, but they phrase it differently. SnagRx explicitly markets “Price locked flat at every dose, for life” and explains the 12-month plan rate does not rise as dose escalates or at renewal. Refills states “Flat pricing, the same price at every dosage” and positions that as part of its monthly offering. For patients concerned about hidden titration upcharges, both claim the same protection; SnagRx emphasizes the price lock on its annual plan.

3. Clinician access, pharmacies, and state availability

SnagRx names its four US 503A partner pharmacies and identifies OpenLoop Health as its clinician network and states clinicians cover 48 states. Refills describes U.S. compounding pharmacies with third-party USP testing and says it is available in all 50 US States. Both require a prescription following an intake and clinician review. SnagRx notes clinicians typically review intake within five hours and always within 24, while Refills states clinicians may prescribe directly within 24 hours and provides shipment tracking after processing.

4. Shipping, support, and extras

Shipping speed and patient support differ. SnagRx claims next-day UPS or FedEx shipping in temperature-controlled packaging and free shipping. Refills claims express shipping with free discreet shipping and provides 24/7 health concierge support plus unlimited access to clinicians for questions at no extra cost. SnagRx does not advertise coaching or nutrition programming; Refills highlights ongoing patient concierge and clinician access as a feature.

5. Safety and regulatory language

Both providers make the same regulatory point: compounded medications are not FDA-approved and are not reviewed by the FDA for safety, effectiveness, or quality. SnagRx explicitly states its compounded medications are produced in FDA-regulated facilities but are not FDA-approved finished products and recommends prospective users speak with their provider about risks and benefits. Refills states compounded drug products are not approved or evaluated by the FDA and requires a prescription.

Comparison summary

SnagRx vs Refills boils down to a tradeoff: SnagRx offers lower 12-month plan pricing with a stated lifetime price lock and named 503A pharmacies and clinician network; Refills offers month-to-month pricing, 24/7 health concierge, and availability in all 50 US States.

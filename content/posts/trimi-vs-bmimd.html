Head-to-head summary: Trimi vs bmiMD

Both offer compounded GLP-1/GIP options shipped to your door and state-licensed pharmacies, but they differ on drug focus, pricing structure, refunds and clinician access. Compounded medications are not FDA-approved and are not reviewed by FDA for safety, effectiveness or quality.

Key differences to weigh

1. Medication focus and types

Trimi highlights compounded semaglutide and tirzepatide with explicit semaglutide pricing from $99/mo and tirzepatide at $125/month on annual plans. bmiMD emphasizes compounded tirzepatide (GLP-1/GIP) with multi-month subscription options and the lowest monthly equivalent on a 12-month plan.

2. Pricing model and commitments

Trimi advertises flat pricing that never rises as dose increases and lists semaglutide from $99/mo on annual plans and tirzepatide $125/month. bmiMD offers multiple billing options: $179 month-to-month, $159 for three months, $149 for six months, and $139 for the 12-month plan, with the lowest price requiring a 12-month commitment.

3. Shipping and delivery

Both vendors advertise free shipping. Trimi specifies free overnight, temperature-controlled shipping. bmiMD advertises free subscription shipping on every order.

4. Refunds and eligibility

Trimi states: “Not approved by our medical team? 100% automatic refund” and backs a 6-month money-back promise with terms that apply. bmiMD advertises “100% Risk Free. 100% Refundable if not eligible for program.” Both emphasize confirming eligibility, medication, total cost, renewal terms and state availability before enrollment.

5. Clinical oversight and pharmacy handling

Trimi notes prescriptions are provided by US-licensed clinicians through Arora Health and compounded by state-licensed 503A pharmacies, with named pharmacies and certificates of analysis available on request. bmiMD states medications are prepared in state-licensed pharmacies and independently verified in FDA- and DEA-registered labs, with clinician review required for treatment.

Omzo vs Sunlight compares a clinician-guided platform offering compounded semaglutide and tirzepatide with a lower-cost telehealth option offering injections and oral GLP-1s. Early verdict: choose Omzo if you want clinician-reviewed, compounded options and are comfortable with starting-at prices; choose Sunlight if you prioritize lower starting cost and a fully online, fixed-price monthly plan.

How they differ at a glance

This comparison focuses on five main differences supported by source details: pricing structure, medication type and compounding status, care model, shipping and membership terms, and form factors available. The primary keyword Omzo vs Sunlight appears throughout to keep the comparison focused.

Pricing structure and promos

Omzo lists semaglutide starting at $179 and tirzepatide starting at $279. Sunlight advertises personalized GLP-1 plans from $88 per month and notes promos and messages such as “as low as $88” and “Get $140 Off Today!” Sunlight also emphasizes “Same price forever” and no monthly membership while Omzo presents one-time starting prices for specific medications.

Medication types and compounded status

Omzo primarily connects patients to a compounded medication program where drugs are prepared by state-licensed sterile compounding pharmacies. The source states these compounded drugs are not FDA-approved and have not been reviewed by the FDA for safety, effectiveness, or quality. Omzo also notes providers may write prescriptions for branded FDA-approved medications but Omzo does not sell or ship branded medications.

Sunlight offers injections and oral options and presents prescription and telehealth included. The Sunlight source includes a regulatory note that medication-specific approval status must be confirmed and that branded and compounded products are not interchangeable. Both providers require clinician assessment to determine prescriptions.

Care model and clinician involvement

Omzo describes clinician-guided plans reviewed by licensed U.S. providers and states clinical services are provided by third party networks of U.S.-licensed clinicians who determine eligibility and make final prescribing decisions. Sunlight emphasizes a 100 percent online medical visit with prescription and telehealth included and highlights clinician interactions such as dose adjustments reported in landing-page testimonials.

Shipping, membership, and cancellation

Omzo lists fast, discreet delivery and notes FSA and HSA eligibility, but also warns shipping, pricing, eligibility, pharmacy, state coverage and renewal terms may change. Sunlight advertises fast, free shipping often in 1-2 days, free and discreet 2-day shipping, no monthly membership, cancel anytime, and a promise of same price every month. Both sources advise confirming eligibility, costs, renewal terms and state availability before enrollment.

Form factors and user experience signals

Omzo highlights weekly injection formats for semaglutide and tirzepatide in its bullets. Sunlight explicitly notes injections and oral options, and its landing-page copy includes user testimonials describing fast shipping, online visits, clinician support and dose adjustments. Sunlight emphasizes an entirely online process while Omzo highlights clinician-guided plans connected to compounding pharmacies and third party clinicians.

Comparing Pallas Health vs HumeCare+: Pallas focuses on clinician-selected brand-name or compounded GLP-1 with an all-in plan price, while HumeCare+ bundles compounded GLP-1 with glycine, B12, B6, body-composition scans and a protocol aimed at preserving muscle. Early verdict: choose Pallas Health if you want a clinician to pick between branded pens and compounded options with a low intro price; choose HumeCare+ if you want built-in muscle-support ingredients and monthly body-composition tracking.

Price and plan structure compared

Pallas Health lists compounded semaglutide at $139 for the first month then $597 every 12 weeks (a $199/month average) and compounded tirzepatide $179 first month then $897 per 12 weeks ($299/month average). Pallas also offers brand-name Wegovy, Ozempic, Zepbound and Mounjaro as cash-pay options. HumeCare+ lists semaglutide from $199/month and a tirzepatide protocol from $299/month, with promotional pricing noted. Both are cash-pay only; Pallas explicitly says it does not bill insurance and HumeCare+ presents pricing as promotional that may change.

Medication options and clinician role

Both providers offer clinician-prescribed compounded semaglutide or tirzepatide when appropriate, but Pallas advertises a single intake that can lead to either FDA-approved brand-name pens or compounded preparations chosen by a US-licensed clinician. HumeCare+ presents its offering as a protocol: physician-prescribed compounded GLP-1 plus added supportive ingredients, with dose set by your provider. Both note that compounded medications are not FDA-evaluated for safety, quality, or efficacy.

Support features and monitoring

Pallas includes provider care, ongoing check-ins and messaging inside the plan price with no membership fee, and is 100 percent online with asynchronous visits in most states (video visit required in seven states plus Washington, D.C.). HumeCare+ emphasizes a bundled protocol with glycine, vitamin B12 and B6 added to the formulation for muscle support and side-effect relief, monthly body-composition scans to track lean mass, a dedicated app for tracking and 24/7 clinician access. HumeCare+ also advertises free next-day delivery and a 10%-or-your-money-back promise over six months subject to terms.

Regulatory and practical notes

Both providers offer compounded medications dispensed from US-licensed pharmacies but state that compounded medications are not evaluated by the FDA for safety, effectiveness or quality. Pallas clarifies it does not manufacture or compound medications and that eligibility is determined by a US-licensed clinician. HumeCare+ includes company-reported outcome figures and a money-back promise with eligibility and documentation requirements that must be verified in full with the provider.

How they differ in user experience and goals

Pallas positions itself for people who want a clinician to choose between brand-name pens and per-patient compounded preparations with an all-in plan price and no membership fee. HumeCare+ positions itself for people who prioritize muscle-preserving support, monthly body-composition tracking, a connected app, and 24/7 clinician access, with an explicit protocol approach and a money-back promise subject to terms.

Conclusion: Pallas Health vs HumeCare+

In short, Pallas Health vs HumeCare+ splits on clinician flexibility and price versus protocolized muscle support and monitoring. Pallas starts compounded semaglutide as low as $139 first month and emphasizes clinician choice between brand-name and compounded GLP-1 with provider care included. HumeCare+ starts semaglutide at $199/month and bundles glycine, B12, B6, body-composition scans, an app and a money-back promise over six months subject to terms.

Choose Pallas Health if you want a clinician to select between FDA-approved pens and compounded preparations, an all-in plan price, and lower introductory pricing on compounded semaglutide. Choose HumeCare+ if you want a bundled protocol that adds glycine and vitamins for muscle and side-effect support, monthly body-composition scans, a dedicated app and continuous clinician access.

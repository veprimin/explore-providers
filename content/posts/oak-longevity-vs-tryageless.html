Price and billing: flat monthly vs prepaid yearly discounts

Oak Longevity advertises semaglutide starting at $133/mo and tirzepatide at $199/mo with one flat price that covers all dosages and no subscription. Billing is monthly on a non-recurring basis and three-month prescriptions are billed upfront; Oak states no refunds are available after physician approval and once medication has shipped.

Try Ageless notes the discount is already applied to every plan and an extra $50 can be stacked with code SLIM50. The lowest advertised prices require the prepaid yearly plan; monthly rates run higher.

Medication formats and switching flexibility

Oak Longevity sells compounded semaglutide and tirzepatide delivered through its telehealth process. The Oak model emphasizes one price for all dosages and does not require a video call; clinical review is asynchronous by a board-certified physician in the patient’s state.

Try Ageless differentiates itself by offering three formats: semaglutide injection, tirzepatide injection, and needle-free tirzepatide oral drops. Try Ageless permits format switching under one plan with no new consult fee, and the drops are presented as a lower-cost, needle-free option on yearly plans.

Services, shipping, and onboarding experience

Oak includes free health coaching with every plan and free shipping with tracking, and notes same-day approval is available for qualifying patients. Oak does not require insurance or a video visit; patients complete an online questionnaire and a licensed physician reviews eligibility.

Try Ageless includes B12 in every plan, free discreet shipping, unlimited messaging, and states clinicians can move patients between formats without extra consult fees. Try Ageless is LegitScript-certified and HIPAA-compliant according to its site details.

Safety and regulatory qualifiers to know

Both providers dispense compounded medications and explicitly note that compounded products are not FDA-approved and are not reviewed by the FDA for safety, effectiveness, or quality. Try Ageless also calls out that oral tirzepatide drops are an off-label compounded format because tirzepatide is not FDA-approved in oral form. Oak specifies that treatment is available only when a licensed clinician determines it is medically appropriate and that patients with BMI below 20 do not qualify for GLP-1 treatment.

Final comparison summary

Oak Longevity vs Try Ageless comes down to whether you prioritize lower advertised month-to-month pricing and no subscription (Oak Longevity) or wider format choice and a built-in discounted yearly price with a needle-free drops option (Try Ageless). Both use compounded medications and require clinician review.

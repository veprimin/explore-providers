Shed and He & She MD both combine GLP-1 medication with coaching, but they differ on medication options, pricing structure, and how coaching is packaged. Early verdict: choose Shed if you want access to both FDA-approved name-brand options and compounded GLP-1 plus integrated continuous coaching; choose He & She MD if you prioritize lower six-month intro pricing and a bundled app with 1:1 clinician messaging.

Shed vs He & She MD: overview of what each includes

Shed offers name-brand and compounded GLP-1 medications with continuous coaching integrated into treatment, a 2-minute approval assessment, and a limited time 10% off your first month. He & She MD focuses on compounded GLP-1 plans bundled with an app that provides 1:1 clinician messaging, GLP-1 coaching and progress tracking at no extra cost, with promo pricing that applies to the first six-month term.

Direct comparison: the most distinctive differences

  • Medication options: Shed lists both FDA-approved name-brand GLP-1 and compounded alternatives on one platform. He & She MD emphasizes compounded semaglutide (injection and oral) and tirzepatide injection as program options.
  • Coaching model: Shed integrates continuous coaching bundled into treatment. He & She MD includes an app with 1:1 clinician messaging and tracking at no extra cost.
  • Pricing structure and offers: Shed advertises compounded semaglutide starting at $199/month and compounded tirzepatide starting at $299/month and a 10% first month offer. He & She MD advertises semaglutide from $139/month and tirzepatide from $199/month as six-month intro rates that renew higher.
  • Regulatory notes: Both providers note that compounded medications are not FDA-approved and are not reviewed by the FDA for safety, effectiveness or quality.

Shed vs He & She MD: pricing, promos, and commitments

Both providers show promotional pricing, but the structure differs. Shed highlights a limited time 10% off your first month and specific starting monthly amounts for compounded options. He & She MD advertises lower six-month introductory rates such as semaglutide from $139/month and tirzepatide from $199/month, with clear language that those rates apply to the first six months and then renew at higher six-month prices.

Safety, regulation, and what to expect at intake

Both companies note that compounded medications are not FDA-approved and are not reviewed by the FDA for safety, effectiveness or quality. Shed uses a 2-minute online assessment to check eligibility, followed by clinician review before any prescription is issued.

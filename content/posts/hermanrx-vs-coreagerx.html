Quick verdict: HermanRx emphasizes all-inclusive monthly pricing and a documented ~24 hour provider review with ~72 hour delivery, while CoreAgeRx emphasizes flat month-to-month pricing with lower listed starting prices for semaglutide and tirzepatide. This comparison looks at pricing structure, molecule options and dosing, membership and fees, shipping and timelines, and the regulatory status of compounded medications to help decide between HermanRx vs CoreAgeRx.

See HermanRx all-inclusive pricing

Key difference 1: Pricing model and starting costs

HermanRx presents an all-inclusive monthly price for compounded GLP-1 treatments: semaglutide + B12 at $249 per month and tirzepatide + B12 at $349 per month. HermanRx states no membership fee, no separate consultation fee, and free discreet shipping are included in those monthly prices. CoreAgeRx positions itself as “Same Price. Every Month.” with lower listed starting prices: semaglutide at $79 per month and tirzepatide at $129 per month. CoreAgeRx emphasizes no membership fee and no surprise price hikes, but captured landing-page details about eligibility, state coverage and renewal terms were not published or could not be verified on the captured page.

Key difference 2: Molecules, dosing and product presentation

Both providers advertise semaglutide and tirzepatide options. HermanRx explicitly offers compounded semaglutide + B12 and compounded tirzepatide + B12 as its listed GLP-1 options. CoreAgeRx lists tirzepatide up to 7.5 mg and semaglutide up to 1.0 mg with flat monthly pricing. The captured HermanRx evidence repeats that compounded medications are offered and notes they are not FDA-approved as finished products. CoreAgeRx captured materials note that medication-specific approval status must be confirmed and that branded and compounded products are not interchangeable.

Key difference 3: Memberships, fees and billing transparency

HermanRx stresses “no membership fee and no separate consultation charge” with one legible monthly bill that covers provider review, prescription, medication, ongoing support and shipping. CoreAgeRx also claims no membership fees and emphasizes price stability by advertising the same price every month to avoid mid-program increases. However, the CoreAgeRx capture cautions that pricing, eligibility, pharmacy, state coverage and renewal terms may change and that some current details were not published or verified on the captured landing page.

Key difference 4: Provider review and shipping timeline

HermanRx provides specific timeline claims: a roughly 5 minute assessment, provider review in about 24 hours, and delivery in about 72 hours when prescribed, with a wider FAQ window of 3 to 7 business days depending on treatment and pharmacy processing. CoreAgeRx captured text mentions a 3 minute online visit and asks “How fast is shipping?” but did not publish a specific verified ship-to-delivery timeline on the captured landing page.

Key difference 5: Regulatory note and product status

Both sources include regulatory caution language. HermanRx states that compounded medications, when offered, are not FDA-approved and are not reviewed by the FDA for safety, effectiveness or quality. CoreAgeRx captured materials say medication-specific approval status must be confirmed and note that branded and compounded products are not interchangeable. Preserve those qualifiers when considering compounded options from either provider.

Compounding, regulatory notes, and pharmacy practices

Both providers make it explicit that the medications they offer are compounded and prescription-only, and both state compounded medications are not FDA-approved. HealthRx adds that compounded medication is prepared in a licensed 503A pharmacy cleanroom and explains that compounding skips brand manufacturer markup and the insurance billing layer. Telos Rx reiterates that compounded semaglutide and tirzepatide are prescription-only and subject to approval by a US-licensed provider.

Clinician review, medication selection, and shipping

Clinician review is central for both. HealthRx emphasizes that you are not selecting a medication off a menu; the clinician reviewing your file decides whether semaglutide, tirzepatide, or neither is right. Telos Rx states a US-licensed provider matches the molecule to you. HealthRx also commits to overnight cold shipping with cold chain tracking and no shipping charge on every plan. Telos Rx lists plan-dependent prices and cancel-anytime terms but does not specify overnight shipping in the captured evidence.

Bottom line and how to choose

HealthRx vs Telos Rx centers on three supported differences: tirzepatide starting price (Telos Rx lists as low as $139 while HealthRx starts tirzepatide at $179), prepaid pricing structure and explicit 12-month totals at HealthRx, and HealthRx’s explicit overnight cold shipping and licensed 503A pharmacy note. Both require clinician review, both provide compounded prescription medications, and both state compounded meds are not FDA-approved.

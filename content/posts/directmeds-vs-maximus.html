1. Price and pricing transparency

Directmeds advertises a promotional starting price of $147 and lists standard FAQ prices of $297/month for semaglutide and $399/month for tirzepatide; it also notes a summer promotion that saves $150. Maximus prominently lists $132.99/month for semaglutide as a low flat rate. Neither provider bills insurance; both present direct-pay pricing. If the headline price is the main factor, Maximus shows a lower flat semaglutide headline, while Directmeds includes both promotional and higher FAQ standard rates.

2. Medication options and formats

Directmeds supports compounded semaglutide and tirzepatide and offers injectable and sublingual formats. Maximus focuses on compounded semaglutide and has less published detail about tirzepatide availability. If having a tirzepatide option or a sublingual format matters, the supplied evidence supports Directmeds for broader medication-format choices.

3. Clinical model and support

Directmeds highlights clinician review plus ongoing clinician and nursing support and telehealth visits included in the offering. Maximus notes prescriptions are approved by licensed doctors via telemedicine and emphasizes convenient telemedicine access with no clinic visits. Both require clinician approval before treatment; the distinction in the evidence is that Directmeds explicitly mentions ongoing nursing support.

4. Shipping, supplies and refunds

Directmeds advertises free temperature-controlled next-day UPS/FedEx shipping with medication, visits and supplies included and a full refund if a prescription is not approved. Maximus advertises free priority or expedited shipping and states if you are not approved for treatment, medication costs will be fully refunded. Both include shipping and a refund policy for non-approval in the captured evidence.

5. State availability and regulatory note

Directmeds is not available in Mississippi or Louisiana. Maximus is available in all 50 states except Mississippi. Both providers’ materials note that offered compounded medications are not FDA-approved and are not reviewed by FDA for safety, effectiveness or quality. Treatment is available only when a licensed clinician determines it is medically appropriate.

Conclusion: Directmeds vs Maximus

Directmeds vs Maximus boils down to broader medication and support versus headline price. Directmeds offers clinician and nursing support, injectable and sublingual options, and both promotional and standard FAQ pricing; Maximus emphasizes a low flat semaglutide price at $132.99/month and a streamlined telemedicine workflow. Both use U.S. pharmacies for compounded medication and both note those compounds are not FDA-approved.

Choose Directmeds if you want clinician and nursing support, multiple medication formats including tirzepatide, bundled supplies and next-day temperature-controlled shipping. Choose Maximus if your priority is the lowest published flat semaglutide headline price and a straightforward telemedicine experience.

Early verdict: Found focuses on a medically supervised, biology-centered program with affordable monthly plans starting at $39 while HealthRx centers on lower prepaid pricing for compounded GLP-1s from $99/month and guaranteed overnight cold shipping; choose based on whether you prioritize personalized metabolic evaluation or prepaid compounded medication logistics.

How Found and HealthRx differ at a glance

Found and HealthRx both offer programs that may include semaglutide or tirzepatide, including compounded options, but they differ on clinical framing, pricing structure, insurance handling, shipping, and how medication decisions are made.

1) Clinical approach and program focus

Found emphasizes a biology-first, personalized medically supervised program that targets hormones, blood sugar regulation, sleep, and behavioral factors. The provider describes building plans around each patient’s metabolic biology rather than using a single medication protocol for everyone. HealthRx emphasizes clinician review to decide whether semaglutide, tirzepatide, or neither is appropriate, and describes that what users choose is the plan the medication arrives on. Both require clinician review for prescriptions.

2) Pricing model and payment terms

Found advertises affordable monthly plans with messaging such as getting started for as low as $39 and mentions membership options including a Found membership that can start at $49/mo when billed annually; it also states advertised pricing for branded GLP-1s via insurance with a 12 month commitment and notes copays, coinsurance and deductibles may apply. HealthRx advertises from $99/month on a 12-month prepaid semaglutide plan ($1,163 today; $1,188 at renewal) with tirzepatide plans starting at $179/month and warns the lowest advertised monthly price requires a longer prepaid plan. HealthRx states there is no insurance billing on its plans.

3) Compounded medication and regulatory notes

Both sources include regulatory cautions. Found notes compounded GLP-1s are not FDA-approved or evaluated for safety or efficacy and are not available in all states. HealthRx states compounded medication is not FDA-approved and is prepared by a licensed pharmacy for an individual patient with a valid prescription and that compounded medication is prepared in a licensed 503A pharmacy cleanroom. Both emphasize that treatment is available only when a licensed clinician determines it is medically appropriate.

4) Insurance, shipping and logistics

Found states that most insurance is now accepted, that it handles insurance paperwork, and that it is the only telehealth provider that allows use of insurance benefits to save on compounded GLP-1s with insurance accepted for one third of Americans, while also describing Medicare GLP-1 Bridge Program details for eligible Part D members. HealthRx explicitly states no insurance is part of its plan pricing and highlights overnight cold chain, tracked, free shipping on every plan and that plan prices do not change when dose changes or when dose goes up. Found warns insurance coverage, pricing and terms vary and that copays, coinsurance and deductibles may apply.

5) Transparency on renewals, commitments and results

Found discloses advertised pricing for branded GLP-1s via insurance with a 12 month commitment and notes results vary by individual. HealthRx calls out renewal terms in its prepaid plan example ($1,163 today; $1,188 at renewal) and warns pricing, eligibility, pharmacy, state coverage and renewal terms may change and that the lowest advertised price usually requires a longer prepaid plan.

Quick verdict: Regenics vs BetterMe Rx

Regenics and BetterMe Rx both sell compounded semaglutide and tirzepatide via telehealth, but Regenics emphasizes nurse-practitioner-led care, a Sermorelin add-on, LegitScript certified pharmacies and promotional first-month pricing, while BetterMe Rx emphasizes multiple formats, prepaid term pricing and fast 1–2 day shipping. The early choice depends on whether you want promotional first-month pricing and nurse-practitioner oversight or a wider set of injectable and oral formats with lower long-term plan pricing.

How they differ at a glance

The most distinctive differences are pricing structure and promos, medication formats available, provider model and oversight, shipping and fulfillment timing, and the availability of a secondary peptide line with Regenics. Both sell compounded medications prepared by U.S. licensed pharmacies and both note that compounded medications are not FDA-approved.

Pricing approach and promotional details

Regenics advertises semaglutide at $139 first month and tirzepatide at $189 first month, with renewals reported at higher monthly rates. BetterMe Rx lists monthly prices but highlights lower monthly-equivalent rates when you choose longer prepaid plans, for example semaglutide injectable as low as $145/month on a 12-month plan and tirzepatide injection as low as $215/month on a 12-month plan. Regenics calls out promotional first-month pricing; BetterMe Rx calls out lower long-term equivalents that require selecting a 12-month plan.

Medication formats and product mix

Both providers offer compounded semaglutide and tirzepatide. Regenics presents compounded semaglutide and tirzepatide plus a Sermorelin peptide therapy option. BetterMe Rx offers injectable and oral formats for both semaglutide and tirzepatide, including a needle-free daily tirzepatide tablet option, and notes four medication and administration options overall. If format choice matters, BetterMe Rx documents both injectable and oral alternatives and parity pricing for some tablet options as low as stated equivalents on a 12-month plan.

Provider oversight and clinical model

Regenics positions care as nurse-practitioner-led with licensed nurse practitioners reviewing intake before prescriptions and with a LegitScript certified pharmacy network. BetterMe Rx describes a free evaluation with a U.S.-licensed provider review prior to compounded pharmacy dispensing. Both note treatment is available only when a licensed clinician determines it is medically appropriate.

Shipping and fulfillment

Regenics states free shipping with medication compounded in the USA. BetterMe Rx highlights free 1–2 day delivery and includes medication, provider review, pharmacy compounding and supplies. If rapid delivery is a priority, BetterMe Rx specifies 1–2 day shipping; Regenics emphasizes free shipping and U.S. compounding without a specified delivery window in the captured content.

Regulatory and safety notes

Both providers are explicit that compounded medications are not FDA-approved and are not reviewed by the FDA for safety, effectiveness or quality. Regenics additionally lists its pharmacy network as LegitScript certified. Both programs require a licensed clinician to determine whether treatment is appropriate.

Choosing between Regenics and BetterMe Rx

Both programs require licensed clinician review and both distribute compounded GLP-1s from U.S. pharmacies while noting those compounded medications are not FDA-approved. Choose based on the factors that matter most to you:

  • Choose Regenics if you prioritize a cash-pay entry with promotional first-month pricing for semaglutide from $139 and tirzepatide from $189, nurse-practitioner-led oversight, LegitScript certified pharmacy network and an available Sermorelin peptide add-on.
  • Choose BetterMe Rx if you want injectable and oral administration options including a needle-free tablet, faster stated 1–2 day delivery, and lower monthly-equivalent pricing when you select a 12-month plan.

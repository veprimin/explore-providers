How the two providers differ at a glance

2. Third-party ratings and outcome claims

IVIM Health: The source repeatedly highlights a 4.9-star rating on both Google and Trustpilot and promotes a proprietary GLP-1 ID protocol that the company says may accelerate weight loss up to 40% faster; IVIM also references published observational outcome studies and an internal published comparison claiming an average of 46% more weight loss over 68 weeks versus a separate published GLP-1 trial, with repeated qualifiers that this is not a head-to-head study. Luvo Health: The captured landing page did not supply third-party ratings or published outcome claims in the captured evidence and therefore these details are not verified by the source.

3. Medication formulation and regulatory notes

Both providers’ captured materials show compounded formulations may be offered and include the regulatory note that compounded medications are not FDA-approved and are not reviewed by the FDA for safety, effectiveness, or quality. IVIM explicitly states its offerings include compounded semaglutide and compounded tirzepatide and emphasizes clinician supervision; Luvo’s captured page lists tirzepatide injection, semaglutide injection and tablets, and notes compounded medications, when offered, are not FDA-approved and are provided only when a clinician determines treatment is appropriate.

4. Program structure, clinician review and cancellation

IVIM emphasizes a clinician-guided GLP-1 ID titration protocol, weekly dose adjustments and a clinician evaluation before Rx, and it states the free consultation is no-obligation. The captured Luvo page emphasizes ongoing support, dose changes, and that treatment is available only when a licensed clinician determines it is medically appropriate. Luvo’s captured evidence also includes a cancel-anytime indication but did not publish full pricing, state coverage or renewal terms in the capture; the source warns these details may change and should be confirmed live.

How they differ at a glance

The comparison between Regenics and Maximus centers on five main differences: starting and renewal pricing, clinician type that approves prescriptions, whether additional peptide therapies are offered, pharmacy sourcing and certification, and geographic availability and refund policy. Each point below uses only the supplied facts.

Pricing: promotional first-month vs low flat rate

Regenics advertises compounded semaglutide at $139 first month (renews at $199/month) and compounded tirzepatide at $189 first month (renews at $279/month). Maximus is presented as a low flat rate, priced at $132.99/month for semaglutide. Regenics notes the promotional pricing applies to the first month only; Maximus emphasizes a straightforward, direct-pay flat monthly rate.

Clinician oversight: nurse practitioners vs licensed doctors

Regenics delivers nurse-practitioner-led GLP-1 care, with licensed nurse practitioners reviewing intake before anything is prescribed. Maximus describes prescriptions approved by licensed doctors. Both approaches are telemedicine-based in the supplied evidence.

Learn about Regenics clinician review

Medication options: semaglutide, tirzepatide, plus a peptide

Regenics offers compounded semaglutide and compounded tirzepatide and also lists Sermorelin peptide therapy as an add-on. Maximus focuses on compounded semaglutide in its Weight Loss Protocol and has limited published detail on tirzepatide availability in the supplied material.

Pharmacy sourcing, certifications, and regulatory notes

Regenics states medications are compounded by U.S. licensed compounding pharmacies and that its pharmacy network is LegitScript certified. Maximus fills prescriptions through trusted U.S. pharmacies and notes medications are compounded domestically. Both providers include the regulatory qualifier that compounded medications are not FDA-approved finished products and are not reviewed by FDA for safety, effectiveness or quality.

Availability and refund policy

Maximus states its Weight Loss Protocol is currently available in all 50 states except Mississippi and that if a patient is not approved for treatment, medication costs will be fully refunded. Regenics does not publish state availability or a refund policy in the supplied evidence; it notes treatment is available only when a licensed clinician determines it is medically appropriate.

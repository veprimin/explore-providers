2. Medication type and prescription pathways

Both providers offer access to compounded medications. Omzo describes a Compounded Medication Program where compounded drugs are prepared and shipped by a state licensed sterile compounding pharmacy and notes these compounded drugs are not FDA-approved. Omzo also states providers may write prescriptions for branded FDA approved medications which the company does not sell or ship. Refills offers personalized compounded GLP-1 and also notes compounded drug products are not approved or evaluated for safety, effectiveness, or quality by the FDA. Refills Health offers GLP-1s exclusively from U.S. pharmacies and notes a compounded medication may be prescribed if deemed medically appropriate by partner clinicians.

3. Clinician model and ongoing support

Omzo connects users with licensed U.S. providers through third party clinician networks who determine eligibility and make final prescription decisions. Omzo emphasizes clinician-guided plans reviewed by licensed U.S. providers. Refills requires a health intake quiz with a licensed clinician review; depending on needs you may chat with a doctor or receive a prescription within 24 hours. Refills highlights unlimited clinician access for questions at no extra cost and 24/7 health concierge support.

4. Shipping, membership and cancellation

Refills promotes free discreet shipping on every order, month to month plans, the ability to cancel anytime, and no membership or app fees. Omzo highlights fast, discreet delivery and notes medication costs are included in the fees collected by Omzo and Omzo pays the pharmacies. Specific membership or cancellation terms for Omzo are not supplied beyond shipping policy notes captured.

5. Regulatory notes and quality statements

Both sources explicitly state compounded medications are not FDA-approved and have not been evaluated by the FDA for safety, effectiveness, or quality. Omzo adds that compounded medications offered are produced in FDA registered facilities but are not FDA-approved. Refills notes compounding pharmacies are licensed and inspected by Boards of Pharmacy and that the FDA has not evaluated compounded products for safety, quality, or efficacy.

How Oak Longevity and HumeCare+ differ at a glance

Both sell physician-prescribed compounded GLP-1 medications, but the services differ sharply in price, included supports, and program commitments. Oak Longevity centers on one flat price per medication for all dosages, billed monthly on a non-recurring basis with no subscription and free health coaching and shipping. HumeCare+ packages medication with glycine, B12 and B6, monthly body-composition scans, an app, and a 10%-or-your-money-back promise over six months. Compounded medications offered by both are not FDA-approved finished products and are not reviewed by the FDA for safety, effectiveness, or quality.

Price and billing structure

Oak Longevity lists semaglutide starting at $133/month and tirzepatide from $199/month with one price covering all dosages. Oak bills monthly on a non-recurring basis, states no subscriptions or membership fees, and notes three-month prescriptions are billed upfront. HumeCare+ lists semaglutide from $199/month and tirzepatide protocols from $299/month, and presents pricing as part of a subscription that can be canceled anytime. HumeCare+ also shows promotional adjustments in its listing.

Program content and clinical supports

Oak Longevity includes free health coaching and free shipping, and clinical review is asynchronous with no video call required; licensed clinicians review cases and same-day approval may be available for qualifying patients. HumeCare+ bundles supportive ingredients into the formulation, adds monthly body-composition scans to track lean mass versus fat, offers an app for tracking and food logging, and advertises unlimited clinician access and a U.S. care team available 24/7.

Notable claims and guarantees

Oak promotes low prices and no hidden fees. HumeCare+ advertises a 10%-or-your-money-back promise if you do not lose at least 10% of body weight in six months, with terms that should be verified directly with HumeCare+ for eligibility and documentation. HumeCare+ also reports internal headline results based on its member data; those figures are company-reported and individual results may vary.

Regulatory and eligibility notes

Both providers state treatment is available only when a licensed clinician determines it is medically appropriate. Oak notes patients with BMI below 20 do not qualify for GLP-1 treatment. Both sources state compounded medications are not FDA-evaluated for safety, quality, or efficacy.

Price structure and promotional details

Both providers advertise promotional entry prices but differ in how their standard pricing is presented. Regenics lists semaglutide $139 first month and tirzepatide $189 first month, with renewals noted at $199 and $279. Direct Meds advertises treatment starting at $147 and separately lists standard FAQ prices of semaglutide $297/month and tirzepatide $399/month. The Direct Meds page also references a seasonal promotion that saves $150. These are all presented as promotional or FAQ pricing and may require reading provider pages for terms.

Medication formats, pharmacy sourcing, and shipping

Direct Meds explicitly offers both injectable and sublingual compounded semaglutide and tirzepatide and advertises free temperature-controlled 1 to 2 day shipping via UPS or FedEx. Regenics focuses on compounded semaglutide and tirzepatide plus a Sermorelin peptide option and states medications are compounded in the USA and shipped free. Both note that compounding is performed by licensed U.S. pharmacies; Direct Meds specifies U.S.-based 503A compounding pharmacies and Regenics notes a LegitScript certified pharmacy network.

Availability and cancellations

Direct Meds states no monthly membership fee, month-to-month cancellation for standard customers, and that subscribe-and-save plans require the stated term. It also notes the service is not available in Mississippi or Louisiana and offers a full refund if a prescription is not approved. Regenics indicates no insurance is required, plans are FSA/HSA eligible, and care is nurse-practitioner-led; specific membership or cancellation terms were not verified on the captured Regenics page.

Clinical oversight and safety notes

Both platforms state that treatment is available only when a licensed clinician determines it is medically appropriate. Regenics emphasizes licensed nurse practitioners oversee every prescription. Direct Meds highlights clinician review and nursing support. Both include the regulatory note that compounded medications are not FDA-approved and are not reviewed by FDA for safety, effectiveness or quality.

Which differences matter most

The most distinctive supported differences are: 1) promotional first-month pricing and renewal structure, 2) medication formats including Direct Meds sublingual option, 3) shipping speed and included supplies with Direct Meds, and 4) Regenics offering a Sermorelin peptide add-on and Nurse Practitioner-led model. These factors define tradeoffs between lower initial price versus formats, shipping, and standard FAQ prices.

Conclusion: Regenics vs Directmeds

Regenics vs Directmeds shows Regenics aiming for lower promotional first-month pricing and an added Sermorelin option with nurse-practitioner oversight, while Direct Meds emphasizes injectable and sublingual options, included supplies, next-day temperature-controlled shipping, and clearly stated standard FAQ prices that are higher than its promotional headline. Both use compounded medications that are not FDA-approved and require clinician review.

Choose Regenics if you want lower advertised first-month semaglutide or tirzepatide promotional pricing and a Sermorelin peptide add-on with nurse-practitioner-led telehealth oversight.

Choose Direct Meds if you prioritize injectable or sublingual format options, included supplies and next-day temperature-controlled shipping, and clear standard FAQ pricing and cancellation terms.

SkinnyRx vs HealthRx boils down to two core differences: SkinnyRx emphasizes oral semaglutide tablets with FSA/HSA eligibility and free overnight shipping, while HealthRx highlights lower advertised monthly pricing on prepaid plans and cold chain overnight shipping. Early verdict: choose SkinnyRx if you prioritize FSA/HSA eligibility and oral tablets; choose HealthRx if the advertised $99/month prepaid semaglutide plan or access to tirzepatide are your main priorities.

What each provider offers and how they differ

Both companies offer clinician-reviewed, prescription-based compounded GLP-1 options, but the products, pricing structures, and shipping details differ in specific ways captured below. The most distinctive supported differences are: price and plan structure, medication options and format, FSA/HSA and payment flexibility, shipping and cold chain, and clinician review and prescription process.

Price and plan structure

SkinnyRx is presented as offering daily semaglutide tablets as low as $249/mo, with the option to split payments using Affirm Buy Now Pay Later. HealthRx advertises plans from $99/month on a 12-month prepaid semaglutide plan with a total of $1,163 today and $1,188 at renewal, and tirzepatide plans starting at $179/month. HealthRx notes the lowest advertised monthly price requires a longer prepaid plan and that plan pricing does not change when dose changes.

Medication options and format

SkinnyRx focuses on daily oral semaglutide tablets taken under the tongue as an alternative to injections and describes tablets as designed with clinically supported ingredients to support weight loss and metabolic health. HealthRx offers compounded semaglutide or tirzepatide prepared per prescription and states that the clinician reviewing your file decides whether semaglutide or tirzepatide, or neither, is appropriate.

FSA/HSA eligibility and payment flexibility

SkinnyRx explicitly lists FSA/HSA eligibility and the ability to use pre-tax dollars toward treatment. HealthRx does not present FSA/HSA eligibility on the captured landing-page evidence, and instead emphasizes that compounded medication skips manufacturer and insurance markups and that there is no consultation fee, no shipping charge, and no price change when dose increases on advertised plans.

Shipping and handling

SkinnyRx advertises free overnight shipping on all orders and states customers receive shipping and tracking information within 2 business days after prescription processing. HealthRx advertises overnight cold shipping with cold chain, tracked, free on every plan, and notes compounded medication is prepared in a licensed 503A pharmacy cleanroom.

Clinician review, prescriptions, and regulatory notes

Both providers require a clinician review and a prescription. SkinnyRx notes treatment is only available if prescribed after an online consultation with a healthcare provider and that clinicians tailor treatment and adjust doses. SkinnyRx also includes a regulatory note that compounded medications are not FDA-approved and are not reviewed by FDA for safety, effectiveness or quality. HealthRx likewise states compounded medication is not FDA-approved and is prepared by a licensed pharmacy for an individual patient with a valid prescription. HealthRx explicitly says the clinician decides whether semaglutide or tirzepatide is the right fit or whether neither is.

Final recommendation

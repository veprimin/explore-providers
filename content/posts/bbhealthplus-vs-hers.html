Bodybuilding Health+ vs Hers is a comparison of two telehealth weight-loss offerings where the main differences are medication type, pricing structure, membership requirements, and program scope. Early verdict: choose Bodybuilding Health+ if you want compounded GLP-1s with no membership and multi-program options; choose Hers if you prefer FDA-approved brand-name Wegovy and a personalized care team.

Direct comparison: medication type and regulatory status

Bodybuilding Health+ offers compounded semaglutide and compounded tirzepatide, whereas Hers promotes FDA-approved brand-name Wegovy and lists other brand options. The regulatory note for both providers is explicit: compounded medications are not FDA-approved and are not reviewed by FDA for safety, effectiveness or quality. Hers emphasizes FDA-approved Wegovy and states it does not offer compounded products.

Price model and membership differences

Pricing and billing are a key distinction. Bodybuilding Health+ lists semaglutide from $179 per month and tirzepatide from $209 per month and highlights no monthly membership, no hidden fees, and a cancel anytime approach. Hers lists Wegovy from $149 per month for its pill option and injectables from $199 per month while requiring an active Hers Weight Loss Membership to access medication; the membership is $39 for the first month and auto-renews at $149 per month thereafter. Hers notes medication is not available without an active membership and that total cost includes both membership and medication when prescribed.

Care model and program scope

Bodybuilding Health+ positions itself as a multi-program telehealth platform spanning weight management, longevity, and sexual performance with offerings like NAD+ and sermorelin from $129 per month and ED options from $96 per month. It emphasizes provider-reviewed medications and medical oversight by Dr. Mark Rosenberg, M.D. Hers stresses a personalized care team with ongoing dose check-ins and focuses on brand-name, FDA-approved GLP-1s like Wegovy, noting Ozempic may be prescribed off-label at clinician discretion.

Availability and eligibility notes

Both providers state that treatment is available only when a licensed clinician determines it is medically appropriate and that products may not be available in all states. Bodybuilding Health+ warns that compounded GLP-1s are not FDA-approved. Hers notes brand availability depends on clinical eligibility criteria and that some brand medications are only available to patients who meet those criteria.

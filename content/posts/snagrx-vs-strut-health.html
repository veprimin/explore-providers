Core difference: Pricing model and commitment

SnagRx advertises compounded semaglutide from $69/month and compounded tirzepatide from $119/month on a 12-month plan and states the rate is locked flat at every dose for the life of treatment. Month-to-month rates are higher: $99 for semaglutide and $149 for tirzepatide. Strut Health lists programs from $199 per month and focuses its offering on oral semaglutide options. The comparison centers on SnagRx’s annual price lock versus Strut Health’s oral specialization and higher starting monthly cost.

SnagRx vs Strut Health: Key distinctions

1) Formulation and program focus

SnagRx provides compounded injectable GLP-1s including semaglutide and tirzepatide. Strut Health specializes in oral semaglutide lozenges and oral-based program options, with guided consults. If you need an oral option, the supplied evidence shows Strut Health is the oral specialist; SnagRx emphasizes compounded injections.

2) Price structure and transparency

SnagRx markets lower entry pricing on its 12-month plan (semaglutide $69/month; tirzepatide $119/month) and highlights a locked flat price through titration and renewal. The $69 and $119 figures are explicitly tied to the 12-month commitment; month-to-month prices are higher. Strut Health’s captured evidence lists programs from $199/month and/or from $99 in some captured lines for oral listings, but the primary stated starting price in the source is $199 per month for its programs. Both providers note no insurance required and free shipping in their captured landing details.

3) Clinical network and eligibility

SnagRx operates with OpenLoop Health clinicians, names its US 503A compounding pharmacy partners, and states clinician review typically within five hours and coverage across 48 states. SnagRx also notes clinicians retain the decision to prescribe and that the intake and clinician review do not create a doctor patient relationship automatically. Strut Health emphasizes guided consults and that compounded semaglutide is prescribed when a licensed physician determines medical necessity, and that oral semaglutide may be available only for patients who fail or cannot use commercially available GLP-1 medications. Both providers’ sources state compounded medications are not FDA approved.

4) Shipping and turnaround

SnagRx captures next-day UPS or FedEx shipping in temperature controlled packaging for approved orders and says orders ship next day with clinician review typically within five hours. Strut Health’s landing evidence notes free shipping and cancel anytime but does not provide a specific next-day shipping claim in the captured material.

5) Additional services and limitations

SnagRx discloses no coaching or nutrition programming captured in the evidence, names partner pharmacies, states LegitScript certification and lists outcome and refund policies in captured content. Strut Health highlights ongoing progress communication and guided consultations as part of its oral specialization; it also notes a smaller network than larger telehealth marketplaces in the captured material.

How eligibility and prescribing differ

Both sources stress that compounded semaglutide is prescribed based on clinician judgment. SnagRx states OpenLoop Health has exclusionary criteria and clinicians review after checkout; a declined prescription is refunded. Strut Health specifies oral semaglutide is available only to patients who fail or cannot use commercially available GLP-1 medications and that medical necessity is required by a licensed physician.

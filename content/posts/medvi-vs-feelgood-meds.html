MedVi vs FeelGood Meds: how price and promotions compare

Price is a leading difference. FeelGood Meds advertises compounded injections from $149/month and compounded tablets from $249/month, plus a branded option shown from $1,999/month. MedVi promotes a winter first-month price of $179 including fast, free shipping, then notes a standalone month at $299 and a three-month plan at $649 (about $216/month). All prices are subject to each provider’s terms and clinician review.

Medication options and formulation differences

Both platforms offer compounded GLP-1 medications. MedVi highlights clinically dosed compounded semaglutide only and states it prescribes compounded semaglutide, not branded products, and that compounded medications are not FDA-approved. FeelGood Meds lists compounded injections and compounded tablets and also shows a branded medication option at a much higher price. If branded, FDA-approved options matter to you, the providers present different visible options but MedVi emphasizes compounded semaglutide only.

Process, clinician review, and support

Both require clinician review and operate online. MedVi promotes a simple online intake with no clinic visits, physician review, and 24/7 support from certified medical professionals, with prescriptions filled at licensed pharmacies. FeelGood Meds describes clinician-reviewed online weight-management treatment and asks that treatment be available only when a licensed clinician determines it is medically appropriate. Both stress that medication is provided only if clinicians deem it appropriate.

Shipping, membership, and fees

FeelGood Meds explicitly lists no membership fee and free shipping. MedVi’s first-month promo includes fast, free shipping and states no membership or hidden fees in promotional text, while some copied content references a $99 membership + medication cost in other places; MedVi also notes that ongoing pricing is higher after the promotional month. Confirm current membership terms and shipping details with each provider at checkout.

Regulatory and outcome notes

Both sources include the regulatory qualifier that compounded medications are not FDA-approved and are not reviewed by the FDA for safety, effectiveness, or quality. MedVi further details that compounded GLP-1s are produced in FDA-regulated facilities but are not FDA-approved, that results vary, and that medication prescriptions are at the discretion of medical providers. FeelGood Meds repeats the regulatory note and adds a weight loss money back guarantee claim in landing-page text; eligibility and terms were not captured in expanded FAQ content.

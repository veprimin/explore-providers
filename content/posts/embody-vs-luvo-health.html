1. Price and entry cost

Embody publishes a specific low entry price: compounded semaglutide at $69/month, marked as cut from $299, and compounded tirzepatide at $119/month. The source repeatedly frames $69/month as a limited-time offer and notes final pricing may vary based on dose and clinician recommendation. Luvo Health’s captured landing page did not present verifiable starting prices; pricing, eligibility and renewal terms were flagged as not published or could not be verified on the captured page.

2. Medication types and program choices

Embody’s offering in the source is clearly compounded semaglutide and compounded tirzepatide. The listing explicitly notes these are compounded products rather than branded Ozempic, Wegovy or Zepbound. Luvo Health lists multiple formats on the captured page including tirzepatide injection, semaglutide injection and semaglutide tablets and references microdosing options, but the page also warns that when compounded medications are offered they are not FDA-approved.

3. Shipping, delivery and convenience

Embody advertises free shipping with delivery in 1–2 days after clinician approval, calling this one of the faster delivery commitments. Luvo Health’s captured content mentions ongoing support and dose changes and cancel anytime terms, but specific shipping speed or guaranteed delivery timing was not published or could not be verified on the captured landing page.

4. Program features, support and transparency

Embody positions itself as a low-cost, no-frills telehealth program: prescription and telemed visits are included, no insurance is required, there is no monthly membership required, and the source highlights 24/7 support access for dosing and side-effect questions. The trade-off noted in the source is fewer premium features such as integrated coaching or advanced monitoring. Luvo Health emphasizes clinician review, dose adjustments based on clinical response and cancel anytime language, but the captured page lacked verifiable detail on pricing, state coverage and renewal terms; the source advises confirming eligibility and total cost before enrollment.

5. Regulatory and safety notes

Both sources include the same regulatory qualifier about compounded medications: compounded medications are not FDA-approved and are not reviewed by FDA for safety, effectiveness or quality. Embody’s source explicitly reiterates that its compounded medications are not FDA-approved finished products while noting they use the same active ingredients as some branded options. Luvo Health’s page also warns that compounded medications are prepared by licensed pharmacies and are not reviewed by the FDA.

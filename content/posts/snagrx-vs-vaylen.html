SnagRx vs Vaylen is a choice between two cash-pay telehealth sellers of compounded GLP-1s. Early verdict: SnagRx is the lower annualized entry price for compounded semaglutide if you accept a 12-month plan and compounded drug sourcing; Vaylen offers more formats including oral semaglutide and availability in all 50 states but requires prepaying a year.

How pricing and billing compare

Both providers publish annualized low rates on 12-month plans and both sell compounded medications that are not FDA-approved finished products. SnagRx advertises compounded semaglutide from $69/month and tirzepatide from $119/month on a 12-month plan, with month-to-month pricing higher at $99 and $149. Vaylen lists semaglutide injections from $83.25/month, oral semaglutide at $99/month, and tirzepatide at $149/month, all as prepaid 12-month plan rates billed upfront.

Three distinctive differences to judge

1. Price level and price lock

SnagRx positions the annual plan as the bottom of market pricing for compounded GLP-1s, with a stated rate lock meaning the price does not rise as your dose escalates or at renewal. Vaylen also states the same price every month at every dosage level for its prepaid plans, but its headline semaglutide rate is higher at $83.25/month on the 12-month program. Both emphasize flat pricing during titration, but SnagRx highlights the lower $69 figure on the annual plan.

2. Medication formats and molecule options

Vaylen supports three formats: weekly semaglutide injections, daily oral semaglutide tablets, and weekly tirzepatide, all offered as compounded products. SnagRx focuses on compounded semaglutide and tirzepatide injections only. If a needle-free oral option matters, Vaylen includes it; SnagRx does not list an oral format in the supplied evidence.

3. Payment timing and refund policy

Vaylen requires prepaying a full year before clinician review but states a full refund if the clinician declines to prescribe for that billing cycle. SnagRx uses a more typical assessment-first flow where a clinician review occurs after intake and SnagRx states a full refund if the prescription is declined. Vaylen explicitly notes you pay before any clinician review; SnagRx indicates clinician review typically within five hours and always within 24.

4. Availability and clinician coverage

Vaylen states licensed clinicians in all 50 states. SnagRx names its clinician network as OpenLoop Health covering 48 states in the supplied evidence. Both claim LegitScript certification and clinician oversight, with SnagRx naming its four US 503A partner pharmacies and Vaylen stating fulfillment by licensed U.S. pharmacies.

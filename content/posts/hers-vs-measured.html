How they differ: FDA-approved vs compounded medications

Hers offers FDA-approved Wegovy® (brand-name semaglutide) and states it does not offer compounded medications. The provider notes that Wegovy® and Zepbound® are FDA-approved for weight loss and that Ozempic® is FDA approved for type 2 diabetes and may be prescribed off-label for weight loss at a clinician’s discretion. The company explicitly says compounded medications, when offered elsewhere, are not FDA-approved and are not reviewed by FDA for safety, effectiveness or quality.

Measured primarily offers compounded semaglutide and compounded tirzepatide. Measured’s materials repeat that compounded medications are not FDA-approved and are not reviewed by FDA for safety, effectiveness or quality.

Price points differ and include qualifiers. Measured lists promotional compounded semaglutide from $89/mo and compounded tirzepatide from $189/mo. Measured describes promotional pricing and notes that pricing may change.

Speed, support, and care model

Both providers emphasize ongoing support but in different ways. Hers advertises a personalized care team with ongoing dose check-ins and name-brand medication with no price markups. Measured highlights same-day prescription decisions, unlimited messaging, 24/7 care team access, and dietitian support. Measured also promotes free two-day shipping on every order.

Claims and evidence provided

Hers presents brand messaging noting weight changes such as 62 lbs lost in 12 months and 54 lbs lost in 6 months as captured on its landing page. Measured cites internal analysis of members reporting weight data at 6 months with an average loss of 31 lbs and includes a note that individual results vary and are not guaranteed. Measured also shows a 4.8 star Trustpilot rating from 51,000+ users in its materials. The comparison sources include regulatory cautions that compounded medications are not FDA-reviewed.

1. Price and value: clear gap in entry cost

Embody lists a substantially lower entry price, offering compounded semaglutide at $69/month and compounded tirzepatide at $119/month, noting the $69 price was $299 previously. FeelGood Meds shows compounded injections starting at $149/month, compounded tablets from $249/month, and a branded option shown from $1,999/month. Both providers state no insurance is required. Final pricing for Embody may vary based on dose and clinician recommendation and the $69 semaglutide pricing is presented as a limited time offer that may increase. FeelGood Meds asks patients to confirm eligibility, medication, total cost, renewal terms and state availability before enrollment.

2. Medication options: compounded only versus more formats

Embody emphasizes compounded semaglutide and compounded tirzepatide. Its materials note the medications are compounded and not FDA approved as finished products while using the same active ingredients as branded GLP-1s as stated on the site. FeelGood Meds lists compounded injections and compounded tablets and also displays a branded medication option. Where Embody focuses on a lean, prescription-forward model, FeelGood Meds highlights a broader set of formulation choices including a branded path at much higher posted pricing.

3. Delivery and logistics: Embody cites faster arrival

Embody’s captured copy states free shipping with delivery in 1–2 days after clinician approval and describes that as one of the faster delivery commitments in the GLP-1 telehealth space. FeelGood Meds notes free shipping in captured evidence but does not supply a specific delivery timeframe in the captured material. Both emphasize no insurance is required and both use licensed clinician review prior to prescribing.

4. Membership, cancellation, and terms

Embody repeatedly states no monthly membership, cancel anytime, and flat locked in monthly pricing for the stated prices, while also warning final pricing may vary based on dose and clinician recommendation and that the $69 price is a limited time offer. FeelGood Meds advertises no membership fee and a posted money back guarantee in the captured text, and it advises patients to confirm renewal terms, availability, and eligibility before enrollment. The comparison shows Embody markets a simpler, no-frills subscription model, whereas FeelGood Meds emphasizes choice and advises verification of terms.

5. Regulatory and safety notes

Both providers’ captured materials include the regulatory reminder that compounded medications are not FDA approved and are not reviewed by FDA for safety, effectiveness, or quality. Embody’s materials also state prescriptions are handled after a licensed provider review and that medication is filled at a licensed pharmacy. FeelGood Meds notes treatment is available only when a licensed clinician determines it is medically appropriate. The captured evidence does not provide additional safety performance data or clinical outcomes beyond patient anecdotes.

Final comparison notes and how to choose

Embody vs FeelGood Meds shows a trade off: Embody offers the lowest listed entry price for compounded semaglutide and fast 1–2 day shipping after approval, with a no membership, cancel anytime approach. FeelGood Meds starts higher for compounded injections, offers compounded tablets and a branded option at much higher posted pricing, and highlights a money back guarantee while advising patients to confirm terms and availability.

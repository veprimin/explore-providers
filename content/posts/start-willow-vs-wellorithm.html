Medication options and clinical model

Both platforms offer compounded alternatives rather than branded finished products. Start Willow focuses on compounded semaglutide described as a once weekly injection prescribed as part of a personalized plan. Wellorithm offers compounded semaglutide and compounded tirzepatide and uses a 2 minute quiz plus virtual consultation to determine medical appropriateness.

Key regulatory note

Both providers state that compounded medications are not FDA approved and are not reviewed by FDA for safety, effectiveness, or quality. Start Willow explicitly explains its compounded semaglutide is prepared by state licensed compounding pharmacies operating under state board of pharmacy oversight and USP compounding standards. Wellorithm similarly notes compounded medications are not FDA approved.

State coverage and clinician access

Wellorithm highlights licensed clinicians in all 50 states, which is its main strength for patients in states with limited telehealth options. Start Willow is a licensed telehealth platform but the source highlights its direct pay model and convenience rather than a 50 state clinician claim.

Wellorithm describes medication shipped to your home and fast, free shipping in marketing copy but does not state the same 3 day delivery guarantee.

Patient experience, guarantees, and limitations

Start Willow markets no insurance required and no membership fees and emphasizes a direct pay model to bypass insurance prior authorization. The source notes the initial price is $249 for the first month with $299/month ongoing and that the ongoing rate is on the higher end of the list. Wellorithm markets no membership or hidden fees and highlights a weight loss guarantee in landing page copy and promotional references to first month offers in captured text. Wellorithm also lists limited public reviews and unpublished final pricing as drawbacks noted in the source.

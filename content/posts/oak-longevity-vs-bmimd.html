3. Clinical access and review process

Oak Longevity operates with an asynchronous clinical review where patients complete a short online health questionnaire and a licensed physician reviews information without a required video call. Oak notes board certified physicians in your state review every case and same day approval is available for qualifying patients. bmiMD highlights 24/7 clinician access and online refills and delivery, and requires treatment only when a licensed clinician determines it is medically appropriate.

4. Commitments, refunds, and shipping

Oak emphasizes no subscriptions, no membership fees, free shipping, and free health coaching. Oak states you are not auto charged and choose to renew payment each month; three month prescriptions are billed upfront and no refunds are available after physician approval and once medication has shipped. bmiMD states free shipping on every subscription order and advertises a 100 percent refund if not eligible for the program, while noting the lowest monthly price requires longer prepaid commitments.

5. Regulatory status and suitability

Both providers offer compounded medications and both sources include the regulatory note that compounded medications are not FDA approved finished products and are not reviewed by the FDA for safety, effectiveness or quality. Oak includes specific suitability notes: not appropriate for patients under 18, pregnant, or nursing and patients with BMI below 20 do not qualify for GLP 1 treatment; another Oak note says not appropriate for BMI under 22 without comorbidities. bmiMD does not supply expanded eligibility details in the captured evidence beyond clinician determination and availability subject to the live offer.

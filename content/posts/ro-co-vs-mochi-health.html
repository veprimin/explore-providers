Medication type and regulatory status

Ro.co is the only platform in this comparison that offers access to the Wegovy pill, described as the first and only FDA-approved oral GLP-1 weight loss medication. The source states this is an FDA-approved finished medication rather than a compounded product. Mochi Health focuses on compounded semaglutide, compounded tirzepatide, and other compounded options. The supplied regulatory note for both providers states that compounded medications are not FDA-approved and are not reviewed by FDA for safety, effectiveness or quality.

Consultation experience: async review versus live video

Ro.co uses a telehealth model where treatment is available only when a licensed clinician determines it is medically appropriate after review. The captured evidence emphasizes clinician review and traditional telehealth intake. Mochi Health distinguishes itself by offering live video visits with a doctor bookable in about 5 minutes, which the source describes as fundamentally different from the asynchronous questionnaire review most competitors use. Mochi markets 24/7 support and ongoing appointment availability throughout the program.

Pricing structure and starting costs

Ro.co promotes a Get Started Online for Just $39 entry point. The source notes ongoing branded medication pricing varies and can be high depending on the plan, and that branded medications cost significantly more than compounded alternatives. Mochi Health operates a membership-plus-medication model with pricing from $99/month and a promotional 50% Off Your 1st Month with code BESTWLHALFOFF. The source also notes the $39 promotional rate applies to the first month only for new Mochi customers in some offers and that terms vary by state.

When compounded options matter

Mochi Health lists several compounded options with specific starting prices in the source: compounded semaglutide starting at $60, compounded tirzepatide starting at $90, and a compounded oral semaglutide pill starting at $62. The source also repeats the regulatory caution that compounded medications are not FDA-approved. Ro.co emphasizes its avoidance of compounded medication for the Wegovy pill offering, noting FDA-approved finished medication is not compounded.

Final comparison and recommendation

Ro.co vs Mochi Health breaks down to a trade-off between regulatory status and consultation style. Ro.co is best for patients who prioritize access to the FDA-approved Wegovy pill and prefer an injection-free, branded oral GLP-1 option with pricing disclosed before consultation. Mochi Health is best for patients who want synchronous live video visits with a doctor in about 5 minutes and lower-cost compounded medication alternatives under a membership-plus-medication model.

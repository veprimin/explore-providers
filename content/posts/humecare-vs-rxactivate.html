HumeCare+ vs RxActivate delivers two different approaches to clinician-led GLP-1 care. Early verdict: HumeCare+ is more transparent on starting prices and bundles muscle-preserving additions and body-composition scans, while RxActivate requires an eligibility quiz before revealing any pricing or plan details.

How the two providers compare at a glance

Both programs route treatment through licensed clinicians and note that treatment is available only when a clinician deems it medically appropriate. Key differences are pricing transparency, bundled supportive ingredients and scans, and whether plan costs are gated behind intake.

Primary distinctions to watch

  • Pricing disclosure: HumeCare+ publishes starting prices; RxActivate hides pricing behind a contact gate.
  • Bundled protocol: HumeCare+ pairs GLP-1 medication with glycine and B vitamins and body-composition scans; RxActivate describes clinician-matched plans without published bundled supplements.
  • Intake flow: RxActivate requires name, email and phone before showing plans or prices; HumeCare+ shows medication and protocol details and starting monthly costs.
  • Money-back messaging: HumeCare+ advertises a 10% or money-back promise over six months; RxActivate does not display comparable outcome guarantees in the supplied evidence.

Pricing transparency and access

HumeCare+ lists compounded semaglutide starting at $199 per month and a tirzepatide plus B12 plus glycine protocol from $299 per month. RxActivate displayed no price, medication fee, membership fee, shipping charge, cancellation term or billing commitment before requiring contact information. That makes HumeCare+ the clearer option if you need visible starting costs before sign-up.

Protocol contents and tracking

HumeCare+ sells a bundled GLP-1 protocol that includes physician-prescribed compounded semaglutide or tirzepatide, glycine, vitamin B12, and B6, plus monthly body-composition scans and an app. The company positions these additions as muscle-preserving and as side-effect support. RxActivate describes clinician-matched GLP-1 plans but the supplied evidence does not show bundled glycine or B vitamins or body-composition scans.

Eligibility flow, state availability, and lead capture

RxActivate uses a two-minute eligibility questionnaire that requests goal, height, weight, target weight, timeline, medication history, sex assigned at birth, date of birth, state and safety screening. A synthetic test confirmed Alabama as serviceable. The flow required first name, last name, email and phone before revealing any plan or price. HumeCare+ materials display landing-page details and pricing without the same contact-information gate.

If you prefer to confirm likely eligibility and costs only after minimal data entry, HumeCare+ provides visible starting prices; if you are willing to complete intake before seeing prices, RxActivate enforces that gate.

Safety, regulatory notes, and guarantees

Both providers emphasize clinician oversight and that treatment is available only when a licensed clinician deems it medically appropriate. HumeCare+ explicitly notes that compounded medications are not FDA-evaluated for safety, quality, or efficacy and also advertises a 10% or your-money-back promise over six months with eligibility and terms to verify. RxActivate’s supplied evidence notes that medication-specific approval status must be confirmed and that branded and compounded products are not interchangeable.

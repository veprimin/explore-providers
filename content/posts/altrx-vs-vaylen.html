Key comparison: altRx vs Vaylen pricing model and formats

Both providers sell compounded GLP-1 therapies and state that compounded medications are not FDA-approved. The most distinctive differences between altRx and Vaylen are price structure, medication formats, payment timing and refund policy, shipping speed and scope of included services. Below we compare those core areas directly using evidence supplied by each provider.

1. Price and billing structure

altRx advertises semaglutide starting at $89/month and tirzepatide at $149/month as a limited offer, with multi-month plans noted that reduce the monthly rate. altRx emphasizes no membership or hidden fees and a direct-pay model without a required prepaid year. Vaylen publishes lower annualized semaglutide pricing from $83.25/mo on a 12-month plan, but every headline rate is billed upfront as a single annual charge. Vaylen also shows higher month-to-month rates (for example $224/mo for semaglutide) for nonprepaid plans. If you need the lowest effective monthly rate only when you prepay a year, Vaylen’s 12-month rates are among the lowest; if you want to avoid prepaying, altRx positions itself as a pay-as-you-go option.

2. Medication formats and options

Vaylen lists three formats on one platform: weekly semaglutide injections (same active ingredient as Ozempic), daily oral semaglutide tablets, and weekly tirzepatide. Vaylen highlights that a clinician can switch formats without changing providers. altRx focuses on compounded semaglutide and compounded tirzepatide (GLP-1 and GLP-1/GIP) delivered in a complete treatment kit; evidence does not show an oral tablet option for altRx. If access to an oral, needle-free option matters, Vaylen documents that offering.

3. Payment timing, approvals and refund rules

Vaylen requires prepayment for its headline 12-month prices and states you pay before clinician review, with a published full refund for that billing cycle if the clinician declines to prescribe. altRx provides a free short quiz and a licensed provider review or telehealth consultation before prescriptions are sent; altRx emphasizes no commitment and the ability to cancel anytime. These represent opposite flows: Vaylen is pay-first then review with a refund-if-declined policy; altRx uses clinician review as part of the onboarding without a required prepaid year for the headline rates shown.

4. Shipping and turnaround

altRx advertises free, discreet 2-day delivery and typical processing and delivery time of about 4 to 5 business days after approval. Vaylen advertises free discreet shipping in 1 to 3 business days. Both state nationwide availability in all 50 U.S. states, subject to provider prescribing authority and state rules. Use these published delivery windows when timing matters for your start date.

5. What is included and clinician access

altRx includes 100% online medical visits, medication, syringes, prep pads, step-by-step guidance, and ongoing care team support; it highlights buy now pay later options and no membership fees. Vaylen includes telehealth consultations, an injection kit or tablet fulfillment, 24/7 care-team messaging, and a clinician review promised within 24 hours. Vaylen also notes LegitScript certification and licensed U.S. clinicians via OpenLoop Health in its statement. Both emphasize clinician oversight and that prescriptions are provided only when clinically appropriate.

Conclusion and recommendation for altRx vs Vaylen

Both companies offer compounded GLP-1 options and clinician oversight, and both state that compounded medications are not FDA-approved. Choose altRx if you want a low headline monthly price without a required prepaid year, 100% online medical visits, and the option to cancel anytime. Choose Vaylen if you prioritize the lowest annualized monthly rate on a prepaid 12-month plan, want access to an oral semaglutide tablet in addition to injectables, and accept paying before clinician review because Vaylen publishes a full refund if the clinician declines to prescribe.

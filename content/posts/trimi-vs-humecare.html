Trimi: semaglutide from $99/mo on the annual plan; tirzepatide $125/month; flat pricing that never rises as dose increases. HumeCare+: semaglutide from $199/mo (promotional pricing shown) and tirzepatide protocol from $299/mo. HumeCare+ notes prices reflect a promotional discount that may change. Support protocol: ingredients, tracking, and clinician access

Trimi focuses on medication access: compounded semaglutide and tirzepatide prescribed by US-licensed clinicians, with clinical oversight from Arora Health and a named medical director. HumeCare+ sells a bundled protocol: physician-prescribed compounded semaglutide or tirzepatide plus glycine, vitamin B12, and B6 intended for muscle support and side-effect relief, plus an app for tracking and unlimited clinician access available 24/7.

In short, Trimi emphasizes flat-price medication delivery and named clinical oversight, while HumeCare+ emphasizes a clinical program adding supportive ingredients, a connected app, and continuous clinician optimization.

Outcome guarantees and body-composition tracking

Trimi offers a 6-month money-back promise: follow the plan for 6 months and, if you don’t lose weight, you can request a refund subject to terms. HumeCare+ advertises a 10%-or-money-back promise over six months and says you should verify full terms and eligibility. HumeCare+ also provides monthly body-composition scans that track lean mass versus fat mass and reports internal headline results from member analyses; Trimi cites peer-reviewed clinical trials behind the active ingredients but notes compounded medications themselves are not FDA-approved.

Shipping, cancellations, and operations

Trimi: free overnight, temperature-controlled shipping is highlighted across its plans; medications are shipped from state-licensed compounding pharmacies named on the site and a certificate of analysis is available on request. HumeCare+: free next-day delivery and discreet shipping are offered, plus cancel subscription anytime language; HumeCare+ is cash-pay only and notes pricing shown reflects a promotional discount that may change.

Regulatory notes and what is and is not claimed

Both providers state that compounded medications are not FDA-approved and are not evaluated by FDA for safety, quality, or efficacy. Trimi says compounded medications are prepared per individual prescription and are not themselves FDA-approved as drugs. HumeCare+ similarly states compounded medications are not evaluated by the FDA and distinguishes them from branded, FDA-approved GLP-1 products.

Pricing and promotions compared

Directmeds advertises a promotional starting price of $147 and notes a Summer promotion saving $150. Its FAQ lists standard prices of $297/month for semaglutide and $399/month for tirzepatide. LTTL captured messaging highlights up to $140 off your first month with a code that is auto-applied and shows various captured price points such as $249 and $129 for 4 doses per month, while advising that users confirm current pricing and terms.

Check LTTL first-month credit details

Membership, cancellation and refund rules

Directmeds states no separate monthly membership fee, month-to-month cancellation for customers, and that subscribe-and-save plans require the stated term. Directmeds also advertises a full refund if a prescription is not approved. LTTL captured details caution that pricing, eligibility, pharmacy and renewal terms may change and recommend confirming eligibility, total cost, renewal terms and state availability before enrollment. No expanded LTTL FAQ content was captured.

Compounding, testing and regulatory notes

Both providers offer clinician-reviewed compounded GLP-1 medications and include the regulatory note that compounded medications are not FDA-approved. Directmeds specifies U.S.-based 503A compounding pharmacies. LTTL emphasizes state-licensed pharmacies with medication prepared and independently verified in FDA registered labs and states batch confirmation every 3 to 6 months to stay within plus or minus 10 percent of target strength according to captured details.

Shipping, delivery and availability

Directmeds advertises free temperature-controlled UPS or FedEx delivery generally in 1 to 2 days and free next-day shipping phrasing appears in captured landing-page evidence. LTTL states medications are delivered to your door when needed but specific transit times were not captured. Directmeds additionally notes it is not available in Mississippi or Louisiana; LTTL materials advise confirming state coverage before enrollment.

Final comparison and recommendation

Directmeds vs LTTL differ most on advertised pricing and promotions, membership and cancellation transparency, compounding verification language, and explicit shipping timing. Both state that treatment is available only when a licensed clinician determines it is medically appropriate and both note compounded medications are not FDA-approved.

Choose Directmeds if you want promoted starting pricing, explicit standard FAQ pricing disclosure, no separate monthly membership fee, month-to-month cancellation for standard plans, free temperature-controlled next-day shipping and an explicit note that a full refund is available if a prescription is not approved.

Choose LTTL if you prioritize an auto-applied up-to-$140 first-month credit and captured claims of independent lab verification and periodic batch confirmation from state-licensed pharmacies, and if you will confirm eligibility, price and renewal terms before enrolling.

JoinFridays vs TMates pit cost and medication choice against clinician availability. Quick verdict: JoinFridays is the lower-cost, flexible option with compounded tirzepatide and semaglutide starting at $117/month, while TMates charges a flat $199/month and emphasizes 24/7 U.S. doctor access.

How they differ at a glance

This comparison uses only supplied facts. The clearest differences are starting price and pricing model, available medications, clinician access and coverage, membership and cancellation terms, and the regulatory status of compounded meds. Below we compare those points directly.

1. Pricing model and starting cost

JoinFridays: Starting at $117/month with multi-month plans that reduce the effective monthly rate; standalone one-month plan listed as $249. TMates: $199/month flat at every dose, with plan tiers shown for multi-month options but a consistent dose price. TMates bills its semaglutide program as a flat $199/month with no step-up fees.

2. Medication options and formulations

JoinFridays offers both compounded tirzepatide and compounded semaglutide options on one platform and also lists name brand options with separate pricing. TMates focuses on clinically dosed compounded semaglutide. Both providers offer compounded medications; the sources note that compounded medications are not FDA-approved and are not reviewed by FDA for safety, effectiveness or quality.

3. Clinical access and oversight

TMates highlights 24/7 access to U.S.-based doctors across all 50 states and virtual check-ins. JoinFridays emphasizes personalized, clinician-guided care and clinician review to determine if treatment is medically appropriate, but the supplied evidence does not claim 24/7 U.S. doctor access.

4. Membership, cancellation, and shipping

JoinFridays advertises no membership fees and cancel anytime with no long-term contract and active promo codes. TMates advertises free shipping, cancel anytime, and licensed coverage in all 50 states. Both platforms present multi-month plan pricing options that change effective monthly rates.

5. Regulatory and sourcing notes

Both providers present compounded medications. The sources explicitly state that compounded medications are not FDA-approved and are not reviewed by FDA for safety, effectiveness or quality. TMates adds that its medications are formulated in U.S.-based facilities; JoinFridays notes it does not manufacture compounded medications and that actual product appearance and labeling may differ from website images.

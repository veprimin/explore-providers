Shed vs Pallas Health: headline differences at a glance

This direct comparison focuses on the most distinctive, supported differences: medication routes offered, coaching and support model, pricing structure and starting costs, clinician role and plan inclusions, and regulatory and shipping notes.

1. Medication options and how they are chosen

Shed offers both FDA-approved name-brand GLP-1s and compounded semaglutide or tirzepatide options on one platform, letting patients choose name-brand or compounded based on budget and preference. Pallas Health also provides brand-name Wegovy, Ozempic, Zepbound and Mounjaro plus compounded semaglutide and tirzepatide, but emphasizes clinician personalization: a US-licensed clinician reviews your history and chooses the path that is right for you.

2. Coaching and ongoing support

Shed integrates continuous coaching into treatment, pairing medication with ongoing behavioral support rather than issuing a prescription only. Pallas Health includes provider care, ongoing check-ins and care-team messaging inside one all-in plan price with no membership fee; care is asynchronous in most states and a video visit is required in some states.

3. Pricing structure and starting costs

Shed advertises compounded Semaglutide starting at $199/month and compounded Tirzepatide starting at $299/month and offers 10% off your first month. Shed does not publish standard pricing for all plans and provides a personalized quote after intake. Pallas Health lists compounded semaglutide at $139 for the first month, then $597 every 12 weeks (a $199/month average); compounded tirzepatide is $179 first month, then $897 every 12 weeks ($299/month average). Pallas’s compounded plans are priced by medication rather than by milligram, so titration does not raise plan price.

4. Clinician review and plan inclusions

Both platforms require a clinician review. Shed uses a 2-minute approval assessment to check eligibility, with a licensed provider confirming treatment before prescriptions are issued. Pallas emphasizes a US-licensed clinician personalizing the plan and includes provider care, check-ins and messaging inside the plan price; Pallas is cash-pay only and does not bill insurance.

5. Shipping, renewals, and regulatory notes

Pallas provides free expedited and discreet shipping on every plan and compounds medications in the USA via US-licensed compounding pharmacies. Shed’s materials note medication is shipped to your home after clinician approval and highlights that compounded medications are not FDA-approved and are prepared by a licensed pharmacy. Both providers include the regulatory qualifier that compounded medications are not FDA-approved and are not reviewed by FDA for safety, effectiveness or quality.

How they differ: personalization, pricing, and membership

Found and Oak Longevity both offer GLP-1 access in telehealth formats, but the evidence highlights three distinctive differences: Found centers on a biology-first personalized program and insurance integration, while Oak Longevity promotes one flat price for all dosages and no subscriptions. Found advertises flexible, affordable monthly plans and insurance handling; Oak advertises semaglutide from $133/month and tirzepatide from $199/month with free shipping and free health coaching.

1. Program model and clinical review

Found offers a personalized medically supervised program that evaluates hormones, blood sugar regulation, sleep, and behavior to tailor care. Oak Longevity operates with an online health questionnaire and asynchronous clinician review; Oak notes board-certified physicians review every case and no video call is required. Both state that prescriptions are subject to clinician review and clinical eligibility.

2. Pricing and membership structure

Found highlights “get started for as low as $39” and emphasizes “most insurance now accepted” and affordability with flexible pricing and cancel anytime options. Oak Longevity advertises semaglutide from $133/month and tirzepatide from $199/month, one flat price for all dosages, billed monthly on a non-recurring basis with no subscription or membership fees.

3. Medication format and regulatory notes

Both providers offer compounded GLP-1 medications. The evidence for each preserves the regulatory qualifier that compounded medications are not FDA-approved and are not reviewed by FDA for safety, effectiveness or quality. Found also references branded GLP-1s and notes some medications are FDA-approved for conditions other than weight and includes statements about insurance commitments for branded GLP-1s in certain plans. Oak emphasizes compounded semaglutide and tirzepatide and gives eligibility notes such as BMI thresholds.

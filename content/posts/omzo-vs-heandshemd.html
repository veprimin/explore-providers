Primary differences in Omzo vs He & She MD

This comparison uses the available evidence to highlight the most distinctive differences: pricing structure and terms, whether medications are compounded or branded prescriptions, bundled coaching and clinician access, formats and medication options, and shipping or renewal disclosures.

1. Pricing structure and promotional terms

Omzo lists semaglutide starting at $179 and tirzepatide starting at $279 as presented on its platform. He & She MD advertises semaglutide injections starting at $139/month and tirzepatide at $199/month as six-month introductory rates that renew higher. He & She MD specifically notes the intro rates apply to the first six months and that plans renew at a higher six-month price after that term. Omzo states starting prices but does not provide the same six-month promo detail in the captured evidence.

2. Compounded medications and regulatory notes

Both providers offer compounded GLP-1 options in the captured evidence and include regulatory disclosures. Omzo explains its Compounded Medication Program is prepared by a state-licensed sterile compounding pharmacy and notes those compounded drugs are not FDA-approved and have not been reviewed by the FDA for safety, effectiveness, or quality. He & She MD similarly states medications included in its GLP-1 Program are produced in USP-compliant, state-regulated pharmacies but are not reviewed by the FDA for safety, efficacy, or quality. Both preserve the regulatory qualifier that compounded products have not been FDA-reviewed.

3. Bundled support, clinician access and formats

Omzo frames care as clinician-guided plans reviewed by licensed U.S. providers and notes providers make the final determination on prescriptions. He & She MD bundles medication with an app that includes 1:1 clinician messaging, GLP-1-specific coaching, and progress tracking at no extra cost according to the captured evidence. He & She MD also lists three medication formats: semaglutide injection, oral semaglutide, and tirzepatide injection. Omzo references clinician-guided plans and the possibility of prescriptions for branded medications but emphasizes its compounded medication program.

Check Omzo medication options and clinician review

4. Shipping, membership and enrollment details

He & She MD states free expedited shipping, no membership fee, and that no insurance is needed; its landing evidence highlights free expedited shipping on every order and that medications ship straight to the door. Omzo mentions fast, discreet delivery and that medication costs are included in fees collected by Omzo, with pharmacies paid by Omzo, while also noting users are responsible for filling branded prescriptions if prescribed. Both sources advise checking eligibility with a clinician; Omzo explicitly notes clinicians determine eligibility and that Omzo is not a medical practice and does not create a doctor patient relationship.

He & She MD notes medications require consultation and prescription by a clinician through its platform and that not all customers will qualify.

Bottom line: Omzo vs He & She MD

Omzo centers on clinician-guided access to personalized compounded semaglutide and tirzepatide with listed starting prices and explicit clinician review, while He & She MD pairs compounded GLP-1 medication with a coaching app, 1:1 clinician messaging, and lower six-month introductory monthly rates that renew at higher prices. Both disclose that compounded medications are not FDA-approved and that clinician determination is required.

Shed vs Try Ageless: what differs most

This comparison focuses on the most distinctive supported differences: medication format and name-brand availability, coaching and ongoing support, entry price structure and discounts, format switching and plan flexibility, and regulatory notes about compounded medications. 1. Medication type and formats

Shed offers both FDA-approved name-brand GLP-1 and compounded options on one platform. Try Ageless sells only compounded semaglutide and tirzepatide formats: injections and needle-free tirzepatide oral drops. That makes Shed the platform where you can choose branded medications or compounded alternatives, while Try Ageless emphasizes three compounded formats including the uncommon oral drops.

2. Coaching and ongoing support

Shed integrates continuous coaching into treatment rather than only issuing a prescription. The platform positions coaching as part of the monthly plan to support titration and lifestyle changes. Try Ageless highlights clinician-prescribed plans and format switching under a plan but does not claim integrated continuous coaching as part of its core features in the supplied facts.

3. Price entry points and discounts

Try Ageless advertises the lowest entry price on its menu with tirzepatide oral drops from $159 per month on the yearly plan, semaglutide from $199/mo yearly, and tirzepatide injection from $249/mo yearly. Try Ageless states a built-in discount on every plan plus an extra code SLIM50 that stacks for $50 off. Shed lists compounded semaglutide starting at $199/month and compounded tirzepatide starting at $299/month and supplies a limited time 10% off your first month. Shed does not publish a standard price list and says patients receive a personalized quote after intake.

4. Format switching and plan flexibility

Try Ageless allows clinicians to switch you between semaglutide injection, tirzepatide injection, and tirzepatide drops under the same plan with no new consult fee and advertises cancel anytime flexibility. Shed offers both medication types on one platform and issues prescriptions only after a licensed clinician review. Shed emphasizes integrated coaching but notes coaching is bundled into the monthly cost rather than charged separately.

5. Regulatory and safety notes

Both providers supply compounded medications and include the regulatory qualifier that compounded medications are not FDA-approved and are not reviewed by FDA for safety, effectiveness, or quality. Try Ageless additionally notes that oral tirzepatide drops are an off-label compounded format because tirzepatide is not FDA-approved in oral form. Shed notes name-brand options provide regulatory assurance at higher cost while compounded options are not FDA-approved.

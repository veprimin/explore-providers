Quick verdict: Omzo and HealthRx both provide clinician-reviewed access to compounded semaglutide and tirzepatide, but they differ in price structure, how medications are delivered, and how costs are presented. Omzo lists semaglutide starting at $179 and emphasizes clinician-guided compounded programs with medication costs included in fees, while HealthRx advertises plans from $99/month on a 12-month prepaid semaglutide plan with overnight cold shipping.

How Omzo vs HealthRx approach pricing and plans

Omzo presents personalized compounded semaglutide with semaglutide starting at $179 and tirzepatide starting at $279, noting medication costs are included in fees collected by Omzo. HealthRx frames pricing as monthly plans, with semaglutide from $99/month on a 12-month prepaid plan ($1,163 today; $1,188 at renewal) and tirzepatide plans starting at $179/month. HealthRx states the lowest advertised monthly price requires a longer prepaid plan.

Three key differences: price model, shipping, and medication sourcing

1) Price model and what you pay for

Omzo: Starting prices are presented as single figures (semaglutide starting at $179; tirzepatide starting at $279) and Omzo states medication costs are included in fees Omzo collects and pays to pharmacies. HealthRx: Emphasizes monthly prepaid plans, for example $99/month on a 12-month semaglutide plan, and notes the lowest monthly price requires committing to a longer prepaid plan. HealthRx also highlights that the plan price does not change when your dose does and that no consultation fee or shipping charge is added on its plans.

Compare Omzo pricing options

2) Shipping and handling

Omzo promotes fast, discreet delivery as a feature. HealthRx specifies overnight cold shipping with cold chain, tracked, free on every plan. HealthRx explicitly markets cold chain shipping as part of its service and that shipments are tracked.

3) Medication sourcing and regulatory notes

Both providers offer compounded medication options and both include regulatory cautions. Omzo describes a Compounded Medication Program where medications are prepared and shipped by a state-licensed sterile compounding pharmacy and notes these compounded drugs are not FDA-approved and have not been reviewed by the FDA for safety, effectiveness, or quality. HealthRx states compounded medication is prepared in a licensed 503A pharmacy cleanroom and emphasizes that compounded medication is not FDA-approved and is prepared per prescription for an individual patient.

Clinical review, eligibility, and what is not supplied

Both services say licensed clinicians review files and determine whether semaglutide or tirzepatide is appropriate. Omzo notes clinical services are provided by third party networks of U.S.-licensed clinicians who determine eligibility and make the final prescription decision. HealthRx likewise says the clinician reviewing your file decides whether semaglutide or tirzepatide is the right fit, or whether neither is. Neither source supplied expanded FAQ answers, exact state-by-state availability, or independent clinical outcome data in the captured evidence.

How to choose between Omzo and HealthRx

Near-term cost focus: If your comparison centers on a lower advertised monthly rate with cold chain overnight shipping and a prepaid plan structure, HealthRx documents plans from $99/month on a 12-month prepaid semaglutide plan and tirzepatide plans starting at $179/month. If you prefer pricing framed as starting fees with medication costs included in the platform fees and a platform emphasis on clinician-guided compounded programs, Omzo lists semaglutide starting at $179 and tirzepatide starting at $279.

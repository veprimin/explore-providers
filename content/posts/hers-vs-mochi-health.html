Medication type: FDA-approved brand versus compounded formulations

In this pair, medication type is a primary divide. Hers advertises FDA-approved Wegovy which it states is not a compounded product. The provider emphasizes name brand medication and notes that compounded medications, when offered elsewhere, are not FDA-approved and are not reviewed by FDA for safety, effectiveness or quality. Mochi Health emphasizes compounded semaglutide and tirzepatide options, including a compounded once-weekly semaglutide injection and a compounded oral semaglutide pill. Mochi also repeats the regulatory note that compounded medications are not FDA-approved and are not reviewed by FDA for safety, effectiveness or quality.

Pricing and membership: starting costs and how you pay

Hers publishes starting pricing such as Wegovy pill from $149/mo and injectable from $199/mo and states that an active Hers Weight Loss Membership is required, with a promotional $39 first month that auto-renews at $149/month thereafter. The source notes total cost includes membership plus medication and that medication is not available without an active membership. Mochi Health reports membership-plus-medication pricing that starts as low as $99/month and advertises 50% Off Your 1st Month with Code BESTWLHALFOFF and a promotional first month $39 rate for new customers, after which the standard rate is $79 per month. Mochi states ongoing cost varies by plan and medication and that members continue care beyond their first month.

Clinical access and ongoing care: teams versus live video visits

Hers highlights a personalized care team with ongoing dose check-ins as part of its care model. Mochi Health differentiates itself by offering live video visits with a doctor in about 5 minutes, noting that most competitors use asynchronous text review. Mochi says the synchronous video visit allows a real-time conversation where a physician evaluates the case and explains the treatment plan directly, and offers 24/7 support plus ongoing appointment availability.

State availability and prescription conditions

Hers notes not available in all 50 states and that prescriptions are required and subject to clinician review and eligibility. Mochi states reliable GLP-1 prescription access in all 50 states and that a licensed physician evaluates cases during video consultations before any prescription is issued. Both sources include the regulatory reminder that compounded medications are not FDA-approved when relevant.

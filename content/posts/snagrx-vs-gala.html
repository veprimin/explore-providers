This comparison looks at SnagRx vs Gala across price transparency, clinician and pharmacy disclosure, shipping speed, bundled care, and regulatory notes. Both are direct-pay telehealth platforms offering compounded semaglutide and tirzepatide, no insurance required, and claim a single flat price at every dose. Below we compare the most distinctive differences supported by the sources.

SnagRx vs Gala: price transparency and structure

SnagRx posts explicit pricing: compounded semaglutide from $69/month and tirzepatide from $119/month on a 12-month plan; month-to-month rates are $99 and $149. The $69 and $119 figures are the 12-month plan rates and SnagRx states the rate is locked flat at every dose and at renewal. Gala promotes a flat all-inclusive price and a $220 promotion but the base monthly price was not visible in the captured page text; the page also advertises compounded semaglutide from $249/mo in a headline. Both claim no monthly membership and cancel anytime policies.

Clinician involvement and ongoing care

SnagRx uses a named clinician network, OpenLoop Health, and states clinicians will review the intake typically within five hours and always within 24; an OpenLoop clinician meets with individuals after checkout to determine eligibility and prescribe. Gala includes provider visit, prescription, and provider check-ins and dose monitoring as part of the service. Gala emphasizes ongoing check-ins in its bullets; SnagRx highlights clinician review speed and a clinician network by name.

Pharmacy naming, compounded status, and regulatory qualifiers

SnagRx names its four US 503A partner pharmacies and states compounding is used; it adds the regulatory note that compounded medications are not FDA-approved and are not reviewed by FDA for safety, effectiveness or quality. SnagRx also states its compounded medications are produced in FDA-regulated facilities but are not FDA-approved finished products. Gala likewise notes that compounded medications are not FDA-approved. Gala does not show named pharmacy partners in the captured text.

Shipping, timing, and refunds

SnagRx claims next-day UPS or FedEx shipping in temperature-controlled packaging, with a full refund if the prescription is declined. Gala claims free two-day shipping. Both state no insurance is required, no monthly membership, and cancel anytime terms in their marketing text.

Marketing claims and transparency differences

SnagRx emphasizes bottom-of-market annual pricing, the price lock at every dose, named pharmacies, and a stated clinician network; it also discloses that the advertised low rates require a 12-month commitment and that month-to-month rates are higher. Gala emphasizes a promotional discount (claimed $220 off) and bundled provider check-ins but the captured page did not show a clear, readable base monthly price. Conclusion: SnagRx vs Gala and final recommendation

SnagRx vs Gala comes down to pricing transparency and disclosed partnerships versus bundled provider check-ins and a promotional offer. SnagRx provides explicit 12-month plan prices ($69/mo semaglutide, $119/mo tirzepatide), names its US 503A pharmacies and clinician network, and promises next-day shipping and a clinician review timeline. Gala promises a single flat price at every dose, provider check-ins, and free two-day shipping but the base price was not exposed in the captured text and the page shows a promotional discount.

Choose SnagRx if you want clearly posted 12-month pricing, a named clinician network and named 503A partner pharmacies, and next-day shipping with a stated clinician review timeline. Choose Gala if you prioritize bundled provider check-ins and the promotional offer but confirm the base price, renewal terms, and state availability before enrolling.

Shed vs Omzo is a direct comparison between two telehealth weight loss platforms that both offer GLP-1 treatment options but differ in medication mix, coaching, and pricing. Early verdict: choose Shed if you want integrated continuous coaching and the option of name-brand GLP-1; choose Omzo if you prefer clinician-guided compounded plans with a lower starting price.

Shed vs Omzo: top differences at a glance

This comparison focuses on the most distinctive differences supported by the sources: medication types offered, coaching and care model, starting price and offers, clinician review and prescribing approach, and regulatory notes about compounded medications.

1. Medication mix: name-brand plus compounded vs primarily compounded

Shed uniquely offers both FDA-approved name-brand GLP-1 options and compounded alternatives on one platform. Omzo primarily connects patients to compounded semaglutide and tirzepatide programs and notes that if a branded medication is prescribed the patient must fill it at a pharmacy because Omzo does not sell or ship branded drugs. Both platforms state that compounded medications are not FDA-approved.

2. Coaching and care model

Shed integrates continuous coaching into treatment, branding itself as more than a prescription and positioning coaching as part of the bundled program. Omzo describes clinician-guided plans reviewed by licensed U.S. providers and emphasizes provider judgment for prescriptions, but captured evidence does not indicate continuous coaching is integrated the same way as Shed.

3. Pricing and promotions

Shed lists compounded semaglutide starting at $199/month and compounded tirzepatide starting at $299/month and advertises 10% off your first month. Omzo shows semaglutide starting at $179 and tirzepatide starting at $279. Shed also notes that standard pricing is not fully published and that patients receive personalized quotes after intake.

4. Clinician review and how prescriptions are handled

Both platforms require clinician review. Shed uses a 2-minute approval assessment and issues prescriptions only after a licensed clinician determines treatment is appropriate. Omzo connects users with licensed medical providers who determine eligibility and make the final prescribing decision; Omzo states its assessment does not create a doctor patient relationship and that clinical services are provided by third party clinician networks.

5. Regulatory notes and pharmacy model

Both sources explicitly state that compounded medications are not FDA-approved and are not reviewed by FDA for safety, effectiveness, or quality. Omzo adds that its compounded drugs are prepared by state licensed sterile compounding pharmacies and that it does not produce or manufacture medications. Shed similarly notes compounded medications are prepared by a licensed pharmacy and are not FDA-approved.

Conclusion: choosing between Shed and Omzo

In the Shed vs Omzo comparison, Shed stands out for offering both name-brand and compounded GLP-1 together plus integrated continuous coaching and a fast 2-minute assessment, along with a 10% first month offer. Omzo is positioned as clinician-guided compounded care with lower listed starting prices for semaglutide and a model where branded scripts must be filled separately by patients. Both require clinician review and note compounded medications are not FDA-approved.

Choose Shed if…

You want integrated continuous coaching alongside GLP-1 treatment and the option to choose between FDA-approved name-brand medications and compounded alternatives, and you value a fast 2-minute assessment plus a first month promotion.

Choose Omzo if…

You prefer clinician-guided compounded semaglutide or tirzepatide plans with lower advertised starting prices and want to confirm eligibility, pharmacy, state coverage, and renewal terms before enrolling.

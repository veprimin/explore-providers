How MedVi and Lttl differ: price, medication type, and verification

This comparison focuses on the most distinctive supported differences between MedVi and Lttl: promotional pricing structure, medication type and compounding, clinician review and support model, and pharmacy verification and dose confirmation.

Price and promotional offers

MedVi advertises a winter promotional first month starting at $179 including fast, free shipping, but notes a higher ongoing standalone month price ($299) and a three-month plan at $649. Lttl advertises up to $140 off your first month with a code auto-applied and lists pricing cues such as $249 and $129 in captured evidence. Both operate as direct-pay for compounded medications; specific ongoing pricing details for Lttl beyond the first-month credit were not published on the captured landing page.

Medication type and regulatory notes

MedVi provides clinically dosed compounded semaglutide and emphasizes semaglutide only, with explicit regulatory language that compounded medications are not FDA-approved and not reviewed by FDA for safety, effectiveness, or quality. Lttl offers compounded GLP-1 medications and includes the same regulatory note that compounded medications are not FDA-approved. Each provider makes claims about compounding; MedVi also contrasts compounded options with branded products but does not claim FDA approval for its compounded drugs.

Clinician review, support, and eligibility

MedVi describes a fully online intake with no clinic visits needed and 24/7 support from certified medical professionals; OpenLoop Health clinicians review cases after checkout and retain prescribing discretion. Lttl states treatment is available only when a licensed clinician determines it is medically appropriate and advises confirming eligibility, medication, total cost, renewal terms, and state availability before enrollment. Both require clinician review and both emphasize that prescriptions are subject to clinical determination.

Pharmacy standards and dose verification

MedVi says its compounded GLP-1s are produced in FDA-regulated facilities and that it partners with multiple USA certified pharmacies, while noting compounded medications are not FDA-approved. Lttl states its medications are compounded by U.S.A. licensed pharmacies, independently verified in FDA registered labs, and confirmed every 3 to 6 months to stay within plus or minus 10 percent of the target strength. The captured evidence for Lttl emphasizes independent verification and regular confirmation of target strength.

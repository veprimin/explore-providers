Key difference 1: Medication types and regulatory status

Pallas Health offers both FDA-approved brand-name GLP-1s such as Wegovy, Ozempic, Zepbound and Mounjaro, and clinician-personalized compounded semaglutide or tirzepatide prepared in the USA. The source notes that compounded medications are not FDA-approved and are not reviewed by FDA for safety, effectiveness or quality. Ro.co is the only mainstream platform on the list offering the Wegovy pill, the FDA-approved oral GLP-1, a finished medication rather than a compounded product. Ro emphasizes the regulatory assurance of an FDA-approved finished product.

Key difference 2: Pricing entry point and ongoing cost structure

Pallas lists compounded semaglutide at $139 for the first month then $597 every 12 weeks, a $199/month average, and compounded tirzepatide at $179 first month then $897 every 12 weeks, a $299/month average. Pallas also shows cash-pay pricing for brand-name pens (examples listed). Ro.co advertises you can get started online for just $39, with ongoing branded medication pricing varying by plan and generally higher than compounded alternatives. Ro states pricing is disclosed before you complete the consultation.

Key difference 3: Care model and platform experience

Pallas Health is 100 percent online and asynchronous in most states, with a US-licensed clinician reviewing health history to personalize a plan; a video visit is required in seven states plus Washington, D.C. Pallas includes clinician review, ongoing check-ins and care-team messaging in the plan price with no separate membership fee. Ro.co is described as an established full-service telehealth platform that prescribes FDA-approved medications after a licensed clinician review and highlights platform trust and transparent pricing before consultation.

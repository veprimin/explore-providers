How Sunlight vs TMates differ at a glance

This comparison uses only captured evidence from each provider. It compares five key differences: starting price and pricing model, medication types and formulation, clinician access and oversight, geographic and product availability, and regulatory notes about compounded or branded medications.

1. Price and pricing model

Sunlight: Personalized GLP-1 from $88/mo, promoted as “Same price forever” with no price increases ever and no hidden fees. Evidence includes marketing copy and landing page captures that list plans starting at $88 and a Summer Promo noting “As low as $88.” Sunlight highlights free 1-2 day shipping and statements like “No Monthly Membership” and “Cancel Anytime.”

TMates: Flat $199/month at every dose, no step-up fees as dosage escalates. TMates materials emphasize a straightforward flat-rate model so cost does not increase as dose increases. Other TMates pricing rows captured show month bundles at varying per month rates for longer commitments, but the core claim is $199/month flat for the program.

2. Medication types and formulation

Sunlight: Offers “injections & oral options” and “Personalized GLP-1” products. The source emphasizes prescription and telehealth included with no insurance required. It does not specify which branded or compounded products are used in the captured evidence. A regulatory note for Sunlight specifies that medication-specific approval status must be confirmed and that branded and compounded products are not interchangeable.

TMates: Focuses on clinically dosed compounded semaglutide formulated in U.S.-based facilities. TMates notes its semaglutide is compounded and that compounded medications are not FDA approved as finished products. TMates also states it does not offer branded Ozempic, Wegovy, or Zepbound, and highlights the flexibility of compounded dosing.

3. Clinician access and program oversight

Sunlight: Advertises 100% online medical visit with telehealth and clinician involvement captured in landing-page evidence. Claims include clinician reviews and customer reports of clinician dose adjustments, but the source advises users to confirm eligibility, medication, total cost, renewal terms and state availability before enrollment.

TMates: Promotes 24/7 access to U.S.-based doctors and virtual check-in appointments for ongoing medical oversight. The captured FAQ emphasizes actual physician availability not just messaging, and licensed coverage in all 50 states.

4. Geographic availability and shipping

Sunlight: Landing evidence states free shipping in 1-2 days and free discreet 2-day shipping in promotional copy. Users are still advised to confirm state availability and eligibility prior to enrollment.

TMates: States licensed coverage in all 50 states and that it ships to most states, subject to medical eligibility. TMates emphasizes U.S. formulation of medications and domestic sourcing as part of its quality messaging.

5. Regulatory and product notes

Sunlight: The captured content includes a regulatory note that medication-specific approval status must be confirmed and that branded and compounded products are not interchangeable.

TMates: Explicitly notes that compounded medications are not FDA approved and are not reviewed by FDA for safety, effectiveness or quality. TMates frames compounded semaglutide as giving more dosage flexibility and access at a lower price point versus branded finished products.

How Refills and TMates differ at a glance

This comparison focuses on five distinctive differences supported by the sources: price and flat-rate structure, clinician access and supervision, available formulations and drugs, regulatory and compounded medication notes, and shipping, cancellation, and membership terms. Both providers use compounded medications, which the sources note are not FDA-approved finished products.

1. Price and flat pricing model

Price is the clearest split. Refills advertises personalized compounded GLP-1 from $99/month (~$5/day) with flat pricing the same at every dosage and specific pricing examples such as semaglutide at $99/mo and tirzepatide at $159/mo. TMates advertises a flat $199/month, the same price at every dose with no step-up fees. The comparison is direct: Refills positions itself as a lower entry price, while TMates positions itself as a higher-cost flat program.

2. Clinician access and ongoing supervision

TMates emphasizes continuous physician availability: 24/7 access to U.S.-based doctors and virtual check-in appointments for ongoing oversight. Refills offers licensed clinician review after the intake quiz, clinician chats or prescription within 24 hours in many cases, and unlimited access to a patient care team and clinicians for questions at no extra cost. The difference is one of emphasis: TMates highlights 24/7 U.S.-based doctors, while Refills highlights on-demand clinician review and unlimited clinician consultations bundled into a monthly price.

3. Formulations and medications offered

Refills lists multiple options including semaglutide and tirzepatide and also notes a GIP plus GLP-1 dual agonist may be available. Refills states tirzepatide is priced at $159/mo in its examples. TMates centers on compounded semaglutide and highlights clinically dosed compounded semaglutide formulated in U.S. facilities; TMates mentions tirzepatide publicly but with limited detail and the site emphasizes semaglutide as its primary program. If choice of drug formulation matters, Refills documents both semaglutide and tirzepatide pricing details while TMates focuses on semaglutide with clinical dosing and domestic formulation.

4. Regulatory note and compounded medication status

Both sources include the same regulatory qualifier: compounded drug products are not approved or evaluated for safety, effectiveness, or quality by the FDA. Refills explicitly states compounded medications are highly regulated and compounding pharmacies are licensed and inspected, but reiterates the FDA has not evaluated those products. TMates likewise states its medication is compounded, not an FDA-approved finished product, while noting it uses the same active ingredient as branded options.

5. Shipping, membership, cancellation, and availability

Refills promotes free discreet shipping, express shipping options, availability in all 50 states, no monthly membership or app fees, month-to-month plans with the ability to cancel anytime and no cancellation fees. TMates advertises free shipping, cancel anytime, shipping to most or all states with availability based on medical eligibility, and a direct-pay flat model. Both note prescriptions are required and availability depends on medical eligibility or clinician review.

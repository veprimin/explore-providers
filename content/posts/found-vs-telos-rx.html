How the approaches differ: biology-first versus medication-first

Found frames itself as a “biology-first” program that builds a personalized medically supervised weight-loss plan around hormones, blood sugar regulation, sleep, and behavior. Telos Rx presents a medication-centric offering: compounded semaglutide and tirzepatide prescribed online after review by a US-licensed provider, emphasizing GLP-1 and GLP-1 + GIP options. Both note that compounded medications are not FDA-approved; Found explicitly calls out compounded semaglutide and tirzepatide and includes regulatory language that they are not FDA-approved and not available in all states. Telos Rx also states compounded medications are not FDA-approved and that treatment is available only when a licensed clinician determines it is medically appropriate.

Clinical review and eligibility

Both platforms require clinician review. Found says Rx is subject to provider review and clinical eligibility; Telos Rx similarly requires approval by a US-licensed provider and notes pay $0 if not approved in some captured messaging. Telos Rx emphasizes that a licensed provider decides whether semaglutide or tirzepatide is right for the patient, while Found emphasizes a broader medical evaluation that informs a personalized plan.

Pricing and insurance: starting costs and coverage differences

Found lists “get started for as low as $39” and states most insurance is now accepted, with Found handling insurance paperwork and noting specific programs like a Medicare GLP-1 Bridge Program in captured landing-page evidence. Found also provides multiple starting figures across plan types in the captured evidence. Telos Rx lists semaglutide as low as $99 per month and tirzepatide as low as $139 per month, and includes statements like “as low as $99 per month” and “pay $0 if not approved” in captured messaging. Telos Rx warns that pricing, eligibility, pharmacy, state coverage, and renewal terms may change and that your price depends on your plan.

Medications offered and compounded status

Both providers offer GLP-1 class medications and reference compounded semaglutide and tirzepatide. Found lists tirzepatide and semaglutide and notes brand-name options in captured offers while preserving the regulatory note that compounded GLP-1s are not FDA-approved or evaluated for safety or efficacy and not available in all states. Telos Rx specifies semaglutide acts on GLP-1 and tirzepatide acts on GLP-1 plus GIP, and that both are compounded and prescription-only.

Quick verdict: HumeCare+ vs LTTL

The HumeCare+ vs LTTL choice comes down to protocol depth versus straightforward compounded dosing. HumeCare+ bundles physician-prescribed semaglutide from $199/month or tirzepatide protocols from $299/month with glycine, B6 and B12 plus monthly body composition scans and a 10% or your money back promise over six months. LTTL emphasizes full-dose compounded GLP-1 delivered from U.S. licensed pharmacies with pricing offers including up to $140 off your first month. Compounded medications are not FDA-approved and are not reviewed by FDA for safety, effectiveness or quality.

Price and promotion comparison

HumeCare+ lists semaglutide starting at $199/month and a tirzepatide protocol from $299/month. LTTL shows promotional pricing with up to $140 off your first month and references prices such as $249 and $129 in captured materials. HumeCare+ notes pricing shown reflects a promotional discount that may change. LTTL’s landing capture emphasizes an auto-applied code for first-month savings.

Protocol components and support

HumeCare+ sells a bundled protocol not just a prescription. Its protocol pairs physician-prescribed compounded semaglutide or tirzepatide with glycine and vitamins B6 and B12, a dedicated app, and continuous clinician access. HumeCare+ positions glycine and B vitamins as muscle-preserving and side-effect support built into the formulation. LTTL focuses on delivering full-dose compounded GLP-1 medications intended to help regulate appetite and fullness. LTTL materials captured do not document added supportive ingredients or an app in the same way.

Distinctive difference 1: muscle support and tracking

HumeCare+ includes monthly body composition scans that track lean mass versus fat, and it states it tracks lean mass not just weight. LTTL’s captured materials do not show an equivalent body-composition tracking feature.

Distinctive difference 2: money-back promise and member claims

HumeCare+ advertises a 10% or your money back promise over six months with terms to verify eligibility. HumeCare+ also cites internal analysis of member results. LTTL’s captured materials do not show a comparable refund promise in the captured content.

Distinctive difference 3: pharmacy verification and dosing consistency

LTTL states medications are compounded by U.S. licensed pharmacies and prepared for delivery with independent verification and confirmations every 3 to 6 months to stay within plus or minus 10 percent of target strength. HumeCare+ also offers compounded medications dispensed from U.S. pharmacies but includes a regulatory note that compounded medications are not FDA-evaluated for safety, quality, or efficacy.

Clinician access, delivery, and logistics

HumeCare+ emphasizes unlimited clinician access, a U.S. care team available 24/7, free next-day delivery, and cancel anytime terms in captured content. LTTL highlights delivery from state-licensed pharmacies and emphasizes on time delivery and independent lab verification in captured content. Both require a licensed clinician to determine medical appropriateness and both use compounded medication language that notes the lack of FDA review.

Which to choose: HumeCare+ vs LTTL

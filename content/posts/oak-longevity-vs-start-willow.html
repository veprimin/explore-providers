How these providers differ at a glance

This comparison focuses on the most distinctive supported differences: starting and ongoing price, shipping speed and delivery guarantees, clinical model and personalization, subscription or billing structure, and the regulatory status of compounded medications. Oak Longevity and Start Willow both offer compounded GLP-1 medications; all regulatory qualifiers below reflect each provider’s stated details.

1. Price and billing structure

Oak Longevity advertises semaglutide starting at $133/month and tirzepatide from $199/month, with a one-price covers all dosages approach and no subscription or membership fees. Oak bills monthly on a non-recurring basis and notes three-month prescriptions are billed upfront; Oak also states no refunds are available after physician approval and once medication has shipped. Start Willow lists semaglutide from $249 for the first month and $299/month ongoing, with no membership fees but a stated ongoing rate increase to $299/month.

2. Shipping speed and delivery commitments

Oak Longevity includes free shipping with tracking and says most patients receive medication within a few days of approval, with same-day approval available for qualifying patients. Start Willow guarantees delivery in 3 days or less from its licensed pharmacy network and advertises same-day prescriptions and free 2-Day shipping when in stock. If delivery speed and a formal guarantee matter, Start Willow explicitly commits to a 3-day delivery window.

3. Clinical model and personalization

Oak Longevity operates with an asynchronous clinical review: patients complete an online questionnaire and a licensed clinician reviews the case; Oak states no video call and no insurance is required. Oak also offers free health coaching included with every plan. Start Willow describes a personalized treatment plan created in collaboration with a Willow doctor and emphasizes clinically guided personalization; it also uses a direct-pay model that bypasses insurance pre-authorization. Both providers require a clinician to determine medical appropriateness.

4. Compounded medication status and eligibility notes

Both Oak Longevity and Start Willow offer compounded versions of semaglutide and tirzepatide and state that compounded medications are not FDA-approved finished products and are not reviewed by the FDA for safety, effectiveness, or quality. Oak additionally notes patients with BMI below 20 do not qualify for GLP-1 treatment and that treatment is available only when a licensed clinician determines it is medically appropriate. Start Willow notes compounded semaglutide is prepared by state-licensed compounding pharmacies operating under state board of pharmacy oversight and meeting USP standards.

How SkinnyRx vs Vaylen differ at a glance

This comparison focuses on five distinctive differences supported by the source: pricing model and headline rates, medication formats and molecule options, payment and refund policies, shipping and fulfillment speed, and regulatory and testing notes.

Pricing model and headline rates

SkinnyRx lists oral semaglutide tablets starting at as low as $249/mo. Vaylen publishes annualized prepaid prices that lower monthly cost to $83.25/mo for semaglutide injections on a 12 month plan and $99/mo for oral semaglutide on a 12 month plan. Vaylen also publishes month to month rates such as $224/mo for injectable semaglutide and $249/mo for oral semaglutide.

Medication formats and molecule options

SkinnyRx centers on daily oral semaglutide tablets and positions that format as an alternative for people who prefer not to use injections. Vaylen offers three formats and two molecules on one platform: weekly semaglutide injections, daily oral semaglutide tablets, and weekly tirzepatide injections. Vaylen notes clinicians can switch formats without changing providers.

Payment, review and refund differences

SkinnyRx operates on a prescription-after-online-consultation model where a provider completes prescription review before fulfillment; its cancellation policy allows full refund any time before a licensed provider completes the prescription review. Vaylen requires prepaying the 12 month plan before clinician review but states a full refund is available for that billing cycle if the clinician declines to prescribe. Vaylen also discloses no published terms for unused portion of a prepaid year if you cancel mid-treatment.

Shipping and fulfillment speed

SkinnyRx advertises free overnight shipping on all orders and promises shipping and tracking information within 2 business days after the prescription is written. Vaylen advertises free discreet shipping in 1 to 3 business days.

Regulatory and quality notes

Both providers offer compounded medications and include regulatory language that compounded drugs are not FDA approved or reviewed for safety, effectiveness, or quality. SkinnyRx adds that partner pharmacies perform third party testing every 3 to 6 months, confirming concentration within plus or minus 10 percent of the active ingredient. Vaylen also emphasizes that its medications are compounded and not FDA approved.

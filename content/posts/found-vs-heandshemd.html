Found vs He & She MD: key differences at a glance

This direct comparison focuses on the most distinctive differences supported by the sources: pricing and introductory structures, personalization and clinical approach, bundled coaching and clinician access, medication formats and regulatory notes, and insurance and shipping policies. 1) Pricing structure and introductory rates

Found: Pricing is described as affordable monthly plans with “get started for as low as $39” and membership tiers noted (Found membership starts at $49/mo when billed annually) and various advertised monthly figures. Found advertises specific promotional offers and notes pricing via insurance with 12 month commitments in some cases.

He & She MD: Headline prices are six-month introductory rates. Compounded semaglutide injections start at $139/month for the first six months (regularly $199/month), oral semaglutide and tirzepatide injections start at $199/month for the first six months. The site emphasizes that promo rates apply only to the initial six-month term and plans renew at higher six-month prices.

2) Personalization and clinical approach

Found: Positions itself as a biology-first, personalized medically supervised weight loss program that builds plans around individual metabolic biology, including hormones, blood sugar regulation, sleep, and behavioral factors. Rx is subject to provider review and clinical eligibility.

He & She MD: Focuses on a two-part promise: medication plus support. Plans include clinician consultation and prescription through the platform, but the emphasis in the source is on bundling medication with an app that provides clinician messaging and GLP-1-specific coaching rather than a broader biology-first evaluation.

3) Bundled coaching, clinician access, and app features

Found: Emphasizes holistic whole-body approach and personalized programs; captured copy highlights medically supervised programming, but a dedicated coaching app with 1:1 messaging is not described in the captured evidence.

He & She MD: Every plan bundles the He & She MD app with 1:1 clinician messaging, GLP-1 coaching and progress tracking at no extra cost. The source explicitly markets coaching and clinician access as built in, with no separate membership fee.

4) Medication formats and regulatory notes

Found: Lists tirzepatide and semaglutide, including compounded versions. The source repeatedly qualifies that certain medications are FDA-approved for conditions other than weight and that compounded GLP-1s are not FDA-approved or evaluated for safety or efficacy and may not be available in all states.

He & She MD: Offers semaglutide injection, oral semaglutide, and tirzepatide injection. The medication programs are sold as compounded GLP-1s produced in USP-compliant, state-regulated pharmacies but the source states these medications are not reviewed by the FDA for safety, efficacy, or quality.

5) Insurance, shipping, and membership logistics

Found: Notes that most insurance is now accepted and that Found handles insurance paperwork, with claims about Medicare GLP-1 Bridge Program access for eligible Part D members and various membership pricing details. Many pricing statements are subject to insurance approval and clinical eligibility and include qualifiers about copays and deductibles.

He & She MD: States there is no insurance needed, free expedited shipping on every order, and no membership fee. Pricing structure is sold as six-month terms with promo pricing that renews at higher rates.

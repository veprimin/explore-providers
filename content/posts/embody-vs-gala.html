This direct comparison, Embody vs Gala, contrasts what each platform emphasizes: Embody highlights a lowest-entry price and rapid free shipping, while Gala highlights provider check-ins, a flat price promise, and a $220 promotion. Both supply compounded semaglutide and tirzepatide via telehealth, and both note that compounded medications are not FDA approved.

Top differences at a glance

The most distinctive supported differences between Embody and Gala are: price and visible entry cost, delivery timing and shipping promise, inclusion of provider check-ins and monitoring, transparency of base price on the landing page, and promotional messaging or claims about flat pricing.

Price transparency and entry cost

Embody lists semaglutide at $69/month and tirzepatide at $119/month and explicitly markets that $69 semaglutide price as cut from $299. Gala advertises a promotion and claims a flat all-inclusive price and a $220 discount, but the base dollar price was not visible in captured page text. If immediate visible low cost matters, Embody supplies a concrete starting price while Gala requires verifying the final price and promotion details.

Provider check-ins and ongoing care

Gala specifically highlights provider check-ins and dose monitoring included along with the provider visit and prescription. Embody describes prescription and telemed visits included and 24/7 support access for dosing and side effect questions, but positions itself as a lean, no-frills program without coaching or advanced monitoring. Choose Gala if ongoing scheduled check-ins are a deciding factor; choose Embody if you prefer lower price with clinician review and on-demand support.

Shipping speed and delivery promises

Embody promises free shipping and delivery in 1 to 2 days after clinician approval. Gala also advertises free two day shipping. Both platforms emphasize fast home delivery, but Embody repeatedly calls out 1 to 2 day arrival while Gala frames delivery as free two day shipping.

Compounded medication and regulatory note

Both sources make the regulatory point that compounded medications are not FDA approved and are not reviewed by FDA for safety, effectiveness or quality. Embody states its offerings are compounded and not FDA approved; Gala repeats the same regulatory note. Both require a licensed clinician determining medical appropriateness.

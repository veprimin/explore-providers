He & She MD vs Luvo Health centers on three clear differences: transparent intro pricing and renewal structure, bundled clinician coaching, and how much public pricing and terms are verifiable. Quick verdict: He & She MD provides explicit six-month introductory prices and a coaching app with 1:1 clinician messaging; Luvo Health’s captured landing page did not verify current pricing, renewal, or state-availability details so prospective patients should confirm those items before enrolling.

See He & She MD pricing and coaching

Pricing transparency and billing terms

He & She MD lists specific introductory rates: compounded semaglutide injections start at $139/month and oral semaglutide or tirzepatide injections start at $199/month, each described as six-month introductory rates that renew higher. The source emphasizes that those headline prices are promotional and that plans renew at a higher regular six-month price after the first term. By contrast, Luvo Health’s captured landing-page evidence did not publish or verify current pricing, renewal terms, or state coverage; the advice in Luvo’s material is to confirm eligibility, total cost, renewal terms and state availability before enrollment.

Support model and bundled services

He & She MD bundles a coaching app that includes 1:1 clinician messaging, GLP-1-specific coaching, and progress tracking at no extra cost, and it markets free expedited shipping with no membership fee. Luvo Health indicates ongoing support and dose changes and says dose adjustments are based on clinical response and that treatment is available only when a licensed clinician determines it is medically appropriate, but captured details about what support is bundled or costed were not published or verified on the captured page.

Medication formats and regulatory notes

Both providers offer semaglutide and tirzepatide in multiple formats according to the captured evidence, but the sources emphasize different specifics. He & She MD advertises semaglutide injection, oral semaglutide, and tirzepatide injection as offered formats. Luvo Health lists tirzepatide injection, semaglutide injection, and semaglutide tablets and also mentions microdosing options in captured content. Both sources include the regulatory note that compounded medications are not FDA-approved and are not reviewed by the FDA for safety, effectiveness or quality; He & She MD explicitly states that its compounded GLP-1s are produced in USP-compliant, state-regulated pharmacies but are not reviewed by the FDA.

State availability, eligibility and verification

He & She MD notes that medications require consultation and prescription by a clinician through its platform and that not all customers will qualify for a prescription. The captured Luvo Health evidence repeatedly cautions that treatment is available only when a licensed clinician determines it is medically appropriate and that pricing, eligibility, pharmacy, state coverage and renewal terms may change; the captured landing page did not verify those items, so the source recommends confirming them before enrolling.

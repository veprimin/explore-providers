Shed pairs medications with continuous coaching and a 2-minute approval assessment; Regenics positions itself as a straightforward, nurse-practitioner–led telehealth option with compounded semaglutide starting at $139 first month.

How the two providers differ at a glance

This comparison highlights five supported differences: medication types offered, coaching and clinical model, pricing and promotional structure, shipping and pharmacy notes, and assessment or approval process. All statements preserve source qualifiers about compounded medications and clinician review.

1. Medication options: name-brand plus compounded vs compounded only

Shed offers both FDA-approved name-brand GLP-1 medications and compounded alternatives on one platform, letting patients choose based on budget and preference. The source emphasizes that name-brand options carry higher costs but provide regulatory assurance, while compounded options are more affordable. Regenics offers compounded semaglutide and tirzepatide prepared by U.S. licensed compounding pharmacies and also markets an injectable peptide, Sermorelin. Both providers note that compounded medications are not FDA-approved as finished products and are prepared by licensed pharmacies based on a provider’s prescription.

2. Care model and coaching vs nurse-practitioner oversight

Shed integrates continuous coaching into treatment rather than simply issuing a prescription, positioning coaching as part of the bundled monthly cost and supporting titration and lifestyle changes. Regenics delivers care under licensed nurse practitioners who review intake and oversee prescriptions; the platform is described as nurse-practitioner-led and straightforward for cash-pay entry. These are distinct approaches: Shed emphasizes behavioral support alongside medication, while Regenics emphasizes clinician oversight by NPs and a simpler telehealth workflow.

3. Pricing and promotions: starting prices and first-month deals

Shed lists compounded semaglutide starting at $199/month and compounded tirzepatide starting at $299/month, and it advertises a limited-time 10% off your first month. Shed does not publish a single standard price and provides personalized quotes after intake; coaching is bundled into the monthly cost. Regenics promotes promotional first-month pricing: semaglutide $139 first month (renewing $199/mo) and tirzepatide $189 first month (renewing $279/mo). Regenics’ promotional pricing applies to the first month only, with renewals at higher rates according to the source.

4. Shipping, pharmacy network, and extras

Regenics states medications are compounded in the USA, shipped free, and its pharmacy network is LegitScript certified. Shed’s captured landing-page details list starting prices and the 10% first-month offer but do not duplicate Regenics’ free-shipping claim; both stress that compounded medications are not FDA-approved and require a licensed provider’s prescription. Regenics also offers Sermorelin peptide therapy as an additional product line; Shed highlights the option to explore a Shed Membership.

5. Assessment and approval timing

Shed promotes a 2-minute approval assessment to check eligibility quickly, followed by clinician review and a prescription only when a licensed provider determines treatment is appropriate. Regenics requires intake review by a licensed nurse practitioner before prescribing. Both platforms make clear treatment is available only when a licensed clinician determines it is medically appropriate.

Mochi Health and Luvo Health both offer online weight-management care, but they differ most on clinical model, pricing transparency, medication approach, and published availability. Early verdict: Mochi Health emphasizes synchronous live video visits with doctors and a membership-plus-medication pricing model starting from $99/month; Luvo Health’s specific pricing and eligibility details were not published or could not be verified on the captured landing page.

Mochi Health vs Luvo Health: top differences at a glance

This comparison focuses on the most distinctive supported differences: clinical visit model, pricing and membership structure, medication types and compounded status, state coverage and verification, and how fast you can see a clinician.

1. Clinical visit model and patient experience

Mochi Health: Mochi distinguishes itself by offering live video visits with a licensed physician bookable in about 5 minutes, enabling a real-time conversation where a doctor evaluates your case and explains your treatment plan directly. The source calls this model fundamentally different from the asynchronous text intake used by most competitors.

Luvo Health: The captured Luvo landing page emphasizes clinician-reviewed online treatment and ongoing support and dose changes, but current details about its exact intake workflow were not published or could not be verified on the captured affiliate landing page.

2. Pricing model and transparency

Mochi Health: Pricing is membership plus medication, with new-patient promotions such as 50% off the first month via code BESTWLHALFOFF and standard pricing that can start from $99/month. The site notes the $39 promotional rate applies to the first month only for new customers and that ongoing pricing varies by plan and medication type.

Luvo Health: The captured information did not verify specific starting prices. The Luvo landing page lists month 1, month 2-3, and 3+ month columns and mentions cancel anytime and dose adjustments based on clinical response, but pricing and renewal terms may change and were not published or could not be verified on the captured page.

3. Medication types, compounded status, and regulatory notes

Mochi Health: Mochi lists compounded semaglutide and tirzepatide options with promotional starting amounts (compounded semaglutide starting $99 $60 and compounded tirzepatide starting $199 $90 shown on the captured landing page). The source also includes a regulatory note that compounded medications are not FDA-approved and are not reviewed by FDA for safety, effectiveness or quality.

Luvo Health: The Luvo content notes availability of tirzepatide injection, semaglutide injections and tablets, microdosing options, and reiterates that compounded medications, when offered, are prepared by licensed pharmacies and are not reviewed or approved by the FDA. Specific pricing and which formulations are offered were not verified on the captured affiliate page.

4. State coverage, eligibility and support

Mochi Health: The source states Mochi offers reliable GLP-1 prescription access in all 50 states, plus 24/7 support and ongoing appointment availability throughout the program. A licensed physician evaluates your case during the video consultation before any prescription is issued.

Luvo Health: The captured Luvo content warns that pricing, eligibility, pharmacy, state coverage and renewal terms may change and that treatment is available only when a licensed clinician determines it is medically appropriate. The landing page did not publish or verify full state coverage details on capture.

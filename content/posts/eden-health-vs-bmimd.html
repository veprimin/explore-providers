Quick verdict: Eden Health vs bmiMD for GLP-1 and tirzepatide plans

The comparison Eden Health vs bmiMD shows Eden Health lists clinician-guided semaglutide starting at $99 and tirzepatide at $129 with a lifetime price guarantee and free expedited shipping, while bmiMD lists compounded tirzepatide with tiered monthly pricing from $179 month-to-month down to $139 for a 12-month plan and free shipping.

How they differ: price model, medication type, and commitments

This comparison focuses on the three most distinctive differences supported by the sources: pricing and guarantees, whether the product is compounded, and program structure and clinician access.

1) Pricing structure and guarantees

Eden Health publishes fixed monthly prices by medication: Semaglutide for $99 and Tirzepatide for $129, with repeated statements that there is one monthly price guaranteed for life. The source emphasizes no insurance required, no membership fees, and free expedited shipping. bmiMD lists multiple billing options for tirzepatide: $179 month-to-month, $159 for a three-month plan, $149 for a six-month plan, and $139 for a 12-month plan, with the 12-month option delivering the lowest monthly equivalent. bmiMD also advertises free shipping on every subscription order and a Best Price Guaranteed statement for those plans.

2) Medication type and regulatory notes

Eden Health describes semaglutide and tirzepatide as GLP-1 medications and notes treatment is available only when a licensed clinician determines it is medically appropriate. The Eden source includes a regulatory caution that compounded medications, when offered, are not FDA-approved and are not reviewed by FDA for safety, effectiveness or quality. bmiMD explicitly offers compounded tirzepatide and repeats the regulatory note that compounded medications are not FDA-approved and are not reviewed by FDA for safety, effectiveness or quality.

3) Program features and clinician access

Eden frames its offering as a guided GLP-1 program with medication, care, and support combined under a single monthly price, and notes most programs stop at the prescription but Eden brings medication, care, and support together. bmiMD highlights 24/7 clinician access, online refills and delivery, and confirms product strength every 3 to 6 months to remain within plus or minus 10 percent of target strength for their compounded tirzepatide. bmiMD also markets a 100 percent risk free, 100 percent refundable statement if not eligible for the program on its landing page.

Conclusion: Eden Health vs bmiMD

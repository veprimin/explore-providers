Strut Health vs Mochi Health: primary differences

This comparison of Strut Health vs Mochi Health focuses on the five most distinctive differences supported by the sources: pricing structure, oral specialization, live video visits availability, compounded medication regulatory notes, and geographic prescription access.

1. Pricing structure: flat starting rate versus membership plus meds

Strut Health advertises oral GLP-1 “From $199 per Month” and markets itself as a no-frills, accessible telehealth option with clear starting pricing. Mochi Health uses a membership-plus-medication model with pricing that starts as low as $99/month for membership plus medication costs and promotional offers such as 50% off the first month with code BESTWLHALFOFF. The sources note Mochi’s first month promotions and that ongoing cost varies by plan and medication.

2. Oral specialization versus broader treatment types and live video care

Strut Health emphasizes specialized oral-focused treatment options including oral Semaglutide lozenges and a clinical team focused on oral medication management. Mochi Health offers a broader set of telehealth services beyond weight loss and distinguishes itself by offering live video visits with a doctor bookable in about 5 minutes, which the source calls fundamentally different from asynchronous intake reviews.

3. Live video visits and appointment model

Mochi Health highlights synchronous, live video visits where a licensed physician discusses goals and treatment in real time and offers reliable GLP-1 prescription access in all 50 states. Strut Health describes guided consultations and ongoing progress communication but the captured evidence emphasizes an oral specialization rather than live video as a defining workflow.

4. Compounded medications and regulatory note

Both providers rely on compounded medication offerings in part. Strut Health describes GLP-1 compounded weight management therapy with Semaglutide and explicitly states compounded Semaglutide is prepared by a U.S. licensed compounding pharmacy and is not FDA approved. Mochi Health lists compounded semaglutide and tirzepatide options and the source similarly includes the regulatory note that compounded medications are not FDA approved and are not reviewed by FDA for safety, effectiveness or quality. The regulatory qualifier is present in both sources and must be considered by prospective users.

5. Availability and service breadth

Mochi Health advertises GLP-1 prescription access in all 50 states and additional services such as dermatology, hair, mental health, and intimacy treatments. Strut Health is framed as a focused oral GLP-1 provider with a smaller network than larger telehealth marketplaces according to the captured pros and cons.

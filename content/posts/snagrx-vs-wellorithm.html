Quick verdict: SnagRx is the lower-cost option if you commit to a 12-month plan and want a price lock at every dose, while Wellorithm is the better option if you need licensed clinician access in all 50 states. This article compares SnagRx vs Wellorithm across price transparency, state coverage, medication types, shipping and clinician model.

See SnagRx pricing and plan details

Price transparency and plan commitments

SnagRx lists compounded semaglutide from $69/month and compounded tirzepatide from $119/month on a 12-month plan. SnagRx states month-to-month rates are $99 and $149, and the advertised $69 and $119 require the 12-month commitment. Wellorithm does not publish fixed prices sitewide; it provides personalized quotes after the intake quiz and advertises starting figures such as $147 in some messaging but pricing is not published the same way as SnagRx.

State coverage and clinician availability

SnagRx operates with a clinician network called OpenLoop Health that the vendor says covers 48 states. Wellorithm states it has licensed clinicians in all 50 states, which the source identifies as a primary strength for patients in states with limited GLP-1 telehealth options.

Medication types and regulatory notes

Both providers offer compounded semaglutide and compounded tirzepatide rather than branded finished products. The source material for both notes that compounded medications are not FDA-approved and are not reviewed by the FDA for safety, effectiveness or quality. SnagRx names four US 503A partner pharmacies and emphasizes its compounded supply chain; Wellorithm states its compounds are produced at licensed pharmacies.

Delivery, onboarding, and guarantees

SnagRx advertises next-day UPS or FedEx shipping in temperature-controlled packaging and says a clinician typically reviews intake within five hours and always within 24 hours. SnagRx also states a full refund if the prescription is declined and a 6-month weight-loss guarantee with terms. Wellorithm advertises free, fast shipping and presents a virtual consultation model with ongoing virtual check-ins; it also markets a weight-loss guarantee.

Clinical process and eligibility screening

SnagRx uses an online intake followed by clinician review through OpenLoop Health; the SnagRx source explains that OpenLoop clinicians apply exclusionary criteria and retain prescribing discretion. Wellorithm uses a two-minute quiz to check eligibility, then a licensed clinician in the patient’s state reviews and prescribes if medically appropriate. Both note that treatment is available only when a clinician determines it is appropriate.

Who should pick each provider

Choose SnagRx if you want predictable, locked pricing across dose titration and you are willing to commit to a 12-month plan to access the $69 semaglutide or $119 tirzepatide rates. Choose Wellorithm if you need licensed clinician access in all 50 states or if you require a provider that gives a personalized quote based on intake rather than fixed public pricing.

Final comparison note

SnagRx vs Wellorithm differs most on published pricing and state coverage. SnagRx publishes a low annual plan price and a price lock at every dose and names its pharmacies and clinician network. Wellorithm emphasizes 50-state clinician coverage and provides personalized pricing after intake. Both use compounded medications that are not FDA-approved as finished products, and both require clinician approval for treatment.

Choose SnagRx if you want locked, low annual pricing. Choose Wellorithm if 50-state clinician coverage is essential.

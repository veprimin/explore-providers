Pallas Health vs Vaylen: both are cash-pay telehealth options for GLP-1 care, but Pallas offers clinician choice of brand-name or compounded medication while Vaylen publishes very low prepaid 12-month rates. Early verdict: choose Pallas if you want access to FDA-approved pens plus a compounded fallback and clinician-determined route; choose Vaylen if you want the lowest effective monthly rate on a prepaid plan and a needle-free oral option.

Direct comparison at a glance: core differences

Pallas Health and Vaylen both sell clinician-supervised weight-loss plans, but they differ in three main areas: medication pathways, pricing structure, and prepay versus post-review workflows. Each provider states that compounded medications are not FDA-approved, and both platform disclosures say eligibility is determined by a US-licensed clinician.

1. Medication options and clinician choice

Pallas Health: clinician reviews your health history and personalizes the plan, offering FDA-approved Wegovy, Ozempic, Zepbound and Mounjaro, or compounded semaglutide and tirzepatide when a brand medication is not the right fit. Pallas says both routes come from one intake, so a clinician can pick brand-name pen or a per-patient compounded preparation.

Vaylen: offers compounded semaglutide injectable, oral semaglutide tablets, and tirzepatide injectable on one platform. Vaylen highlights a needle-free oral tablet option and the ability for a clinician to switch molecules or format without changing providers. Unlike Pallas, Vaylen does not offer branded pens; all medications it markets are compounded.

2. Pricing and billing models

Pallas lists compounded semaglutide at $139 for the first month, then $597 every 12 weeks, which Pallas calculates as a $199/mo average. Compounded tirzepatide is $179 first month, then $897 every 12 weeks ($299/mo average). Brand-name pens are cash-pay with monthly figures published per product. Pallas says its compounded plans are priced by medication rather than by milligram, so titrating up does not raise the plan price.

Vaylen publishes annualized prepaid 12-month rates: semaglutide injectable from $83.25/mo on the 12-month program or $224/mo month to month; oral semaglutide $99/mo on the 12-month program or $249/mo monthly; tirzepatide $149/mo on the 12-month program or $299/mo monthly. Vaylen emphasizes that every 12-month rate is billed upfront in a single charge and that the clinician review follows payment, with a full refund if declined.

3. Evaluation flow, refunds, and timing

Pallas positions clinician review before treatment and refunds the first payment in full if a clinician decides treatment is not right for you. Pallas also notes that a video visit is required in seven states plus Washington, D.C., while most states are asynchronous (43 of 50 need no video visit).

Vaylen requires prepayment for the annual plan before a clinician reviews the file. Vaylen states a full refund for that billing cycle if the clinician declines to prescribe. Vaylen touts clinician review within 24 hours and free discreet shipping in 1 to 3 business days. Pallas notes free expedited shipping but emphasizes compounded plans renew every 12 weeks rather than monthly.

4. Shipping, availability, and care access

Pallas: free expedited and discreet shipping, compounded in the USA, care-team messaging and provider care included in the plan price, weekday care-team hours 9 AM to 5 PM ET, and no emergency services. Pallas is cash-pay only and does not bill insurance; FSA/HSA use may require a Letter of Medical Necessity.

Vaylen: free discreet shipping in 1 to 3 business days, licensed clinicians via OpenLoop Health, claims availability in all 50 states, HIPAA protection, and 24/7 care-team messaging included. Vaylen also notes cancel anytime and a published refund if the clinician declines in that cycle. Vaylen warns that prepaid terms for unused portions of a year are not published.

SnagRx vs Sunlight: quick verdict

SnagRx is the lower-cost compounded option on a 12-month plan with a stated price lock at every dose; Sunlight starts higher but advertises personalized plans and says prices stay the same forever. Early verdict: choose SnagRx if you want the lowest annual-plan price and named partner pharmacies; choose Sunlight if you need injection or oral options and a different starting price point.

Pricing and plan structure

Both providers offer telehealth prescriptions and ship medication in 1-2 days, but their advertised starting prices and commitment terms differ. SnagRx lists compounded semaglutide from $69/month and tirzepatide from $119/month on a 12-month plan, with month-to-month equivalents of $99 and $149. Sunlight advertises plans starting at $88/month and promotes injections and oral options. The SnagRx low rates require the 12-month plan; Sunlight shows a Summer Promo and mentions as low as $88 but does not have the SnagRx-style monthly breakdown captured in the source.

Price guarantees and flat pricing

Both vendors emphasize stable pricing, but with different framing. SnagRx states its rate is locked flat at every dose and at renewal for the life of treatment and calls that out as a differentiator. Sunlight uses the phrase same price forever and no price increases ever. The sources show SnagRx explicitly ties its $69 and $119 figures to a 12-month plan and notes month-to-month costs are higher. Sunlight claims same price forever and no hidden fees but the captured source also warns that pricing, eligibility, pharmacy, state coverage and renewal terms may change.

Medication type and regulatory notes

SnagRx supplies compounded semaglutide and tirzepatide and the source repeatedly states these are compounded medications produced in FDA-regulated facilities but not FDA-approved finished products. SnagRx also explicitly warns that compounded medications are not FDA-approved and that outcomes may vary. Sunlight markets personalized GLP-1 plans and lists injections and oral options, while the source includes the regulatory note that medication-specific approval status must be confirmed and that branded and compounded products are not interchangeable.

Clinician access, state coverage and transparency

SnagRx names its clinician network as OpenLoop Health, says clinicians typically review intake within five hours and that the network covers 48 states. SnagRx also names its four US 503A partner pharmacies. Sunlight emphasizes a 100 percent online medical visit and clinician support in testimonials, but the captured source warns readers to confirm eligibility, medication, total cost, renewal terms and state availability before enrolling. The Sunlight source captures strong customer testimonials about clinician responsiveness, but specific state coverage and pharmacy partners were not captured.

What each provider highlights most

SnagRx highlights bottom-of-market annual-plan pricing, a price lock at every dose, named US 503A pharmacies, next-day temperature-controlled shipping and an OpenLoop clinician network. Sunlight highlights personalized plans from $88/month, injections and oral options, free 1-2 day shipping, and a same-price-forever message plus customer testimonials about support and results. The sources show SnagRx ties its lowest prices to a 12-month commitment; Sunlight shows promotional language including a Summer Promo and a call to confirm terms before enrolling.

Final comparison and how to decide

Both platforms deliver telehealth prescriptions and fast shipping, but the most distinctive differences are starting price on an annual plan, how the price guarantee is framed, medication type transparency, named pharmacy partners and clinician network speed. Use SnagRx if you prioritize the lowest annual-plan rate for compounded semaglutide or tirzepatide and want named 503A pharmacies and a stated clinician network with fast review. Use Sunlight if you want plans that advertise injections and oral options and a stated same-price-forever promise with promotional starting pricing at a different level.

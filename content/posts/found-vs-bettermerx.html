Found vs BetterMe Rx: Overview and how they differ

Both providers offer telehealth access to GLP-1 treatments, but the most distinctive differences are pricing structure and membership, medication formats and compounding, insurance handling and billing, shipping and delivery, and program focus and personalization. The rest of this article compares those points directly using only the supplied facts.

1. Pricing structure and plan commitments

Found presents “get started for as low as $39” and highlights “affordable monthly plans” with references to varied pricing such as membership starting at $49/mo when billed annually and other price mentions like $50/mo for certain brand options. Found also documents offers and discounts tied to cash payment or insurance in-network status and notes advertised pricing may be linked to 12 month commitments for branded GLP-1s.

BetterMe Rx lists concrete monthly figures and 12-month equivalents. Compounded GLP-1 treatment is “from $145/month on a 12-month plan.” Specifics include semaglutide injection $205/mo or as low as $145/mo on a 12-month plan; tirzepatide injection $275/mo or as low as $215/mo; oral semaglutide $250/mo or as low as $185/mo; oral tirzepatide $265/mo or as low as $215/mo. BetterMe Rx states lowest prices require longer 12-month plans.

2. Medication formats and compounding

Found mentions semaglutide and tirzepatide and indicates both branded and compounded options are offered, with explicit regulatory language that compounded GLP-1s are not FDA-approved and not available in all states. BetterMe Rx offers compounded semaglutide and tirzepatide in injectable and oral formats and notes the needle-free daily tirzepatide tablet as an uncommon cash-pay option. BetterMe Rx also repeats that compounded medications are not FDA-approved.

3. Insurance, membership and billing

Found emphasizes insurance involvement: “Most insurance now accepted,” “we handle insurance paperwork,” and specific Medicare Bridge program references such as getting Wegovy, Foundayo, or Zepbound for as little as $50/mo through Medicare for eligible Part D members. Found states insurance coverage, pricing and terms vary and Rx is subject to provider review and clinical eligibility.

BetterMe Rx is a cash-pay program with “No membership fee” and “No insurance or membership fee required.” BetterMe Rx notes longer prepaid plans yield lower monthly-equivalent pricing and that treatment is available only when a licensed clinician determines it is medically appropriate.

4. Shipping, supply and included services

BetterMe Rx states medication, provider review, pharmacy compounding, supplies and free 1–2 day shipping are included. It also notes longer supplies are billed according to the selected plan and that there are no hidden fees. Found mentions handling insurance paperwork and program features like medically supervised support, but explicit shipping promises were not supplied in the captured Found evidence.

5. Program focus, personalization and eligibility

Found markets a “biology-first personalized medically supervised program” that targets hormones, metabolism, sleep and behavioral factors and builds a program around each patient’s metabolic biology. BetterMe Rx describes a model based on provider review and pharmacy compounding that offers multiple administration formats and plan lengths. Both providers state Rx is subject to clinician/provider review and clinical eligibility. Both preserve regulatory notes that compounded medications are not FDA-approved.

How the two providers differ at a glance

This comparison uses only the supplied facts to contrast Embody and Try Ageless across price, medication formats, shipping and delivery, included extras, and regulatory notes. Both sell compounded semaglutide and tirzepatide via telehealth prescriptions and both note that compounded medications are not FDA-approved.

1. Price and entry cost

Embody advertises compounded semaglutide at $69/month reduced from $299 and compounded tirzepatide at $119/month. Try Ageless lists prepaid yearly plan per-month pricing from $159 for oral drops, $199 for semaglutide injection, and $249 for tirzepatide injection. Try Ageless also states a discount is already applied and an extra code is available.

2. Medication formats and flexibility

Embody provides compounded semaglutide and compounded tirzepatide injections. Try Ageless sells three formats: semaglutide injection, tirzepatide injection, and needle-free tirzepatide oral drops. Try Ageless highlights the ability to switch molecules or formats under one plan with no new consult fee.

3. Shipping, delivery and extras

Embody promises free shipping and medication delivered in 1–2 days after clinician approval. Try Ageless offers free discreet shipping and includes B12 in every plan for energy support during weight loss. Try Ageless notes the discount is built into every plan and an extra code SLIM50 can stack additional savings.

4. Commitments and plan terms

Embody promotes no monthly membership and cancel anytime, emphasizing flat locked-in monthly pricing and no long-term commitment. Try Ageless offers cancel-anytime flexibility and notes its lowest advertised per-month prices require the prepaid yearly plan for the stated per-month rates.

5. Safety and regulatory notes

Both providers state their medications are compounded and not FDA-approved finished products. Try Ageless explicitly notes that oral tirzepatide drops are an off-label compounded format because tirzepatide is not FDA-approved in oral form. Embody states compounded medications are not FDA-reviewed for safety, effectiveness or quality.

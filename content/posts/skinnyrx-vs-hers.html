Key difference 1: FDA-approved brand medication versus compounded oral tablets

Hers emphasizes FDA-approved Wegovy and other name-brand GLP-1s. The source states Wegovy and Zepbound are FDA-approved for weight loss and Ozempic is FDA-approved for type 2 diabetes and may be prescribed off-label for weight loss. Hers explicitly says it does not offer compounded medications for Wegovy and markets name-brand products with no price markups. SkinnyRx, by contrast, promotes compounded semaglutide tablets. The source notes that compounded medications are not FDA-approved and are not reviewed by the FDA for safety, effectiveness, or quality. That regulatory distinction is the most fundamental difference between these providers.

Key difference 2: Price structure and membership

Both providers list starting prices but their billing models differ. Hers lists medication from $149/mo for the Wegovy pill and notes medication requires an active Hers Weight Loss Membership, with membership fees billed separately and medication unavailable without an active membership. SkinnyRx lists oral semaglutide as low as $249/mo and mentions options to split payments with Affirm Buy Now Pay Later. The sources preserve that memberships and separate fees are part of Hers pricing, while SkinnyRx highlights an as low as $249/mo starting price and payment flexibility.

Hers does not advertise a shipping policy or third-party testing in the captured evidence but emphasizes access to brand-name GLP-1s through its membership and clinician oversight. The evidence supports SkinnyRx as focused on fast shipping and documented lot testing for its compounded products.

Key difference 4: Care model and monitoring

Both providers require a prescription and clinician involvement. Hers highlights a personalized care team with ongoing dose check-ins and states treatment is available only when a licensed clinician determines it is medically appropriate. SkinnyRx states prescriptions are written after an online consultation with a healthcare provider, describes ongoing patient access to providers and a patient care team, and notes dose adjustments as needed. Both advertise clinician review, but Hers specifically calls out ongoing dose check-ins as part of its personalized care team.

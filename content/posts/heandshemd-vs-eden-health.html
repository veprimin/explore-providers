Head-to-head: what each provider sells

He & She MD bundles compounded GLP-1 medication with a coaching app that includes 1:1 clinician messaging, GLP-1-specific guidance and progress tracking. Eden Health shows monthly pricing at every dose with semaglutide at $99/month and tirzepatide at $129/month, and advertises a lifetime price guarantee. Both state free expedited shipping and no insurance required.

Distinctive difference 1: Bundled coaching and clinician access

Distinctive difference 2: Pricing structure and guarantees

He & She MD offers promotional six-month introductory rates: semaglutide injections start at $139/month and oral semaglutide and tirzepatide injections at $199/month, but the source repeatedly notes these are six-month intro rates that renew 30 to 45 percent higher. Eden lists flat monthly prices at every dose with a stated lifetime price guarantee for those monthly prices in the captured evidence. That makes Eden’s advertised ongoing price certainty a key contrast to He & She MD’s time-limited promos.

Distinctive difference 3: Medication formats and regulatory notes

He & She MD offers three formats: semaglutide injection, oral semaglutide and tirzepatide injection, with captured notes that medications are produced in USP-compliant, state-regulated pharmacies but are compounded and not reviewed by the FDA for safety, efficacy or quality. Eden lists semaglutide and tirzepatide pricing and notes that treatment is available only when a licensed clinician determines it is medically appropriate. The Eden evidence also flags that compounded medications, when offered, are not FDA-approved and are not reviewed by FDA for safety, effectiveness or quality.

Distinctive difference 4: Renewal terms and transparency

The He & She MD source repeatedly warns that headline prices are six-month intro rates and that plans renew at higher six-month prices, including examples of renewal totals. Eden’s captured material highlights a lifetime price guarantee but the detailed renewal or eligibility mechanics were not published in the captured evidence. The article preserves that gap rather than inferring details.

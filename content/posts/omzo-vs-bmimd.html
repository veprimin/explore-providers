Overview: Omzo vs bmiMD at a glance

Both providers offer clinician-reviewed telehealth for GLP-1 or GLP-1/GIP therapy and rely on state-licensed compounding pharmacies when compounded medications are used. Important regulatory note for both: compounded medications are not FDA-approved and are not reviewed by FDA for safety, effectiveness or quality. The two most visible differences in the evidence are medication variety, pricing structure, and shipping or subscription terms.

Key difference 1: Medication options and clinician guidance

Omzo emphasizes clinician-guided GLP-1 care and lists both compounded semaglutide and tirzepatide as available through its platform. The Omzo site states clinicians determine eligibility and may prescribe compounded medications or write prescriptions for branded medications that the patient fills elsewhere.

bmiMD centers its offering on compounded tirzepatide (GLP-1/GIP) with clinician review and 24/7 clinician access. bmiMD’s evidence focuses on tirzepatide specifically and its subscription options rather than semaglutide.

Key difference 2: Pricing model and commitments

Omzo presents starting prices for specific medications: semaglutide starting at $179 and tirzepatide starting at $279. Pricing is presented as starting amounts and Omzo notes that medication costs are included in the fees they collect, with clinicians making final prescribing decisions.

bmiMD publishes tiered monthly pricing for tirzepatide with explicit month-to-month and multi-month equivalents: $179 for a one-month plan, $159 per month for a three-month plan, $149 per month for a six-month plan, and $139 per month for a 12-month plan. bmiMD notes the lowest price requires a 12-month commitment and that total prepaid charges should be confirmed.

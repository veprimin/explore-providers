Mochi Health vs CoreAgeRx: three decisive differences

This comparison focuses on the most distinctive differences supported in the source evidence: visit format and clinical experience, pricing structure and transparency, and medication type and regulatory notes. Both platforms provide clinician-reviewed telehealth care for GLP-1 treatment when clinically appropriate, but they present key trade offs.

1. Visit format and clinical experience

Mochi Health emphasizes live video visits with a licensed physician bookable in about 5 minutes. The source says this synchronous model is fundamentally different from the asynchronous text-review model used by many GLP-1 platforms and lets a doctor answer questions and explain treatment plans in real time. CoreAgeRx advertises a 3-minute online visit but the captured evidence does not detail whether visits are synchronous video or primarily an asynchronous intake. If a real-time conversation with a physician matters to you, the evidence supports Mochi Health as the clearer option.

2. Pricing structure and predictability

Mochi Health operates on a membership-plus-medication model with pricing starting from $99 per month and a promotional 50% off first month via code BESTWLHALFOFF. Mochi also lists doctor-prescribed treatments starting at $39 for the first month of membership with the promotional rate applying to new customers only and a standard rate thereafter. The source highlights that ongoing pricing varies by plan and medication and that pricing transparency is lower than flat-rate competitors.

CoreAgeRx positions itself on predictable flat monthly pricing with no membership fee. The captured evidence lists semaglutide at $79 per month and tirzepatide at $129 per month and repeatedly states Same Price. Every Month. CoreAgeRx emphasizes no price hikes, no membership fees, and no billing surprises. The evidence also cautions that pricing, eligibility, pharmacy, state coverage and renewal terms may change, and that treatment is available only when a licensed clinician determines it is medically appropriate.

3. Medication offerings and regulatory notes

Mochi Health lists a range of options that include compounded semaglutide and tirzepatide starting from specified promotional prices and notes compounded medications are not FDA approved and are not reviewed by FDA for safety, effectiveness or quality. Mochi also references FDA-approved branded options and a variety of treatment categories including dermatology, hair, mental health, and intimacy. CoreAgeRx lists tirzepatide up to 7.5 mg and semaglutide up to 1.0 mg at flat monthly prices and notes medication-specific approval status must be confirmed and that branded and compounded products are not interchangeable. Both sources preserve clinician discretion and clinical appropriateness as prerequisites to treatment.

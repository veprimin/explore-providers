Quick verdict: Pallas Health vs CoreAgeRx

Pallas Health and CoreAgeRx both offer clinician-reviewed online GLP-1 programs, but they differ clearly: Pallas emphasizes clinician selection between FDA-approved brand pens and US-compounded semaglutide or tirzepatide with an all-in plan price, while CoreAgeRx emphasizes flat monthly pricing with no membership fee and a consistent per-month charge. Early verdict: choose Pallas if you want a clinician to pick brand-name or a compounded option inside a single intake; choose CoreAgeRx if you prioritize predictable same-price-every-month billing.

Distinctive difference 1: Clinician choice of brand vs compounded

Pallas Health presents both routes from one intake: a US-licensed clinician reviews your health history and can choose either an FDA-approved brand-name pen or a per-patient compounded semaglutide or tirzepatide prepared in the USA when a brand medication is not the right fit. CoreAgeRx emphasizes available semaglutide and tirzepatide dosing but the captured evidence focuses on price consistency rather than an explicit same-intake choice between brand and compounded products.

Distinctive difference 2: Pricing structure and predictability

Pallas lists an introductory first-month price and renewal cadence for compounded plans: semaglutide $139 first month then $597 every 12 weeks (about $199/mo avg); tirzepatide $179 first month then $897 every 12 weeks ($299/mo avg). Brand-name options at Pallas are shown as cash-pay monthly figures. CoreAgeRx markets a flat monthly price and stresses “Same Price. Every Month.” with tirzepatide at $129/month and semaglutide at $79/month on the captured page.

Distinctive difference 3: Billing, membership and plan inclusions

Pallas advertises one all-in plan price that includes clinician review, ongoing check-ins and care-team messaging with no membership fee and a self-serve portal. CoreAgeRx highlights no membership fee and no billing surprises with an emphasis that price will not increase month to month. The specific inclusions and renewal terms for CoreAgeRx were not fully published on the captured landing page and should be confirmed before enrollment.

Distinctive difference 4: State requirements and visit type

Pallas notes it is 100% online and asynchronous in most states, with a video visit required in seven states plus Washington, D.C. CoreAgeRx promotes a short 3-minute online visit on the captured page; captured evidence also warns that treatment is available only when a licensed clinician determines it is medically appropriate.

Other practical notes from the sources

Both providers require clinician eligibility determination. Pallas states it does not bill insurance and that cash-pay is required; FSA/HSA use may require a Letter of Medical Necessity. CoreAgeRx also states no insurance requirement for the visit but captured landing-page details on insurance handling, pharmacy partner and renewal terms were not published or could not be verified on the captured page.

1. Live video physician visits vs in-app coaching

Mochi Health’s defining claim is synchronous live video visits with a licensed physician bookable in about 5 minutes. The source describes this as fundamentally different from the asynchronous text-review intake used by most competitors. He & She MD, by contrast, bundles a coaching app with 1:1 clinician messaging, GLP-1-specific guidance and progress tracking. That means Mochi prioritizes a real-time conversation with a doctor while He & She MD prioritizes ongoing coaching and messaging support.

2. Pricing model and promotional terms

Mochi advertises a membership-plus-medication model starting from $99/month and a 50% off first month promotion using code BESTWLHALFOFF. The source states the $39 promotional rate applies to the first month only for new customers and that ongoing pricing varies by plan and medication. He & She MD offers introductory six-month rates – for example semaglutide injections from $139/month as an intro – but the source warns these renew at higher six-month prices after the initial term. Both providers present promotional rates that change after an initial period or month, so compare ongoing costs carefully.

3. Support, app features, and access

Mochi lists 24/7 support and ongoing appointment availability alongside its live video visits, positioning continuing care and quick access as features. He & She MD includes its app with 1:1 clinician messaging, coaching, and progress tracking at no extra cost and highlights free expedited shipping and no membership fee. The distinction here is synchronous video plus continuous support at Mochi versus a bundled coaching and messaging experience at He & She MD.

4. Medication options and regulatory notes

Both sources reference compounded GLP-1 medications. Mochi lists compounded semaglutide and tirzepatide options with starting rates and explicitly notes that compounded medications are not FDA-approved and are not reviewed by FDA for safety, effectiveness or quality. He & She MD states medications are produced in USP-compliant, state-regulated pharmacies but also notes those compounded GLP-1s are not reviewed by the FDA for safety, efficacy or quality. Neither source supplies clinical efficacy data beyond provider-reported or member-reported figures, so no outcome guarantees are made here.

5. Who each provider is best for

The supplied sources identify slightly different ideal customers. Mochi is described as best for members who want membership plus meds from $99/month and a live physician consultation experience that closely resembles an in-person visit. He & She MD is described as best for patients who have tried a bare-bones GLP-1 subscription and stalled without accountability and who want coaching and clinician access built into the plan rather than sold separately.

How they differ at a glance

Both Embody and YourEra prescribe compounded semaglutide and tirzepatide via telehealth and ship medication to your door, but they differ on pricing structure, what is bundled, shipping language, and required commitments for the lowest rates.

Key differences organized by feature

1. Pricing and entry price

Embody advertises compounded semaglutide at $69/month, reduced from $299, and compounded tirzepatide at $119/month. YourEra advertises compounded semaglutide from $99/month and tirzepatide from $199/month on the 6-month plan, with the listing noting $79.20/month reflects the 6-month plan in some marketing instances. The YourEra lowest advertised rates require a 6-month commitment; Embody emphasizes a flat locked-in monthly pricing and no monthly membership.

2. What’s included and care features

YourEra explicitly bundles the provider consult, prescription, cold shipping, dose adjustments, and 24/7 support into its plan price and calls out Legitscript certification and licensed US clinicians. Embody provides prescription and telemed visits, 24/7 support access for dosing and side-effect questions, and a no-frills model without integrated coaching or advanced monitoring. Embody markets a lean, prescription-forward model while YourEra emphasizes bundled clinical and shipment features.

3. Shipping and delivery

Both providers advertise free shipping with fast delivery. Embody says medication ships free in 1 to 2 days after clinician approval. YourEra says orders ship from licensed US pharmacies and arrive in roughly 1 to 2 days, with cold and discreet shipping noted as included on its plan.

4. Commitment and cancellation

Embody promotes no long-term commitment, cancel anytime, and no monthly membership. YourEra’s lowest monthly rates are tied to a 6-month plan and notes shorter plans cost more per month; it also states there is no membership fee but the 6-month billing structure must be considered for price comparisons.

5. Regulatory and medication type

Both companies dispense compounded semaglutide and tirzepatide and include regulatory notes that compounded medications are not FDA-approved finished products. Embody and YourEra both state prescriptions are issued only if a licensed clinician determines treatment is appropriate after evaluation.

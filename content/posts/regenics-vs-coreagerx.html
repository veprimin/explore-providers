Who reviews care and how visits work

Both providers require a licensed clinician to determine appropriateness for treatment. Regenics is described as nurse-practitioner-led, with licensed nurse practitioners reviewing intake before any prescription. CoreAgeRx emphasizes clinician-reviewed online visits and mentions a 3-minute online visit on captured material. The evidence does not supply further details about appointment length or specific clinical staff credentials beyond these points.

Compounded medication status and regulatory notes

Regenics explicitly states its semaglutide and tirzepatide are compounded by U.S. licensed compounding pharmacies and reiterates that compounded medications are not FDA-approved finished products and are not reviewed by FDA for safety, effectiveness or quality. CoreAgeRx’s captured material includes a note that medication-specific approval status must be confirmed and that branded and compounded products are not interchangeable. Both sources make clear that treatment is subject to clinician review.

Comparative pros and cautions

Regenics pros in the source: lower promotional entry price for month one, Sermorelin add-on, free shipping with U.S. compounding, FSA/HSA eligibility, and care overseen by licensed nurse practitioners. Regenics cautions in the source: promotional pricing applies to the first month only, compounded medications are not FDA-approved finished products, and pricing beyond the promotional month requires reading the fine print. CoreAgeRx pros in the source: flat monthly pricing claimed with no membership fee and no price hikes. CoreAgeRx cautions in the source: pricing, eligibility, pharmacy, state coverage and renewal terms may change and medication approval status must be confirmed.

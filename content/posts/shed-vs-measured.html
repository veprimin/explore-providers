Shed vs Measured: head-to-head overview

This comparison highlights five distinctive differences supported by the sources: medication types offered, coaching and clinical support, pricing and promotions, prescription speed and shipping, and regulatory notes and outcome claims. The comparison uses only the supplied facts and preserves qualifiers where provided.

1. Medication options: name-brand plus compounded vs compounded only

Shed: Offers both FDA-approved name-brand GLP-1 medications and compounded GLP-1 options on one platform. Measured: Promotes compounded semaglutide and tirzepatide. The presence of name-brand options is a primary categorical difference between the two providers.

2. Coaching and ongoing support

Shed: Integrates continuous coaching into treatment and pairs medication with coaching support; coaching is bundled into the monthly cost rather than charged separately. Measured: Offers dietitian support, unlimited messaging, and a 24/7 care team, as described in source bullets. Both provide clinical support, but Shed emphasizes continuous coaching as part of the core model while Measured highlights dietitian support and messaging.

3. Pricing, promotions, and starting costs

Shed: Lists compounded semaglutide injections starting at $199/month and compounded tirzepatide injections starting at $299/month and advertises 10% off your first month. Shed does not publish standard pricing and issues a personalized quote after intake. Measured: Promotes promotional compounded semaglutide from $89/mo and compounded tirzepatide promotional pricing at $189/mo with free two-day shipping. Measured also notes promotional pricing may change.

4. Prescription speed and shipping

Measured: Emphasizes same-day prescription decisions and free two-day shipping on every order. Shed: Highlights a fast 2-minute approval assessment followed by clinician review; medication is then shipped and coaching begins. Both describe quick eligibility checks, but Measured explicitly advertises same-day Rx decisions and free 2-day shipping.

5. Regulatory notes and outcome claims

Both sources include the regulatory qualifier that compounded medications are not FDA-approved and are not reviewed by FDA for safety, effectiveness, or quality. Shed references clinical trials of Wegovy with average weight loss figures in that context and reminds readers that results may vary and are subject to clinician review. Measured reports an internal average member outcome at six months (average loss was 31 lbs) and states individual results vary and are not guaranteed; it also notes that this internal analysis is not independent evidence.

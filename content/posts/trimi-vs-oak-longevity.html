Quick verdict: Trimi vs Oak Longevity

Trimi and Oak Longevity both sell compounded semaglutide and tirzepatide online, but the biggest differences are starting price, subscription and refund policies, shipping speed, and how clinical review is handled. Early verdict: Trimi emphasizes lower starting price on annual plans, overnight temperature-controlled shipping, and a 6-month money-back promise; Oak emphasizes one flat monthly price with no subscription, free coaching, and nonrecurring billing.

How pricing and billing compare

Trimi advertises compounded semaglutide from $99/month on the annual plan and compounded tirzepatide from $125/month with flat pricing that never rises as your dose increases. Oak Longevity lists semaglutide from $133/month and tirzepatide from $199/month, with one flat price that covers all dosages and billing that is non-recurring so you choose to renew each month. Oak explicitly states no subscriptions, membership fees, or hidden costs; Trimi highlights same price at every dose and flat membership coverage on annual plans.

Clinical review, approvals, and refund policies

Both providers rely on licensed clinicians to review cases. Trimi says prescriptions are provided by US-licensed clinicians through Arora Health and notes a 6-month money-back promise: follow your plan for 6 months and you can request a refund if you do not lose weight, with terms applied. Oak Longevity describes board-certified physicians in your state who review cases asynchronously, no video call required, and specifies that no refunds are available after physician approval and once medication has shipped.

Shipping speed and additional services

Trimi advertises free overnight, temperature-controlled shipping. Oak Longevity offers free shipping with tracking, free health coaching included, and notes most patients receive medication within a few days with same-day approval available for qualifying patients. Shipping language differs: Trimi emphasizes overnight and temperature control; Oak emphasizes tracking and included coaching.

Regulatory and eligibility notes

Both sources state that compounded medications are prepared per individual prescription and are not FDA approved as finished products and are not reviewed by FDA for safety, effectiveness, or quality. Oak additionally notes patients with BMI below 20 or under 18, pregnant, or nursing are not appropriate for treatment in some cases; Trimi advises confirming eligibility, pharmacy, state coverage, and renewal terms before enrollment.

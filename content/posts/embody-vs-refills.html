Price and pricing model: immediate difference

Price is the clearest distinction in this Embody vs Refills comparison. Embody promotes compounded semaglutide at $69/month, reduced from $299, and compounded tirzepatide at $119/month. Refills advertises personalized compounded GLP-1 from $99/month (~$5/day) with flat pricing that stays the same at every dosage and lists tirzepatide at $159/month. Both specify that medications are compounded, and both note that compounded drug products are not evaluated by the FDA.

Shipping speed and fulfillment

Embody commits to free shipping and delivery in 1–2 days after clinician approval, highlighting fast turnaround once approved. Refills offers free discreet and express shipping with tracking in about 2 business days after processing and supplies a 4-week refill each month. If immediate post-approval delivery is a priority, Embody is presented as faster; Refills emphasizes discreet express shipping and tracking updates.

Support, clinician review, and ongoing access

Both platforms require a prescription and licensed clinician review. Embody includes prescription and telemed visits as part of the offering and notes licensed provider review handled online with 24/7 support access for dosing and side-effect questions. Refills describes a health intake quiz and licensed clinician review that may include a chat with a doctor within 24 hours, plus unlimited clinician consultations at no extra cost and 24/7 health concierge support. Refills highlights ongoing text and email shipment updates and monthly refill forms.

Medication type and regulatory notes

Both Embody and Refills offer compounded semaglutide and tirzepatide options rather than branded FDA-approved finished products. Embody states its offerings are compounded semaglutide and compounded tirzepatide and notes compounded medications are not FDA-approved finished products. Refills states its GLP-1s are personalized compounded products from U.S. pharmacies subject to compounding regulation and likewise notes that compounded drug products are not evaluated by the FDA.

How Shed and Willow differ at a glance

Both platforms are telehealth-first and require a licensed clinician to confirm medical appropriateness. The most distinctive supported differences are medication form and options, coaching and follow-up, pricing disclosures, assessment speed, and emphasis on progress tracking. Below we compare those points directly using only supplied facts.

1. Medication types and regulatory notes

Shed offers both FDA-approved name-brand GLP-1 medications and compounded GLP-1 options on one platform. The source explicitly states that compounded medications are not FDA-approved and are prepared by a licensed pharmacy based on a provider’s prescription. Willow positions an oral GLP-1 program; the captured evidence notes that medication-specific approval status must be confirmed and that branded and compounded products are not interchangeable. Both providers require a clinician to determine whether treatment is medically appropriate.

2. Coaching, follow-up, and progress tracking

Shed integrates continuous coaching into treatment rather than only issuing a prescription. The platform markets continuous coaching to support titration, lifestyle changes, and plateaus. Willow emphasizes progress tracking tools and follow-up consultations as part of a structured weight management journey. Both present behavioral support, but Shed highlights ongoing coaching bundled into its plans while Willow highlights structured follow-up and tracking infrastructure.

3. Pricing and assessment transparency

Shed advertises specific starting figures for compounded injections and a promotional offer: compounded Semaglutide injections starting at $199 per month and compounded Tirzepatide injections starting at $299 per month, plus a stated 10% off your first month. Shed also notes it does not publish a single standard price online and that patients receive a personalized quote after intake. Willow captured evidence lists a starting price of 299 and indicates higher monthly entry pricing for some plans in the pros and cons, but captured landing-page details were not readable and shipping or fee details were not verified on the captured page.

4. Speed of assessment and workflow

Shed advertises a 2-minute online approval assessment to check eligibility before clinician review. Willow describes a simple digital workflow and telehealth team but the captured evidence does not specify an assessment duration. For users prioritizing a very quick initial eligibility check, the supplied evidence supports Shed’s 2-minute assessment claim.

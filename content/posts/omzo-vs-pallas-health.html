Omzo vs Pallas Health: pricing and plan structure

Pricing is a major difference. Omzo lists compounded semaglutide starting at $179 and tirzepatide starting at $279, with medication costs included in fees collected by Omzo. Pallas Health advertises compounded semaglutide at $139 for the first month, then $597 every 12 weeks (about $199/mo average), and compounded tirzepatide $179 first month then $897 every 12 weeks (about $299/mo average). Pallas also publishes cash-pay brand‑name monthly prices for Wegovy, Ozempic, Zepbound and Mounjaro.

What the prices mean for you

Omzo presents starting prices for compounded options and notes medication costs are included in its fees. Pallas emphasizes an intro month price then 12-week renewal pricing for compounded plans and shows brand-name cash-pay figures. Confirm current figures at checkout with either provider.

Primary differences in medication options and regulatory notes

Both providers offer compounded GLP-1s but handle brand-name drugs differently. Omzo connects patients with licensed providers who may prescribe compounded medications or write prescriptions for branded FDA-approved medications; Omzo does not sell or ship branded medications. Pallas Health offers both FDA-approved brand-name GLP-1s and clinician-personalized compounded semaglutide or tirzepatide from intake.

Regulatory note: Both sources state that compounded medications are not FDA-approved and are not reviewed by the FDA for safety, effectiveness or quality.

Care model, clinician review and included services

Omzo: Clinical services are provided by third party networks of U.S.-licensed clinicians who determine eligibility and make prescribing decisions. Omzo is not a medical practice and says the online assessment does not create a doctor-patient relationship. Pallas Health: A U.S.-licensed clinician reviews your history and personalizes the plan; Pallas frames provider care, check-ins and messaging as included in one all-in plan price with no membership fee and offers asynchronous care in most states.

Shipping, renewal cadence and logistics

Omzo notes fast, discreet delivery and FSA/HSA eligibility as described on its landing content, and that medication programs use state-licensed sterile compounding pharmacies. Pallas Health advertises free expedited, discreet shipping from U.S. compounding pharmacies and clarifies compounded plans renew every 12 weeks for the stated renewal prices.

Other practical considerations

Pallas highlights that compounded plans are priced by medication rather than by milligram so titration does not raise the plan price, that it is cash-pay only and that a video visit is required in seven states plus Washington, D.C. Omzo emphasizes that providers make final prescribing determinations, that compounded medications supplied through its program are prepared by state-licensed sterile compounding pharmacies and that these compounded drugs are not FDA-approved.

Conclusion: Omzo vs Pallas Health

Omzo and Pallas Health both provide clinician-reviewed access to compounded GLP-1s, but Pallas builds brand-name and compounded options into a single intake with an all-in plan price and explicit first-month intro pricing. Omzo positions itself on personalized clinician-guided compounded programs with stated starting prices and notes that providers may instead prescribe brand-name products that patients fill elsewhere. Choose based on whether you prefer an all-in plan that includes provider care plus brand-name access or a platform focused on clinician-guided compounded offerings.

## Ingredients breakdown

### Tadalafil (15mg)

Tadalafil is the active ingredient in Cialis. It has the longest half-life of
the common PDE5 inhibitors, which is where the extended response window comes
from. The [FDA label for Cialis](https://www.accessdata.fda.gov/drugsatfda_docs/label/2011/021368s020lbl.pdf)
covers the approved single-ingredient product.

- Improves penile blood flow
- Supports a response window of up to 36 hours
- Allows for more spontaneity than a short-acting tablet

### Sildenafil (40mg)

Sildenafil is the active ingredient in Viagra and the most studied ED compound
there is. It acts faster than tadalafil but does not last as long — which is
why the two are combined here rather than either being used alone.

- Fast, strong erectile response
- The largest body of clinical evidence of any ED drug
- Pairs with tadalafil to cover both onset and duration

### Apomorphine (3mg)

Apomorphine is a dopamine receptor agonist. It is
[FDA-approved for Parkinson's disease](https://www.accessdata.fda.gov/drugsatfda_docs/label/2017/021264s016lbl.pdf),
not for erectile dysfunction, and is used off-label in compounded ED formulas.

The reason it appears here is that it works on the central nervous system
rather than on blood flow. PDE5 inhibitors do nothing for desire; if arousal
signalling is the limiting factor, a vascular-only drug will underperform no
matter the dose. That is the gap apomorphine is meant to fill — though the
evidence base for it in ED is much thinner than for sildenafil or tadalafil.

- Acts on arousal and motivation pathways, not circulation
- Addresses a contributor that PDE5 inhibitors leave untouched
- Off-label use, with limited long-term combination data

## How BraveRx works

Two mechanisms, running at once:

1. **Vascular.** Two PDE5 inhibitors at different speeds — sildenafil for
   onset, tadalafil for duration — rather than one compound doing both jobs
   imperfectly.
2. **Neurological.** Apomorphine acts on dopamine pathways involved in arousal.

BraveRx puts onset at around 15 minutes and the response window at up to 36
hours. Both figures vary considerably between individuals.

## The compounding caveat

BraveRx is a compounded medication. Compounded combinations are **not
FDA-approved as finished products**, even when the individual ingredients are
approved on their own. Tadalafil and sildenafil have been through FDA review as
single-ingredient drugs; this three-drug combination has not, and the FDA does
not verify the safety or effectiveness of compounded drugs.

That is not an accusation of illegitimacy — compounding is legal, and BraveRx
prescribes through licensed clinicians and dispenses through partnered
pharmacies. It does mean there is less published safety and efficacy data behind
this specific combination than behind a branded tablet.

If that trade-off bothers you, an FDA-approved generic from
[Ro](/ro-ed-treatment-review-trusted-reliable-15-off-first-order/) is the more
conservative route.

## Verdict

BraveRx is a reasonable pick if single-ingredient medication has underdelivered
for you and you think arousal, not just blood flow, is part of the picture.
Layering two PDE5 inhibitors at different speeds is sensible, and publishing the
per-ingredient dosages is more than several competitors do.

The honest counterweight is that this is a compounded combination without FDA
review as a finished product, and apomorphine's ED evidence is the weakest part
of the formula. At about $13 a dose you are paying a real premium over generic
sildenafil for that bet. Worth it if the generics have already failed you;
not the place to start if they have not.

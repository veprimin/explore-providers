Key differences at a glance

The comparison centers on four distinctive differences: starting price and discounts, available molecules, pharmacy and lab handling, and regulatory notes about compounded medications. Both providers require clinician review and make compounded medications available, with specific pricing and preparation differences below.

1. Pricing and introductory offers

LTTL lists pricing details that include specific amounts and a promotion: up to $140 off your first month with a code auto-applied and noted prices such as $249 and $129 for certain dose packs. Telos Rx lists per month starting points: semaglutide as low as $99 per month and tirzepatide as low as $139 per month, and states you may pay $0 if not approved. Telos Rx also says prices like $299 and $399 with lower as low as amounts. Both emphasize that pricing and eligibility are subject to the live offer and clinician approval.

See Telos Rx pricing and molecule options

2. Medication options and clinical matching

LTTL promotes a powerful, full-dose GLP-1 weight loss medication compounded and delivered, framed as a single full-dose GLP-1 offering. Telos Rx offers two molecules: semaglutide, which acts on GLP-1, and tirzepatide, which acts on GLP-1 and GIP. Telos Rx states a US-licensed provider decides which molecule is right for you.

3. Pharmacy preparation and lab verification

LTTL specifies that compounded medications are prepared by U.S.A. licensed pharmacies, independently verified in FDA registered labs, and confirmed every 3 to 6 months to stay within plus or minus 10 percent of the target strength. Telos Rx indicates semaglutide and tirzepatide are compounded medications and prescription-only, prescribed after review by a US-licensed provider, but does not provide the same lab verification language in the captured evidence.

4. Regulatory and clinician review notes

Both providers state treatment is available only when a licensed clinician determines it is medically appropriate. Both include the regulatory caution that compounded medications are not FDA-approved and are not reviewed by FDA for safety, effectiveness or quality. Telos Rx additionally notes individual results vary and are not guaranteed. LTTL repeats the compounded medication regulatory note in the captured evidence.

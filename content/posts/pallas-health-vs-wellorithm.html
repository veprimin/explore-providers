The question of Pallas Health vs Wellorithm comes down to three main differences: medication offerings, state coverage, and pricing transparency. Quick verdict: choose Pallas Health if you want a clinician who can select between FDA-approved brand pens and compounded preparations; choose Wellorithm if you need guaranteed clinician access in all 50 states and only compounded options.

Start with Pallas Health: clinician selects brand or compounded

How they differ: medication options and clinician choice

Pallas Health presents both FDA-approved brand-name GLP-1s and clinician-personalized compounded semaglutide or tirzepatide. Its site lists Wegovy, Ozempic, Zepbound and Mounjaro as brand options, and also offers compounded semaglutide and tirzepatide prepared in the USA when a brand medication is not the right fit. Wellorithm offers compounded semaglutide and tirzepatide only, with no branded pen options published.

Check Wellorithm: compounded semaglutide and tirzepatide

Pricing transparency and starting costs

Pricing is a clear point of divergence. Pallas Health lists compounded semaglutide at $139 first month, then $597 every 12 weeks (a $199/mo average), and compounded tirzepatide at $179 first month, then $897 every 12 weeks ($299/mo average). Pallas also publishes cash-pay brand pricing for Wegovy, Zepbound, Ozempic and Mounjaro. Wellorithm does not publish complete pricing on its site; marketing copy highlights “Only $147” and other starting figures, but the company states you receive a personalized quote after the intake quiz and pricing is not fully published.

State coverage and access

Wellorithm emphasizes licensed clinicians in all 50 states, positioning that as a core advantage for patients in states where other telehealth platforms have limited coverage. Pallas Health operates 100% online with asynchronous care in most states and notes that a video visit is required in seven states plus Washington, D.C. The two providers therefore differ on geographic coverage and visit requirements.

Care model, check ins, and what is included

Pallas Health markets an all-in plan price that includes clinician review, ongoing check-ins and care-team messaging with no membership fee. It also highlights a self-serve portal and a free companion app. Wellorithm describes fully virtual consultations and medication shipped to your home after an online assessment; it promotes virtual check-ins to keep titration on track. Wellorithm advertises “No membership or hidden fees” on its marketing copy, but detailed plan inclusions and recurring pricing are provided after intake.

Regulatory notes and shipping

Both providers offer compounded medications and include the regulatory qualifier that compounded medications are not FDA-approved and are not reviewed by FDA for safety, effectiveness or quality. Pallas states its compounded medications are prepared by US-licensed compounding pharmacies and offers free expedited, discreet shipping. Wellorithm also markets fast, free shipping in its promotional copy.

Final take on Pallas Health vs Wellorithm

Pallas Health is built for patients who want a choice between FDA-approved brand-name GLP-1 pens and clinician-personalized compounded preparations, with published pricing and an all-in plan that includes provider care and messaging. Wellorithm is built for patients who need guaranteed licensed clinician access in all 50 states and are comfortable with compounded-only offerings and a personalized price quote after intake.

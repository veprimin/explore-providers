Pallas Health vs Willow: what differs most

This direct comparison focuses on the most distinctive supported differences: medication format and choices, pricing structure, care and support model, compounded medication regulatory notes, and eligibility or clinician review. The comparison uses only supplied facts and preserves qualifiers exactly.

1. Medication format and choices

Pallas Health offers FDA-approved brand-name Wegovy, Ozempic, Zepbound and Mounjaro, plus clinician-personalized compounded semaglutide and tirzepatide prepared in the USA when a brand medication is not the right fit. Willow positions an oral GLP-1 program and highlights that medication-specific approval status must be confirmed. Willow also notes that compounded medications, when offered, are not FDA-approved.

2. Pricing and how plans renew

Pallas lists compounded semaglutide as $139 first month, then $597 every 12 weeks which is a $199/month average, and compounded tirzepatide $179 first month then $897 every 12 weeks which is a $299/month average. Pallas also lists cash-pay brand prices on its site for Wegovy, Zepbound, Ozempic and Mounjaro. Willow shows a starting price of 299 in captured evidence but landing-page details were not fully readable. The supplied evidence states Willow has higher monthly entry pricing for some plans.

3. Care delivery and support

Pallas is 100% online with one all-in plan price that includes clinician review, ongoing check-ins and care-team messaging, with no membership fee. Pallas is asynchronous in most states, with a video visit required in seven states plus Washington, D.C., and care-team hours are weekdays 9 AM to 5 PM ET. Willow is telehealth-first and emphasizes progress tracking tools and follow-up consultations, describing a structured weight management journey that pairs medication with behavioral scaffolding intended to support long-term outcomes.

4. Regulatory and safety notes

Both sources preserve important qualifiers about compounded medication. Pallas explicitly states that compounded medications are not FDA-approved and are not reviewed by FDA for safety, effectiveness or quality and that Pallas does not manufacture or compound medication. Willow notes that medication-specific approval status must be confirmed and that compounded medications, when offered, are not FDA-approved and are not reviewed by FDA for safety, effectiveness or quality.

5. Eligibility and clinician review

Pallas repeatedly states that eligibility is determined by a US-licensed clinician and that treatment is cash-pay only. Willow states treatment is available only when a licensed clinician determines it is medically appropriate.

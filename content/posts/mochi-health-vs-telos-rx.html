How the two providers differ at a glance

This direct comparison of Mochi Health vs Telos Rx focuses on the most distinctive differences supported by the sources: consultation style and clinician interaction, pricing structure and transparency, scope of services and support, and how compounded medication status and regulatory notes are presented.

Consultation style and clinician interaction

Mochi Health: The platform emphasizes live video visits with a licensed physician bookable in about 5 minutes. Mochi describes the synchronous video model as a real-time conversation where a doctor answers questions and evaluates the case before prescribing.

Telos Rx: Treatment decisions are made after review by a US licensed provider. The captured Telos Rx evidence does not describe synchronous live video visits and focuses on clinician review to determine which molecule is right for the patient.

Pricing structure and transparency

Mochi Health: Pricing is membership plus medication, with membership-plus-medication plans starting from $99 per month. Mochi advertises 50% off the first month with code BESTWLHALFOFF and promotional first-month rates such as $39 for the first month in some offers. The source notes ongoing cost varies by plan and medication and that ongoing pricing is less transparent than flat-rate competitors.

Telos Rx: The captured Telos Rx evidence lists semaglutide as low as $99 per month and tirzepatide as low as $139 per month if a licensed provider approves you. Telos states your price depends on your plan and that you may pay $0 if not approved. The source repeatedly notes that compounded medications are prescription-only and subject to medical approval by a US licensed provider.

Scope of services and patient support

Mochi Health: In addition to GLP-1 treatments, Mochi advertises dermatology, hair, mental health, and intimacy services, 24/7 support, and ongoing appointment availability. Mochi markets membership-plus-medication care with ongoing appointments throughout the program.

Telos Rx: The captured Telos Rx evidence is narrowly focused on GLP-1 options, explaining semaglutide acts on GLP-1 and tirzepatide acts on GLP-1 plus GIP. Telos emphasizes a clinician-decided molecule match and does not provide the same breadth of services or 24/7 support claims in the captured material.

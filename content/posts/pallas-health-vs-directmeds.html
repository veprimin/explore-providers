Pallas Health vs Directmeds answers whether you want a clinician to choose between brand and compounded GLP-1s or a service focused only on compounded options. Early verdict: choose Pallas Health if you need access to both FDA-approved brand-name GLP-1s and clinician-personalized compounded semaglutide or tirzepatide; choose Directmeds if you prioritize transparent standard pricing for compounded injectable and sublingual options with fast shipping.

Key difference 1: Brand-name options versus compounded-only

Pallas Health offers FDA-approved Wegovy, Ozempic, Zepbound and Mounjaro plus clinician-personalized compounded semaglutide and tirzepatide prepared in the USA when a brand medication is not the right fit. Directmeds focuses on clinician-reviewed compounded semaglutide and tirzepatide in injectable and sublingual formats. Both providers note that compounded medications are not FDA-approved.

Key difference 2: Pricing structure and starting offers

Pallas Health advertises compounded semaglutide at $139 first month, then $597 every 12 weeks (a $199/month average); compounded tirzepatide is $179 first month, then $897 every 12 weeks (a $299/month average). Brand-name cash-pay prices are listed separately on Pallas. Directmeds advertises treatment starting at $147 with a summer promotion saving $150; its FAQ lists standard pricing of $297/month for semaglutide and $399/month for tirzepatide. Both state no monthly membership fee.

Key difference 3: Shipping, format and availability

Pallas Health offers free expedited, discreet shipping and notes compounded medications are prepared by US-licensed compounding pharmacies. Directmeds advertises free temperature-controlled next-day UPS/FedEx shipping and provides injectable and sublingual compounded formats. Directmeds is not available in Mississippi or Louisiana; Pallas requires a video visit in seven states plus Washington, D.C., and is asynchronous in most states (43 of 50 need no video visit).

Key difference 4: Billing, cancellation and refunds

Pallas is cash-pay only and does not bill insurance; it notes FSA/HSA use may require a Letter of Medical Necessity. Pallas compounds renew every 12 weeks, so timing matters for cancellation. Pallas also states a full refund is provided if a clinician decides treatment is not right for you. Directmeds advertises month-to-month cancellation for standard plans, subscribe-and-save plans require a stated term, and a full refund is offered if a prescription is not approved.

Care model and clinical review

Both services use clinician review to determine eligibility. Pallas describes a US-licensed clinician personalizing the plan and includes provider care, check-ins and messaging in the plan price. Directmeds provides clinician review, telehealth visits, nursing support and supplies included in the price. Neither provider claims FDA review for compounded products.

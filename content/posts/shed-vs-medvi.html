How the two providers differ at a glance

This direct comparison focuses on the most distinctive differences supported by the source: medication breadth, pricing and promotions, coaching and support, and the enrollment process and shipping. Both platforms prescribe after clinician review and both offer compounded medication options, but their emphases and price structures diverge.

Medication options: name-brand plus compounds versus compounded semaglutide only

Shed offers both FDA-approved name-brand GLP-1s and compounded alternatives on one platform, explicitly letting patients choose based on budget and preference. The source notes that name-brand options carry higher costs while compounded options are more affordable. MedVi, by contrast, offers clinically dosed compounded semaglutide only; the source states MedVi does not offer branded options and focuses on compounded semaglutide.

Pricing and promotions

Shed lists compounded semaglutide starting at $199/month and compounded tirzepatide starting at $299/month, and advertises 10% off your first month. Shed does not publish a single standard price and gives personalized quotes after intake. MedVi advertises a promotional first month at $179 including fast, free shipping, with ongoing standalone months at $299 and a three-month plan at $649 as noted in the source. The source warns MedVi’s ongoing price is substantially higher than the first-month promo.

Coaching and support

Shed integrates continuous coaching into treatment and positions coaching as part of the bundled monthly cost. The source frames Shed as a platform for patients who want behavioral support alongside medication. MedVi emphasizes 24/7 support from certified medical professionals and a simple online process; the source highlights MedVi’s clinically dosed compounded semaglutide plus round the clock support, but it does not describe an integrated coaching membership equivalent to Shed’s coaching model.

Enrollment process, clinic visits and shipping

MedVi’s process is entirely online with no clinic visits required, and the landing evidence states fast, free shipping is included in the promotional first month. Shed uses a 2-minute online assessment to check eligibility and requires a licensed clinician to confirm treatment; medication is shipped after clinician review. Both platforms rely on clinician review before prescriptions are issued and both note that compounded medications are not FDA-approved.

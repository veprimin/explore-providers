How Trimi and Embody compare: overview

This Trimi vs Embody comparison focuses on the most distinctive differences supported by the source: price and plan structure, shipping speed and delivery promise, medical and pharmacy oversight, refund and commitment policies, and the compounded medication regulatory note. I compare each criterion directly so you can pick the program that matches your priorities.

Price and plan structure

Embody advertises a lower entry price: semaglutide at $69/month and tirzepatide at $119/month, with $69 listed as cut from $299. Embody highlights no membership and cancel anytime. Trimi lists semaglutide from $99/month on the annual plan and tirzepatide at $125/month, and emphasizes flat pricing that never rises as dose increases. Trimi also promotes a single flat membership that covers medication, clinician visits, and free shipping on annual plans.

Shipping speed and delivery

Embody promises free shipping that arrives in 1 to 2 days after clinician approval. Trimi advertises free overnight, temperature-controlled shipping. Both providers state free shipping, but Embody emphasizes a 1-2 day delivery window while Trimi highlights overnight and temperature-controlled handling.

Medical oversight and pharmacy sourcing

Trimi states prescriptions are written by US-licensed clinicians through Arora Health, led by a board-certified physician, and that medications are compounded by state-licensed 503A pharmacies, including named pharmacies with certificates of analysis available on request. Embody describes licensed telehealth provider review and fulfillment by a licensed pharmacy but does not list specific pharmacy names in the captured evidence.

Commitments, refunds, and membership terms

Embody promotes no monthly membership and the ability to cancel anytime, framing its model as no long-term commitment. Trimi highlights an annual plan pricing structure and a 6-month money-back promise: follow the plan for 6 months and you may request a refund if you do not lose weight, with terms applying. Trimi also mentions automatic refund if not approved by their medical team. These differences affect flexibility and risk tolerance.

Regulatory note and medication status

Both providers state their medications are compounded and include the regulatory qualifier that compounded medications are not FDA-approved and are not reviewed by FDA for safety, effectiveness, or quality. Trimi further notes that the peer-reviewed clinical trials behind the active ingredients are cited but are not a guarantee of individual results. Embody notes its compounded medications use the same active ingredients as branded options and that final pricing may vary based on clinician recommendation.

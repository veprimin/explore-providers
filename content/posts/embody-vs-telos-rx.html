Key differences at a glance: price, shipping, clinician process

The most distinctive differences between Embody and Telos Rx are: starting price, shipping speed and commitments, how pricing is presented relative to clinician approval, and explicit regulatory notes about compounded medications. Both platforms prescribe compounded GLP-1s after a licensed provider review and both note that compounded medications are not FDA approved.

1. Starting price and published offers

Embody advertises compounded semaglutide at $69/month, reduced from $299, and compounded tirzepatide at $119/month. Telos Rx lists semaglutide as low as $99/month and tirzepatide as low as $139/month, with prices presented as conditional on licensed provider approval.

2. Shipping and delivery commitments

Embody specifies free shipping and delivery in 1 to 2 days after clinician approval. Telos Rx captured details do not publish a similar guaranteed delivery window on the captured landing page. If fast post-approval delivery is a priority, Embody documents a 1 to 2 day delivery claim.

3. Clinician review and eligibility framing

Both providers require review by a licensed clinician. Telos Rx frames prices explicitly as available if a licensed provider approves you and notes pay $0 if not approved. Embody states prescription and telemed visits are included and that medications ship after approval, with final pricing potentially varying by dose and clinician recommendation.

4. Product type and regulatory note

Both platforms offer compounded semaglutide and tirzepatide. Both source rows include the regulatory note that compounded medications are not FDA approved and are not reviewed by FDA for safety, effectiveness or quality. Embody explicitly states its medications are compounded and not FDA approved in the captured content.
